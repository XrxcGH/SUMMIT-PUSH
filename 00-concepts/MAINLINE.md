# MAINLINE℠ — FIRST AGE OF RAIL

**Theme:** The golden age of transcontinental rail: two rival railway companies race to claim junctions, light the signals, and keep the freight moving down a contested mainline.

**Narrative:** The frontier is stitched together by a single ribbon of steel, and two railway companies both claim the right-of-way. In MAINLINE, alliances of three robots load freight, raise fuel drums to the depot shelves, and hang signal lanterns high on the Union Tower to seize control of the line — because whoever holds the signals owns the railroad. As the final whistle nears, robots scramble up the signal gantries and throw the great semaphore switches, flipping the boards to their colors for all the territory to see. Claim the junctions. Light the lanterns. Own the MAINLINE.

## Game Pieces
### FREIGHT CRATE
- Shape/dims: Cube, 12.0 in x 12.0 in x 12.0 in, slightly compliant faces, ~2.0 lb
- Material: Ballistic-nylon fabric skin over a laser-cut corrugated-plastic internal frame with foam corner blocks (2018 POWER UP cube construction — proven durable, ~$15 to fabricate; team version: cardboard box wrapped in duct tape)
- Handling challenge: Large and floppy-faced: forces wide claws or top-down pinchers, squeeze-force tuning, and stack placement without toppling the crates below. Stacking to Level 3 (35 in) demands an elevator or 4-bar with controlled release.
- Count/staging: 30 total: 10 staged per alliance half (6 in a taped FREIGHT YARD zone beside the siding, 4 along the wing near midfield) plus 5 behind each alliance's Loading Dock for human-player feed. No crate preloads.
- Scoring role: The volume currency and the low floor. Fixed points on flatcar stacks (uncontested, own-side) and pushed into Depot ground bays where each crate adds +1 Signal Weight toward depot ownership — a kitbot shoving crates literally fights for territory.

### FUEL DRUM
- Shape/dims: Cylinder, 8.0 in diameter x 12.0 in tall, rigid with rolled top rim, ~3.0 lb
- Material: Roto-molded HDPE drum with molded rim lip (vendor-supplied; team version: 8 in concrete form tube with plywood end caps and gaffer tape)
- Handling challenge: It rolls when dropped and MUST be scored standing upright on a shelf: forces a wrist/reorientation mechanism or clever passive funneling. Ground intake of a rolling cylinder plus 90-degree reorientation to vertical is the signature mechanism problem of the game.
- Count/staging: 12 total: 3 staged upright per alliance half beside each Depot face, 3 behind each Loading Dock. No preloads.
- Scoring role: The mid-value territory piece. Upright drums on Depot shelves score fixed points AND carry the heaviest depot Signal Weight (+2 low shelf, +3 high shelf), so drum specialists swing depot ownership hardest.

### SIGNAL LANTERN
- Shape/dims: Torus (ring), 10.0 in outer diameter, 3.0 in round cross-section (4.0 in inner hole), ~1.0 lb
- Material: Dense closed-cell foam ring with embedded fabric skin (identical construction to a gym/pool foam ring; team version: pool noodle bent into a hoop with fiber tape — under $5)
- Handling challenge: Smallest piece, and it must be HUNG on 15-degree up-angled hooks at up to 72 in: demands a precise end effector, tall elevator or extending arm, and AprilTag auto-alignment because a 4 in hole over a 1.315 in hook leaves little error budget at full extension.
- Count/staging: 14 total: up to 3 preloaded per alliance (one per robot), 4 NEUTRAL lanterns staged on the centerline 4 ft either side of the Union Tower (contested in auto), 2 behind each Loading Dock.
- Scoring role: The crown-jewel currency. Lanterns on Union Tower hooks score the highest fixed points and are the ONLY source of Tower Signal Weight — the alliance with more weight owns the Tower and its 1 pt/sec accrual. Every lantern hung visibly shifts the Headlamp.

## Field Layout
Standard 27 ft x 54 ft carpeted field, 180-degree rotationally symmetric. CENTERLINE (the "MAINLINE"): three contested structures form a wall-to-wall chokepoint band. (1) UNION TOWER at dead field center: 3.5 ft x 3.5 ft footprint, 8 ft tall plywood-and-lumber tower. Each alliance-facing side carries 6 SEMAPHORE HOOKS (two each at 36 in, 54 in, 72 in, angled 15 degrees up) for lanterns; the top carries the HEADLAMP, a 360-degree lamp glowing red, blue, or white (neutral) to show ownership. (2) Two DEPOTS (Depot WEST and Depot EAST) on the centerline, each 5 ft wide x 2 ft deep x 5 ft tall, centered 4.5 ft from each side wall. Each Depot has a red face and a blue face: per face, 4 ground-level crate bays (floor push-in slots 13 in wide), 3 low drum shelves at 18 in, 2 high drum shelves at 42 in (shelf surfaces tilted 15 degrees back toward the structure so drums settle inward). A 6 ft SIGNAL MAST on each depot swings a counterweighted semaphore paddle toward the owning alliance. Travel lanes: two 4.5 ft gaps between Tower and each Depot, and 4.5 ft between each Depot and the side wall — real chokepoints, passable by one robot. NEUTRAL ZONE staging: 4 neutral lanterns on the centerline, two 4 ft to each side of the Tower. ALLIANCE HALVES: each alliance has (a) a FLATCAR SIDING 10 ft from its alliance wall, parallel to the right side wall: three 36 in x 36 in flatcar decks at 11 in height with a 1.5 in retaining lip, accepting crate stacks up to 3 high (top of L3 = 35 in); (b) the SIGNAL GANTRY, an overhead truss 8 ft from the alliance wall spanning 20 ft, with three physically separated CLIMB STATIONS on 6 ft centers (rung details in endgame); a 4 ft x 4 ft BOARDING PLATFORM (2 in raised plywood) under each station. LOADING DOCKS: each alliance's two human-player docks are in the corners of the OPPOSING alliance wall (2018-portal style) — a 14 in x 14 in delivery chute at 24 in height — guaranteeing every cycle crosses the centerline chokepoints. AprilTags (36h11): 2 per Tower face, 1 per Depot face, 1 per Loading Dock, 1 per Gantry climb station, 2 on each alliance wall (16 IDs total per standard layout).

OWNERSHIP ACCRUAL (territory scoring, evaluated continuously): UNION TOWER — alliance with strictly greater total Tower Signal Weight owns it: 2 pts/sec in AUTO, 1 pt/sec in TELEOP; tie or empty = neutral, nobody scores. EACH DEPOT — alliance with strictly greater Depot Signal Weight on its face owns it: 1 pt per full 2 seconds of ownership (per depot). Max weight per depot face = 16 (4 crates + 3 low drums + 2 high drums); max Tower weight per face = 12. When a face is FULL, that side's weight is locked — the territory war becomes a race to fill your face before the opponent out-weighs you. NOTES / wording robustness: a piece SCORES when fully supported by the structure (lantern supported solely by its hook; drum upright with full base on shelf; crate fully within bay or fully atop the stack below) and not in contact with any robot of the scoring alliance. Pieces never descore-able by robots (additive counterplay only — you retake by out-placing, not ripping); pieces knocked off by contact with the structure's owner are returned to the nearest Loading Dock by field staff, opponent-caused knock-offs are a G-foul (+5 to the placing alliance) plus piece restoration. Pieces leaving the field are returned to the nearest Loading Dock. All fixed placement points bank permanently when scored (visible tick on the audience display); ownership accrual is banked per second and never revoked retroactively.

## Match Flow
2:30 total — 0:15 AUTO, 2:15 TELEOP with the final 0:30 as the ENDGAME window (Gantry levers unlock at T-30). A typical cycle: collect from your Loading Dock in the OPPONENT'S corner or from staged field pieces, thread one of the three 4.5 ft centerline chokepoints (Tower gaps or wall gaps), and place on your Depot face, Tower face, or run freight home to your siding. Because both alliances' feed points are cross-field, all six robots continuously cross paths through the chokepoint band — contested space by geometry, not forced intake-vs-intake contact (you score on YOUR face of every centerline structure, so scoring robots are back-to-back, never nose-to-nose). Defense model: a defender can camp a chokepoint or shadow the opposing lantern bot, but three lanes plus the wall gaps mean no lane is fully sealable; standard 5-sec pin count applies. SPECTATOR LEGIBILITY (the chess-match fix): the score state is physically displayed — the Headlamp glows the owning alliance's color, both Depot semaphore paddles point at their owners, and gantry boards flip at endgame. A spectator reads the field in one glance: "blue lamp, two red paddles — red is bleeding points at both depots but blue holds the Tower." Momentum swings are visible the instant a lantern or drum lands. Expected scores: median-alliance ~110-150; strong alliance ~230-280 (roughly 45% ownership accrual, 35% placement, 20% endgame), so the dialogue between territory and freight stays live all match.

## Autonomous
0:15 AUTO, deeply interactive. (1) GAME DATA — THE EXPRESS ORDER: at T=0 the FMS randomly designates Depot WEST or EAST as the EXPRESS DEPOT (its signal mast lamp strobes white; the character "W" or "E" is delivered via game data API). During AUTO only, placements at the Express Depot score DOUBLE fixed points and DOUBLE Signal Weight, and the alliance owning the Express Depot when auto ends banks a +10 EARLY DISPATCH bonus. Every serious auto must branch left/right on the game data — 2018 POWER UP-style software forcing, with AprilTag re-localization mid-path. (2) CONTESTED NEUTRALS: the 4 neutral lanterns sit on the centerline, two per side of the Tower, reachable by either alliance — a first-mover race for extra Tower weight (staged 4 ft apart with approach vectors from opposite faces, so contests are races, not collisions). (3) ALLIANCE COORDINATION: with three robots, one Express-Depot lane, one Tower face, and a siding, alliances must pre-negotiate lane assignments and typically run a designated "signal bot" (lantern to Tower), "express bot" (drum/crate to the strobing depot), and "freight bot" (crates to siding) — collision-free multi-robot choreography is the whole ballgame. (4) LEAVE: each robot fully exiting its starting zone scores 3. (5) GREEN SIGNAL bonus: if all 3 robots Leave AND the alliance owns the Union Tower at T=15, bank +8. Tower accrues at 2 pts/sec during auto, so an auto-hung lantern is worth its 8-16 fixed points plus up to ~20 seconds of early ownership — auto directly seeds the teleop territory war rather than being a disconnected minigame.

## Endgame
THE SIGNAL GANTRY — climb the trusswork and throw the switch. Each alliance's gantry has three separated climb stations on 6 ft centers (no 2019-HAB/2024-stage pileups; each station has its own AprilTag for auto-align). Rung ladder per station: LOW RUNG at 2 ft 6 in, MID RUNG at 4 ft 3 in offset 15 in horizontally behind the low rung, HIGH RUNG at 6 ft 0 in offset another 15 in behind the mid — all 1.25 in schedule-40 steel pipe. The offsets force a genuine traverse (swing, walk, or two-hook handoff), which is the "very tough" part: a high climb is a Rapid-React-traversal-class mechanism and control problem that reads as EXACTLY as hard as it is (s-neff compliant — nobody watches a rung-to-rung swing and thinks it's easy). PARTIAL CREDIT LADDER, no cliff, every tier banks independently at the buzzer (3-second settle): PARK on the 4 ft x 4 ft Boarding Platform = 3; hang supported solely by LOW RUNG (bumpers fully off carpet) = 6; MID RUNG = 12; HIGH RUNG = 18; THE LEVER = +5: a robot supported by the HIGH rung may pull the station's SIGNAL LEVER (a 12 in handle at 6 ft 9 in), which mechanically flips a 3 ft plywood semaphore board to the alliance color with an audible clack — max 23/robot, 69/alliance. Why it rewards: three high-and-lever climbs can swing 69 points — enough to flip a close match — but a median alliance still banks 12-24 from parks and low hangs, so two minutes of freight work is never invalidated (endgame is ~20-25% of a strong score, Crescendo's undervaluation corrected without Steamworks all-or-nothing). Climbing is legal anytime, levers unlock at T-30. Traffic is structurally solved: three stations, 6 ft apart, each single-robot width. The lever flip is the most photographed moment of the season by design.

## Ranking Points
Win = 3 RP, Tie = 1 RP, plus up to 3 bonus RPs that all reinforce the core loop (no side quests). Per-event-tier escalation: | Bonus RP | Regional | DCMP | Champs | — (1) FREIGHT RP (entry volume, target 40-60% of median alliances): alliance scores a combined total of placed pieces (crates + drums + lanterns, any location) of >= 20 | >= 25 | >= 30. Pure volume of the primary verb; a kitbot's flatcar crates count fully. (2) MAINLINE RP (stretch, ~3-3.5x entry difficulty, elite-coordination only): hold ALL THREE centerline structures (Union Tower + both Depots) SIMULTANEOUSLY for >= 15 continuous seconds | >= 20 s | >= 25 s. Requires a lantern bot, a drum bot, and a crate-bay defender working in concert while the opponents counter-place — the RP is literally "win the territory war outright for a sustained moment," and the crowd sees it as lamp-plus-both-paddles in one color. (3) GANTRY RP (endgame = "two robots do the mid-tier action"): alliance Gantry points >= 24 (two MID hangs, or one HIGH+lever plus a low/park) | >= 30 | >= 36. All three RPs are read directly off the physical indicators and the placed-piece counter on the audience display — zero hidden bookkeeping.

## Low Floor
A kitbot chassis with a simple roller-claw or even a plywood plow contributes every second: (1) push staged crates into Depot ground bays — floor-level, 13 in openings, no lift required — and every crate is +2 and, critically, +1 Signal Weight, meaning the simplest robot on the field is actively fighting for territory ownership, not doing a consolation task; (2) shuttle crates onto Flatcar Level 1 (11 in deck, forgiving lip) on its own uncontested side for steady 2s; (3) ferry pieces from its Loading Dock to teammates staging near the centerline; (4) park on the Boarding Platform for 3, or bolt on a simple hook for the 2 ft 6 in LOW RUNG (a static hook + winch, classic first-climber project) for 6. Perceived difficulty matches actual difficulty: pushing a box into a floor slot looks easy and IS easy; nobody gets ambushed by hidden precision (contrast: 35-degree reef branches). A pure drivetrain bot can also legally camp a chokepoint lane on defense in elims. Median rookie contribution: ~30-45 points plus ownership swings — visible on the depot paddle every time.

## High Ceiling
Elite teams chase throughput-weighted territory math that never fully solves: (1) the three piece geometries (floppy 12 in cube, rolling 8x12 cylinder needing reorientation, 10 in foam ring needing precision hangs) cannot all be handled excellently under 115 lb — a true triple-threat robot gives up climb speed or drivetrain, so archetypes persist: Lantern Specialist (tall elevator + ring wrist + traversal climb), Drum/Freight Superbot (fast ground intake + reorienting wrist, mid-height only), Chokepoint Defender/Crate Flooder, and Climb-Anchor. (2) The ownership economy has live tradeoffs: is a 72 in lantern (8 pts + 3 weight) worth 9 seconds of cycle time versus two ground crates (4 pts + 2 weight) in 7 seconds? The answer flips with match state, opponent composition, and how full each face is — capacity locking (16 weight max/face) creates genuine endgame-of-territory calculations, like bearing-off in backgammon. (3) Late-season meta shift is built in: as climbs commoditize, value migrates from accrual-holding to fill-racing and lever denial-by-speed; the Express Depot randomization keeps auto development alive all season (branching multi-piece autos that also steal neutral lanterns). (4) The MAINLINE RP forces elims-relevant coordination scouting: who can hold, not just score. No single-optimal robot exists because holding all three structures requires three different mechanisms by rule of physics, not by rule text.

## Teaching Value
This is a full Rapid-React/Power-Up-class mechanism curriculum for a student team: MECHANICAL — ground intake design for three distinct geometries (compliant-wheel cube intake, cylinder capture, ring capture), indexing/transfer between intake and placement, a real elevator-vs-arm architecture decision (72 in hooks favor elevators; 42 in tilted shelves favor arms), a wrist/end-effector for 90-degree drum reorientation, and a traversal climber (hook geometry, CG management, ratchet winches, spring-loaded passive hooks) with an optional lever manipulator. SOFTWARE — AprilTag 36h11 auto-alignment on Tower/Depot/Gantry faces (small error budgets at height make this mandatory, not optional), full-field pose estimation through chokepoints, auto path planning with runtime branching on the W/E Express game data, multi-robot auto deconfliction, and match-state strategy code (own-vs-place decision aids on the dashboard). STRATEGY/SCOUTING — ownership-time tracking teaches data-driven scouting beyond piece counts. Every mechanism is used every match (no control-panel dead weight): the wrist, the elevator, the climber, and the vision stack all earn points in quals AND elims.

## Buildability
Every element decomposes to a team version in <= 2 sheets of plywood + hardware-store stock, with robot-critical dimensions on round or half-inch values (11 in deck, 18/36/42/54/72 in heights, 13 in bays, 1.25 in pipe rungs, all ramps/tilts at exactly 15 degrees): UNION TOWER — four 2x4 corner posts, one sheet of 1/2 in ply for two faces, hooks are 1.25 in schedule-40 pipe nipples in floor flanges angled with 15-degree wedge blocks; headlamp optional (a colored work light or even a painted flag on a pivot). DEPOT — one sheet of 3/4 in ply (shelves + face) on a 2x4 frame; 15-degree shelf tilt from a single repeated wedge rip; semaphore paddle is a plywood arm on a 3/8 in bolt pivot with a counterweight (teams can flip it by hand during practice). FLATCAR SIDING — half a sheet of ply per three decks on 2x4 legs with 1.5 in lattice lip. GANTRY — two 4x4 posts + one 2x6 header per station, three pipe rungs on floor-flange standoffs (the 15 in offsets are just longer standoffs); lever is a gate-hinge handle linked to a plywood board with rope. GAME PIECES — crate: taped cardboard box; drum: 8 in concrete form tube + ply caps (~$8); lantern: pool-ring or taped pool noodle hoop (~$5). Nothing requires welding, CNC, or purchased field electronics; full practice field ~6 sheets of plywood and ~$250.

## Risks
- Accrual-vs-placement balance is the central tuning risk: if Tower pts/sec is even slightly hot, MAINLINE becomes 'Power Up scale, the sequel' where one lantern bot decides matches — needs playtest tuning of the 1 pt/sec rate and lantern weights (mitigation: capacity lock at 12 weight caps runaway ownership).
- The 4.5 ft chokepoint lanes concentrate defense; pinning and incidental-contact fouls near the Depots could become a referee burden reminiscent of 2026 G415 — the back-to-back face geometry helps, but G-rules around 'contact while opponent is placing' must be drafted very tightly.
- Drum upright placement on a 15-degree back-tilted shelf may drift into 'looks easy, is hard' for mid-tier teams if shelf tolerances are tight — mitigate with a 1 in retaining lip and generous 10 in shelf depth, and validate with a kitbot-grade arm before locking dimensions.
- Additive-only counterplay means a filled, out-weighed Depot face is mathematically dead territory mid-match; if both depots lock early the territory dialogue collapses into pure Tower play — may need a per-face capacity increase or a late-match 'second shelf row' unlock if playtests show early locking.
- Field indicator electronics (Headlamp, mast lamps, Express strobe) are the most failure-prone field components; every ownership state must also be human-readable from the physical semaphore paddles so a dead lamp never hides the score.
- The traversal climb may price out median teams and make GANTRY RP too rare at week-1 regionals (Crescendo-harmony risk in reverse); the 24-point regional threshold deliberately counts park+low combinations, but thresholds must be re-checked against week-1 data.

## Spectator Hook
The final 30 seconds: the whole field state is readable in pure color — the Headlamp and both semaphore paddles blazing one alliance's color while the other alliance's robots swing rung-to-rung up the gantry trusses, and then a robot hanging six feet in the air yanks the signal lever and a three-foot semaphore board CLACKS over to its color as 23 points bank on the screen. Territory lamps flipping in real time plus robots physically throwing giant switches overhead is the clip that trends after every event.
