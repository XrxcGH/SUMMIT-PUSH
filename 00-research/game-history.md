# game-history

## KEY TAKEAWAYS
- FRC games fall into two dominant scoring archetypes that alternate: shooting games (1994, 2006, 2012, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022, 2026) rewarding cycle speed and vision, and pick-and-place games (2005, 2007, 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023, 2025) rewarding precise manipulators — with outliers like racing (2008), moving-goal trailers (2009), and pass-chains (2014).
- Autonomous was introduced in 2003 (Stack Attack) and evolved from scripted routines into a decision layer: 2014 randomized hot goals, 2018 randomized plate colors requiring FMS-data branching, 2019's Sandstorm curtain allowing blind camera-driving, and 2026's 20-second auto whose winner sets the hub-shift pattern for the whole match.
- Endgame design escalated steadily: ramp parking (2000s) -> bar hangs (2004) -> buddy lifts (2007, 2010) -> deployed minibots (2011) -> balancing puzzles (2012 bridges, 2020 generator switch, 2023 charge station) -> multi-level/traversal climbs (2013, 2019 HAB triple buddy climbs, 2022 monkey bars, 2024 chains with harmony, 2025 deep cages); top tiers always demand either heavy mechanism investment or alliance coordination.
- Multi-piece games use three structures: dependency (2019 hatch-before-cargo), segregated destinations (2023 cone nodes vs cube shelves, unified by Links), and asymmetric value (2017 gears vs fuel — the failure case where one piece became near-worthless; 2025 fixed it by making algae removal a gateway and coopertition currency).
- The modern bonus Ranking Point system began in 2016 (Breach/Capture); since then every game has one scoring-volume RP and one endgame RP, which function as the minimum competitive spec sheet; 2025 raised wins from 2 to 3 RP to re-weight winning over RP farming.
- Coopertition recurs as a design theme: coined in 2000, all-cooperative alliance in 2001, shared bridge (2012), shared tote stack (2015), and since 2023 it is implemented as RP-threshold relief (co-op grid, co-op amp button, 2-algae-per-processor lowering the Coral RP).
- 2015 Recycle Rush is the great structural outlier: zero defense, parallel independent fields, average-score qualification ranking instead of win/loss — proving how ranking structure alone can transform gameplay.
- Randomized or state-driven field elements are the main anti-scripting tool: 2014 hot goals, 2016 audience-selected defenses (thousands of field configs), 2018 randomized plates, 2020 FMS-announced control panel colors, and 2026's alternating active/inactive hubs that add a timing/burst dimension to scoring.
- Game pieces trend from commodity sporting goods (tennis balls, soccer balls, inner tubes) to custom manipulator-forcing objects (2005 tetras, 2018 milk-crate cubes, 2023 rubber cones + fabric cubes, 2025 PVC coral + 16-inch algae); shooting years standardize on ~5-10 inch foam balls (2016 boulders, 2020 power cells, 2026 fuel at 5.91 inches with 504 balls on the field).
- 2026 REBUILT specifics: single game piece (Fuel foam balls into your own Hub, 1 pt only while active), 20-second auto (longest ever) determines shift advantage, teleop split into a transition shift + four 25-second alternating hub shifts + 30-second endgame, 3-rung Tower climb (10/20/30, L1 climbable in auto for 15), RPs: Energized 100 fuel, Supercharged 360 fuel, Traversal 50 tower points, win = 3 RP.
- Human players have been active scorers since 1996 (Hexagon Havoc throwing) and repeatedly became strategic actors: 2004 human shooters, 2017 airship pilots, 2024 amplify-button operators and High Note throwers, 2025 processor-to-net feeders.
- Primary sources for verification: FIRST's official game-summaries-1992-2012 PDF (firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf), team358.org history pages for early games, per-game Wikipedia articles for 2013-2026, the 2025 Game Manual Section 6 for exact REEFSCAPE values, and frcmanual.com/2026 for REBUILT.

## REPORT
# A Complete History of FIRST Robotics Competition Games (1992–2026): Structural Design Patterns

**Sources used throughout:** the official [FIRST Game Descriptions 1992–2012 PDF](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf), [Team 358's game history archive](http://team358.org/history/1992-1999/1992MaizeCraze.php), [FIRSTwiki](https://firstwiki.github.io/wiki/frc-games), the [Orlando FRC game history page](https://www.orlandofrc.org/history), the Wikipedia articles for each modern game, the [official 2025 REEFSCAPE Game Manual (Section 6, V13)](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2025/Manual/Sections/2025GameManual-06GameDetails.pdf), and [frcmanual.com's 2026 REBUILT game details](https://www.frcmanual.com/2026/game-details).

## Era 1: The Primordial Years (1992–1998) — one field, one goal, no autonomous

These games were 2-minute, fully driver-controlled matches with no autonomous and no endgame; "strategy" meant possession, denial, and defense of a shared scoring resource.

- **1992 – Maize Craze.** Four robots on a 16'×16' arena covered in a 1.5" layer of **whole corn kernels** (a deliberate traction challenge). Piece: **tennis balls** of differing point values. Mechanic: collect, return to home base, and *defend your cache*. ([FIRST summaries PDF](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf))
- **1993 – Rug Rage.** Five large air-filled **kickballs** (5 pts) and twenty small **water-filled balls** (1 pt); score into your goal and defend it; you could steal from opponents' goals. Introduced wireless robot control ([Team 358](http://team358.org/history/1992-1999/1993RugRage.php)).
- **1994 – Tower Power.** Team-colored **soccer balls** into a shared two-level goal: high goal 3 pts, low goal 1 pt — the first high/low goal risk-reward tradeoff, a pattern FRC reuses constantly (2006, 2010→, 2012, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022).
- **1995 – Ramp 'n Roll.** 24" and 30" **vinyl balls**; robots raced down a 30-ft raceway over a speed bump, then pushed/shot balls **over a 9-foot field goal**; balls could be rescored repeatedly (2 and 3 pts per score). First terrain obstacle + first "shooting over a height" mechanic.
- **1996 – Hexagon Havoc.** Hexagonal carpeted field with a **central goal**; twelve 8" balls (3 pts) and two 24" balls per team (10 pts in the hex goal, 5 pts on corner ramps); belted-in **human players could throw balls** — the origin of the human player as an active scorer ([Team 358](http://team358.org/history/1992-1999/1996HexagonHavoc.php)).
- **1997 – Toroid Terror.** Color-coded **inner tubes (toroids)** placed on pegs on a central goal or over its top — the first pick-and-place-on-peg game (ancestor of 2007 and 2011).
- **1998 – Ladder Logic.** Color-coded **balls** placed onto side goals and a tall central goal; human players could throw directly at goals. Vertical scoring hierarchy on a ladder-like structure.

**Pattern:** pieces were commodity sporting goods; scoring was possession-count at the buzzer; defense of a cache substituted for endgames.

## Era 2: Alliances, Multipliers, and Chaos Math (1999–2004)

- **1999 – Double Trouble.** First **2-team alliances**. Pieces: **"floppies"** — lightweight pillow-like discs with Velcro edges. Scoring by *elevation*: floppies held 2"–8' off the floor = 1 pt; above 8' = 3 pts. Endgame ancestor: any robot climbing onto the central raised **puck ×3 multiplied the alliance score** — a wildly swingy multiplier ([FIRST PDF](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf)).
- **2000 – Co-opertition FIRST.** The word "coopertition" enters the lexicon. 2v2; yellow balls (1 pt) and rarer black balls (5 pts) into goals; endgame points for robots on the ramp and a large bonus for **hanging from the bar** — FRC's first true hang endgame.
- **2001 – Diabolical Dynamics.** Radically experimental: **all four teams played as ONE alliance** maximizing a single score. Small balls 1 pt, large balls 10 pts; 10 pts/robot in the End Zone; **balancing the bridge doubled the score**; ending the match *early* multiplied the score up to ×3; a large ball atop a goal gave a team ×1.1. A pure cooperation experiment with stacked multipliers.
- **2002 – Zone Zeal.** First modern **2v2 alliance-vs-alliance**. Field split into 5 zones; **three 130-lb movable goals** were themselves game objects — robots stuffed soccer balls into goals, then dragged whole goals into their scoring zone, and returned home before the buzzer. Tug-of-war over goals became legendary.
- **2003 – Stack Attack.** Piece: **plastic storage containers (Rubbermaid-style totes)**. Score = containers in your zone, with **stack height** determining multipliers; robots also earned 25 pts for being atop the central ramp ("king of the hill"). **THE landmark innovation: the first 15-second autonomous period**, added to make programming matter ([FIRSTwiki](https://firstwiki.github.io/wiki/autonomous-mode), [Team 358](http://team358.org/history/2003/stackattack.php)). Autos famously raced across the ramp to bulldoze the opponent's pre-staged stack.
- **2004 – FIRST Frenzy: Raising the Bar.** Multi-piece design: **13" balls** scored by robots *passing to human players who shot* into fixed and movable goals, plus three **30" multiplier balls** that, capped on a goal, **doubled that goal's contents**; endgame: **hang from a 10-ft bar (50 pts)**. Auto used IR beacons to find and knock down bonus balls. This is the template year: multi-piece economy + multiplier piece + big hang.

## Era 3: The Modern Template Forms (2005–2014)

- **2005 – Triple Play.** First **3v3 alliances** (permanent ever since). Piece: pyramidal **tetras**. Nine goals in a 3×3 tic-tac-toe matrix; capping a goal claimed *ownership*; **rows of three owned goals scored 10 pts** — territorial control layered on pick-and-place at height (tetras capped ~7-ft goals). Auto featured a camera-tracked **vision tetra**.
- **2006 – Aim High.** Return of shooting: foam **"poof" balls** launched into a raised center high goal (3 pts) vs. floor corner goals (1 pt), with a green vision target for camera-aimed **autonomous shooting**. Endgame: robots raced back and **climbed a ramp to a platform** for bonus points. Match structure (auto / teleop / endgame sprint) is now recognizable.
- **2007 – Rack 'N' Roll.** Pure pick-and-place: inflated **tubes ("ringers")** hung on pegs of a 10-ft central **Rack**; scoring was **exponential in row length** (2^(n−1) per contiguous line), with auto-placed **Keepers** locking spots and **Spoilers** negating opponents' tubes. Endgame: 15 pts for each robot in the home zone **lifted ≥4" off the floor by a partner robot** — the first buddy lift ([FIRST PDF](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf)).
- **2008 – FIRST Overdrive.** A racing game: robots did counterclockwise **laps** around a center barrier while moving **40" inflated Trackballs** over or under a 6'6" **overpass** (hurdling = big points); endgame: placing Trackballs back onto the overpass (12 pts). Innovation: auto became **"Hybrid mode"** — a human **Robocoach** could send IR-remote signals to the robot ([Wikipedia – FIRST Overdrive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIRST_Overdrive)).
- **2009 – Lunacy.** The physics-troll year: played on slick **"regolith"** (low-friction polymer floor) with mandated slippery wheels to simulate lunar gravity. Pieces: 9" **Orbit Balls ("moon rocks," 2 pts)** scored into **trailers hitched to opponent robots** — the goals *moved and evaded you*. **Super Cells** (15 pts) scored only in the last 20 seconds, and human-player **payload logistics** (empty cells delivered to be exchanged) created a mid-match supply chain. No climb; the whole game was relative motion.
- **2010 – Breakaway.** Soccer on a 27'×54' field split into three zones by **bumps with tunnels** beneath; balls into corner goals = 1 pt each (kicking/herding, since possession >3 sec was illegal). Endgame: robots **suspended off the floor from tower bars = 2 pts**, and a robot **suspended from another robot = 3 pts** — the first chained buddy-hang ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakaway_(FIRST))).
- **2011 – Logo Motion.** FIRST's 20th anniversary: **triangle/circle/square inner tubes** hung on wall pegs (1/2/3 pts by row height), **doubled** by assembling the FIRST logo order; auto **Ubertubes** scored double and doubled tubes later hung on the same peg. Endgame: deploy a **Minibot** (an FTC-legal mini robot) onto a pole for a **race** — 30/20/15/10 pts by finish order — a robot-deploys-robot mechanic never repeated ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo_Motion)).
- **2012 – Rebound Rumble.** Foam **basketballs** into three heights of hoops; auto ("Hybrid") could use **Microsoft Kinect gesture control** ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/first-adds-kinect-technology-to-robotics-competition-tool-kit/)). Endgame: **balancing 1–3 robots on seesaw bridges**; crucially, a shared white **Coopertition Bridge** balanced by robots from *both* alliances gave both seeding points — coopertition became a scored in-match mechanic.
- **2013 – Ultimate Ascent.** **Flying discs** shot into low/middle/high goals (1/2/3 pts teleop, doubled in auto; 5-pt colored-disc pyramid-top goal). Endgame: **climb a 3-level pyramid** for 10/20/30 pts — the first vertical multi-level climb ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Ascent)).
- **2014 – Aerial Assist.** One giant **2-ft exercise ball** per scoring cycle; goals at each end (high 10 pts / low 1 pt) but the genius was the **Assist bonus**: each additional alliance robot that possessed the ball in a different field zone before scoring added 10/30 bonus points, plus 10 pts for throwing over the midfield **truss** and 10 for a partner **catch**. Auto had randomized **"hot goals"** (5-pt bonus for scoring in the lit goal) — the first randomized-at-match-start element. No climb; the endgame was the scoring rally itself. It made *passing* the meta ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_Assist)).

## Era 4: The Ranking-Point Era (2015–2026)

- **2015 – Recycle Rush.** The great outlier: **no defense and no robot interaction** — alliances scored in parallel on separate field halves, and qualification ranking was by **average match score**, not W/L. Pieces: gray **totes** (2 pts each per level stacked), green **recycling containers** (4 pts × level, up to 24 atop a 6-stack), and **litter (pool noodles)** stuffed in containers (6 pts) or thrown into the opponent's zone. Auto: moving robots/totes/containers into the Auto Zone, including a 20-pt **stacked-tote set**. Coopertition: both alliances jointly stacking 4 totes on the center step = 20 pts each. No endgame climb; the "endgame" was capping stacks before the buzzer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recycle_Rush)).
- **2016 – FIRST Stronghold.** Medieval siege: **10" foam Boulders** shot into a tower's **high goal (7'1", 5 pts)** or low goal (2 pts) after crossing **randomized defenses** — each alliance selected three of eight obstacle types (moat, drawbridge, portcullis, cheval de frise, ramparts, rock wall, rough terrain, sally port), one was **audience-selected**, and the low bar was fixed, giving thousands of field configurations. Auto: reach (2 pts) or cross (10 pts) a defense. Endgame: **Challenge** (park on the batter, 5) or **Scale** the tower (15). Introduced the **modern bonus Ranking Point system**: RP for **Breaching** (damaging 4 defenses) and **Capturing** (weakening the tower with 8+ boulders and surrounding it) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIRST_Stronghold)).
- **2017 – FIRST Steamworks.** Dual-piece economy with **asymmetric value**: 5" wiffle **Fuel** balls (high boiler goal = 1 kPa each, low = 1/3 kPa; kPa = points) vs. 11" plastic **Gears** delivered to the airship, where **human pilots aboard the airship** cranked rotors (1/2/4/6 gears per successive rotor, 40 pts each in teleop, 60 in auto). Endgame: **rope climb** to a touchpad, 50 pts. RPs: 40+ kPa and all 4 rotors. The lesson learned: gears dominated fuel — a cautionary tale on balancing dual-piece economies ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIRST_Steamworks)).
- **2018 – FIRST Power Up.** Piece: 13" yellow **Power Cubes**. Scoring was **time-based ownership**, not placement count: tip your **Switch** or the center **Scale** with cubes and accrue ~1 pt/second while owned. The headline innovation: **plate colors randomized at match start** (announced seconds before auto), forcing autos with **three-path decision logic** reading the FMS game data. **Vault** cubes bought **Power-Ups** (Force, Boost, Levitate — Levitate granted a free climb). Endgame: climb the Scale rung (30 pts); RPs for **Auto Quest** (all cross line + own switch in auto) and **Face the Boss** (all three robots climbing, enabling buddy-climb bars) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIRST_Power_Up)).
- **2019 – Destination: Deep Space.** Dual pieces with a **dependency chain**: 19" polycarbonate **Hatch Panels** (2 pts) had to seal a bay *before* 13" orange **Cargo** balls (3 pts) could be scored in it — on two 3-level **Rockets** and a central **Cargo Ship**. The auto innovation: the **Sandstorm** — a physical curtain blocked drivers' view for 15 seconds; teams chose between pre-programmed autos or **driving blind via robot cameras**. Endgame: climb back onto the 3-level **HAB platform** (3/6/12 pts), producing iconic **buddy-climb triple-Level-3** lifts. RPs: complete Rocket; 15+ HAB climb points ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destination:_Deep_Space)).
- **2020/2021 – Infinite Recharge.** 7" foam **Power Cells** shot into a 3-tier **Power Port** (bottom 1, outer 2, inner 3; doubled in auto); scoring thresholds unlocked **Stages**: spinning the **Control Panel** 3–5 rotations (10 pts), then rotating it to an **FMS-announced color** (20 pts + RP path) — a randomized *mid-match* task. Endgame: hang from the **Generator Switch**, a giant balancing beam (25 pts/robot, +15 if level); Shield RP at 65+ endgame points. COVID canceled the 2020 season mid-March; **2021** replayed it as **Infinite Recharge at Home** — remote **skills challenges** (AutoNav, Hyperdrive, Interstellar Accuracy, Power Port, Galactic Search) plus a **Game Design Challenge**, the only non-standard season ever ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Recharge), [Orlando FRC](https://www.orlandofrc.org/history)).
- **2022 – Rapid React.** 9.5" inflated **Cargo** (oversized tennis balls) shot into the central omnidirectional **Hub** (upper 2 pts / lower 1, doubled in auto). Endgame: the **Hangar** — traverse four ascending **monkey-bar rungs** (Low 4, Mid 6, High 10, **Traversal 15**), with swinging rung-to-rung climbs. RPs: 20 cargo (18 with a 5-cargo "quintet" auto) and 16 hangar points ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_React)).
- **2023 – Charged Up.** The definitive **two-piece pick-and-place** game: yellow rubber **Cones** (~12" tall, square base) and inflated purple fabric **Cubes** (~9.5") placed on a 3×9 **Grid** per alliance — bottom hybrid row (any piece), middle and top rows with **cone-only posts and cube-only shelves** (2–3 / 3–4 / 5–6 pts by row and period). Three adjacent filled nodes formed a **Link** (5 pts); **Sustainability RP** for enough links, with a **Coopertition grid** lowering the threshold. The **Charge Station** — a balancing seesaw — served both auto (dock 8 / engage 12) and endgame (6/10 per robot, all-three balances iconic); **Activation RP** at 26 charge points ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charged_Up_(FIRST))).
- **2024 – Crescendo.** Piece: orange foam **Note** rings (10" ID / 14" OD). Dual goals with a **human-in-the-loop multiplier**: Notes into your **Amp** (1 pt) banked charges that human players spent to **Amplify the Speaker** (2 pts → 4 pts for 10 seconds / 4 notes) — scoring tempo management as strategy. Endgame **Stage**: climb hanging **chains** (3 pts), **Harmony** (+2 for two robots on one chain), human players threw **High Notes** onto microphone pegs to **Spotlight** climbers (+1), and Notes in the elevated **Trap** = 5. Coopertition button (both alliances Amp a note early) lowered the **Melody RP** (18→15 notes); **Ensemble RP** = 10 stage points + 2 climbers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescendo_(FIRST))).
- **2025 – REEFSCAPE.** Dual pieces again: **Coral** (11.875" lengths of 4" PVC pipe) placed on a hexagonal **Reef** at four levels — L1 trough 3/2 pts (auto/teleop), L2 branch 4/3, L3 6/4, L4 (6 ft) 7/5 — and 16" **Algae** balls removed from the reef and scored in the **Processor** (6 pts, which *feeds the opponent's human player* a net shot) or thrown into the **Net** (4 pts). Endgame **Barge**: park 2, **shallow Cage 6, deep Cage 12** (deep cages hang near the floor, demanding powerful lifting climbs). RPs (final manual): Auto RP (all leave + 1 auto coral), **Coral RP** (≥7 coral on every level; Coopertition — 2 algae in each processor — cuts it to 3 levels), **Barge RP** (16+ pts); **wins now worth 3 RP** ([2025 Manual V13](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2025/Manual/Sections/2025GameManual-06GameDetails.pdf), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reefscape)).
- **2026 – REBUILT (presented by Haas).** Piece: **Fuel** — 504 (!) yellow high-density foam balls, 5.91" diameter, ~0.5 lb, up to 8 preloaded — a return to high-volume ball-shooting. Each alliance shoots into its own **Hub**, but the structural twist is **alternating Hub activation**: a **20-second auto** (longest ever) is followed by a 10-s transition, then **four 25-second Alliance Shifts** in which the hubs alternate active/inactive — and **the alliance that wins auto chooses the shift pattern advantage** (auto winner's hub is inactive in Shift 1, giving them the active hub in the final shift before endgame); both hubs are live in transition and the 30-s endgame. Fuel = 1 pt in an active hub, 0 in an inactive one — making *when* you score as important as how fast. Endgame/auto: climb the ladder-like **Tower** (L1 = 10 teleop/15 auto, L2 = 20, L3 = 30). RPs: win 3 / tie 1, plus **Energized** (100 fuel), **Supercharged** (360 fuel), **Traversal** (50 tower points) ([frcmanual.com](https://www.frcmanual.com/2026/game-details), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebuilt_(FIRST))).

## Structural Design Patterns Across 35 Seasons

**1. The scoring-mechanic pendulum.** FRC alternates between *shooting* games (1995, 2006, 2012, 2013 (hybrid), 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022, 2024 (hybrid), 2026) and *pick-and-place* games (1997, 2003, 2005, 2007, 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023, 2025), with outliers: racing (2008), moving-goal chaos (2002, 2009), and pass-centric play (2014). Shooting games reward cycle speed and turret/vision tech; placement games reward precise manipulators and reward defense more (a misplaced cone is worse than a missed shot).

**2. High/low goal risk-reward is the oldest tool in the box** (1994 → 2026): every shooting game offers a trivial low goal so rookie robots score, and a hard high/inner goal so elite robots differentiate.

**3. Multi-piece economies come in three flavors:** *dependency* (2019: hatch must precede cargo — forces every team to handle both), *segregation* (2023: cone nodes vs cube shelves — allows specialization but links reward mixed rows), and *asymmetric value* (2017: gears >> fuel — the failure mode where one piece becomes strategically dead; 2025 fixed this by making algae *removal* a prerequisite for reef access and a coopertition/RP currency).

**4. Autonomous evolved from novelty to decision point.** Introduced in 2003 as scripted 15-second routines; 2004 added IR beacons; 2008 and 2012 tried human-signaled "hybrid" modes (Robocoach, Kinect); 2014's randomized hot goals and 2018's **randomized plate colors** forced autos to *read game state and branch*; 2019's Sandstorm let camera-driving substitute for code; 2020's doubled auto point values made auto the meta; 2026 makes auto **determine match structure itself** (shift order) and extends it to 20 seconds.

**5. Endgames escalated from parking to acrobatics:** ramp parking (2000, 2003, 2006) → bar hangs (2000, 2004) → buddy lifts/hangs (2007, 2010) → deployed minibots (2011) → balancing puzzles (2012 bridges, 2020 generator switch, 2023 charge station) → multi-level climbs (2013 pyramid, 2016 tower, 2019 HAB with triple buddy climbs, 2022 traversal rungs, 2024 chains with harmony, 2025 deep cages, 2026 tower). The recurring design lever: make the top tier require either extreme mechanism investment or alliance coordination (buddy climbs, harmony, levitate power-up).

**6. Coopertition mechanics recur roughly every decade:** 2000 (name coined), 2001 (all-cooperative match), 2012 (shared bridge), 2015 (shared step stack), 2023 (co-op grid lowers Link RP), 2024 (co-op button lowers Melody RP), 2025 (co-op algae lowers Coral RP + tiebreaker point). Modern designs convert coopertition into *RP-threshold relief*, so it aligns with ranking incentives instead of fighting them.

**7. Ranking structures shape robot design as much as points do.** 2015's average-score ranking (no W/L) produced the least interactive game ever; 2016's bonus-RP invention (Breach/Capture) permanently added two "build a robot that can do X" objectives independent of winning; 2025–26 raised wins to 3 RP to re-weight winning vs. RP farming. Since 2016, every game's two bonus RPs (one scoring-volume, one endgame) are the de facto minimum spec sheet for competitive robots.

**8. Randomization and mid-match state changes** keep autonomous and strategy from being fully scripted: 2014 hot goals, 2016 audience-picked defense, 2018 plate randomization, 2020 FMS-announced control panel color, 2026 auto-determined hub shifts. The 2026 active/inactive hub is the most aggressive version yet — it injects a *timing* dimension (score bursts, hoard during inactive shifts) never before present.

**9. Game pieces trend from commodity to custom:** tennis balls, soccer balls, and kickballs (1992–2004) gave way to purpose-built objects that force manipulator innovation — tetras (2005), toroids (1997/2011/2024), milk-crate cubes (2018), fabric cubes and rubber cones (2023), PVC coral (2025) — while shooting years standardize on foam balls in the 5–10" range for safe high-volume launching.
