# FIRST ASCENT℠ presented by Ridgeline Outfitters

**Theme:** High-alpine mountaineering: two expedition alliances race up opposite flanks of the same peak, stocking their high camps with supplies before a storm closes the mountain — and then making the summit push themselves.

**Narrative:** The forecast window is closing. Two rival expeditions have 2 minutes and 30 seconds to ferry crates, oxygen, and rope up the mountain, establish their camps before the storm hits, and rope up for the final headwall push. Every camp you light is shelter earned; every rung you climb is history written. The mountain doesn't care about your plans — only your preparation. This season, be the FIRST ASCENT.

## Game Pieces
### CACHE CRATE
- Shape/dims: Cube, 12.0 in on each side, slightly pillowed faces, ~2.0 lb
- Material: Sewn ripstop-nylon skin over a polyurethane foam core (Power Up cube construction — proven durable, teams can sew their own or build practice versions from upholstery foam + duct tape for under $10)
- Handling challenge: Large and compliant: forces a wide ground intake or clamping claw and a flat, controlled placement onto shelves. Too big to hoard more than one under a 120 in frame perimeter with other mechanisms aboard — one-at-a-time cycling.
- Count/staging: 20 total: 3 in the neutral Center Cache on the centerline, 2 staged per alliance side at taped marks, up to 1 preloaded per robot, remainder (8+ per alliance) stocked at the alliance's two Outfitter chutes
- Scoring role: The volume piece. Cheapest points per second, backbone of the Supply Line RP, and the required 'shelter' component of every Camp bonus. The kitbot piece.

### O2 CELL
- Shape/dims: Cylinder, 5.0 in diameter x 14.0 in long, domed foam end caps, ~1.5 lb
- Material: 4 in Schedule-40 PVC core wrapped in 0.5 in EVA foam sleeve with molded foam caps (team version: 4 in PVC + pipe-insulation foam + tape, ~$8)
- Handling challenge: Rolls on the carpet and must be reoriented from horizontal to near-vertical and INSERTED into an angled socket — forces a wrist/end-effector with orientation control and vision-assisted alignment. Insertion clearance is a forgiving 0.75 in per side (6.5 in ID sockets) so it looks harder than it is.
- Count/staging: 20 total: 3 in the neutral Center Cache, 2 staged per alliance side, up to 1 preloaded per robot, remainder at Outfitter chutes
- Scoring role: The precision piece. Highest points-per-slot at mid level, gatekeeper of the High Camp via the Summit Socket, and the 'oxygen' component of every Camp bonus.

### ROPE COIL
- Shape/dims: Torus (ring), 10.0 in outside diameter, 2.5 in tube cross-section, ~1.0 lb
- Material: Solid molded rubber/foam ring (large rubber tug-ring dog toy class — durable, cheap, survives being driven over; team version: pool foam ring or garden-hose coil wrapped in tape)
- Handling challenge: Thin and floppy relative to the others: needs a dedicated hook/spear or pinch gripper and a precise hang onto a 1.5 in peg angled 45 deg upward — including the 78 in high pegs at the extension ceiling, where robot stability and pose control matter.
- Count/staging: 20 total: 3 in the neutral Center Cache, 2 staged per alliance side, up to 1 preloaded per robot, remainder at Outfitter chutes
- Scoring role: The altitude piece. Only piece that scores at the 78 in tier, dominant in High Camp math, and the 'rope' component of every Camp bonus. Its 45 deg pegs echo a climber clipping protection — thematically legible.

## Field Layout
Standard 27 ft x 54 ft carpeted field, ROTATIONALLY symmetric (Rapid React style). MIDFIELD — THE CRAGS: each alliance owns one CRAG, a 4 ft x 4 ft footprint, 7 ft 6 in tall plywood-and-pipe rock spire centered on the field centerline, offset 7 ft from opposite long guardrails (Red Crag 7 ft from the scoring-table rail, Blue Crag 7 ft from the far rail). The 9 ft band between the two Crags is the CENTER CACHE: 9 neutral pieces (3 of each type) staged at taped marks straddling the centerline. Because both alliances score at midfield, all six robots cycle through and cross paths in the same central corridor — contested space by geometry, not forced collisions; each Crag scores on ALL FOUR faces so a defender can never blockade every approach. ALLIANCE ENDS: each alliance wall has three driver stations; the two wall corners each hold an OUTFITTER chute (human-player feed, 3 ft wide taped Outfitter Lane in front of each is a no-defense zone). Centered 4 ft in front of each alliance wall stands the HEADWALL, a 12 ft wide, 15-deg-inclined climbing truss (endgame), with the taped BASECAMP zone (12 ft x 4 ft) beneath it — physically separated from the midfield scoring flow, so endgame traffic never collides with late cycles. Each alliance side also has 6 staged pieces (2 of each type) at taped marks 12 ft from its wall. START ZONES: robots start in their Basecamp zone touching the alliance wall or Headwall. AprilTags (36h11): 4 per Crag (one per face, centered 48 in high), 1 above each Outfitter chute, 3 across each Headwall crossbeam — 22 tags total, full-field pose coverage. Tier LED rings wrap each Crag at 30 in, 54 in, and 78 in and blaze alliance-color when a Camp is established; a white LED band marks the pre-announced Storm Tier.

## Match Flow
2:30 match = 15 s AUTO + 2:15 TELEOP (final 30 s is the endgame window with climb protection). A cycle is: collect at your Outfitter corner chute or from staged/loose floor pieces, drive ~20 ft to your midfield Crag, place, return. Both alliances' cycles converge on the same central corridor between the Crags, so robots constantly cross paths; defense is legal everywhere except opponent Outfitter Lanes, robots in contact with their own Headwall in the last 30 s, and pinning limits (5 s count). Because each Crag scores on all four faces, a single defender delays but cannot shut out — smart defenders shade the Center Cache and the corridor instead of camping a face (no intake-vs-intake G415 trap). Spectators read the match at a glance: pieces physically accumulate on the two spires, and the LED tier rings light as Camps are established — a fully lit mountain is a winning mountain. Typical strong-alliance match: 25-35 auto, 20-28 teleop placements, 2-3 Camps (30-60 bonus), 40-65 climb; scores in the 160-220 range with median-alliance matches near 100.

## Autonomous
15 seconds, deeply interactive. THE FORECAST: at the moment auto begins, FMS pushes a game-data string — WHITEOUT, ICEFALL, or GALE — and the field LEDs flash it. Each forecast fixes two things for the whole match: the PRIORITY SUPPLY (Whiteout=Crates, Icefall=O2, Gale=Ropes), which scores DOUBLE auto points, and the STORM TIER (Whiteout=Low, Icefall=Mid, Gale=High), whose Camp bonus pays double all match and anchors the Expedition RP. Robots must read game data and branch autos in code (2018-style, three prepared paths minimum) — pure software teaching value. CHOICES: score your safe own-side staged pieces, or race the contested Center Cache on the centerline where opposing autos arrive simultaneously (higher risk, and the only way to run 4+ piece autos). LEAVE: 3 pts per robot for fully exiting Basecamp. ROPED UP bonus: if all three robots Leave AND the alliance scores ≥4 pieces including at least one of EACH type in auto, +10 — this forces genuine alliance coordination (who takes crates, who takes O2, who takes ropes, deconflicted paths agreed in the queue). Vision: AprilTags on every Crag face enable auto-align for socket insertion and peg hangs; center-cache autos require full-field pose estimation. Auto consequences ripple forward: the Storm Tier revealed at auto start dictates teleop Camp strategy, and Camp progress banked in auto (auto placements count toward Camps) sets up the double-Storm-Camp race.

## Endgame
THE HEADWALL: a 12 ft wide truss inclined 15 deg from vertical, standing 4 ft in front of each alliance wall, divided into THREE independent 4 ft lanes so all three robots climb side-by-side with zero traffic (no 2019-HAB pileups). Each lane carries three 1.5 in OD rungs at 30 in (LEDGE), 54 in (CAMP), and 78 in (SUMMIT); because the wall leans 15 deg, each rung sits ~6.5 in behind the one below, and rungs are staggered 12 in laterally within the lane — reaching the Summit is a genuine two-handoff traversal on an inclined, offset ladder (2022-traversal-class problem: hook geometry, CG management, swing damping, sequencing state machine). POINTS (multi-tier partial credit, no cliff): PARK in Basecamp = 3; supported by Ledge rung = 12; Camp rung = 20; Summit rung = 30. A robot that attempts Summit and falls back to Camp still scores 20; a failed climber still parks for 3 — every stage of the attempt banks something. Scored at match end + 5 s settle, per the highest rung solely supporting the robot (no carpet contact). Robots in contact with their own Headwall during the final 30 s are protected from opponent contact. WHY IT'S TOUGH: the 15-deg lean pushes the robot's CG away from the wall, the lateral stagger defeats simple telescoping hooks, and a 78 in reach sits at the extension ceiling. WHY IT'S REWARDING: 30 points is 6-10 placement cycles — enough to swing any close match — and three simultaneous swinging traversals framed against the alliance wall is the loudest 20 seconds of the event. It can decide a match without ever invalidating two minutes of cycling (max alliance climb 90 vs. typical 100+ from supplies/camps).

## Ranking Points
Win = 3 RP, Tie = 1 RP, plus up to 3 bonus RPs that all reinforce the core game:

| Bonus RP | Regional | DCMP | Champs | Design intent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SUPPLY LINE RP — total supplies scored on Crag + Depot | ≥15 | ≥19 | ≥23 | Entry-volume RP: three median robots at ~5 pieces each hit it ~50% of matches; every kitbot Depot piece counts |
| EXPEDITION RP — Camps established | 2 Camps incl. the Storm Tier camp | All 3 Camps | All 3 Camps AND Storm Tier at full capacity (every slot of that tier filled) | Stretch RP at ~3x entry difficulty: demands coordinated tri-piece scoring at height; the forecast makes the target rotate match-to-match so it never collapses into one rehearsed pattern |
| ASCENT RP — alliance endgame climb points | ≥40 | ≥52 | ≥62 | Exactly 'two robots do the mid-tier action' (2 x Camp rung = 40); escalates toward one Summit + support |

All three RPs are earned by doing MORE of the main game (placing supplies, completing tiers, climbing) — no side-quest mechanisms.

## Low Floor
The documented KITBOT PATH (published like the 2024 KitBot): AM14U-class drivetrain + a fixed-height roller-claw that handles only Cache Crates. That robot: preloads a crate and scores it in auto (Depot, 4 pts) + Leaves (3), then cycles crates all match into the Depot (2 each) and Shelf 1 at a friendly 24 in (3 each) — every one of which counts toward the Supply Line RP and the Camp I crate slot — and parks for 3 at the end. That is 20-30 honest points and RP-relevant contribution every single match, plus a natural graduation ladder: add a wrist, you unlock O2 sockets; add an elevator, Shelf 2 and mid tiers; add hooks, the Ledge rung. Perceived difficulty matches actual difficulty everywhere (s-neff test): the Depot and 24 in shelf look easy and ARE easy; the Summit Socket and traversal look hard and ARE hard. No task humiliates a rookie for trying.

## High Ceiling
Elite teams chase: (1) the universal-vs-specialist end-effector problem — a gripper that handles a 12 in compliant cube, a 5 in cylinder needing 90-deg reorientation, AND a floppy 10 in ring is heavy and slow, so weight forces archetypes: Crate Freighter (volume/low-mid), O2 Surgeon (socket insertion, Summit Socket owner), Ring Alpinist (78 in pegs + Summit rung), and Hybrid mid-tier; no single robot covers the mountain under 115 lb. (2) 4-6 piece Center Cache autos with contested-space path planning and forecast branching. (3) Sub-4-second AprilTag-aligned socket insertions. (4) A repeatable 15-second Summit traversal. The meta stays unsolved because the Forecast rotates the doubled Storm Tier every match (alliance value shifts nightly), Camp completion makes MIXED alliances outvalue three copies of the best robot (2015's fatal monoculture is structurally impossible), and the depot/low-tier economy leaves room for a late-season 'L1-style' revaluation once high tiers saturate at champs-level play.

## Teaching Value
This is a full-curriculum game by construction. MECHANICAL: ground intake (three geometries — students must prototype and down-select), serializer/indexer (piece must travel from intake to placement pose), the canonical elevator-vs-arm decision (flat shelves favor elevators; 45-deg pegs and 30-deg sockets favor arms; the 78 in tier forces continuous elevators or two-stage arms), wrist/end-effector design (O2 reorientation is a classic 2-DOF wrist problem), and a real climber with hook geometry, ratchets, and CG analysis for the staggered traversal. SOFTWARE: game-data parsing and multi-branch autos (Forecast), AprilTag auto-align for socket/peg placement, full-field pose estimation for Center Cache races, mechanism state machines (piece-type modes), and climb sequencing. STRATEGY: scouting must track per-piece capability and Storm Tier performance — a genuine pick-list exercise. Every subsystem a student builds is used in every match; nothing is a control-panel that dies in week 3.

## Buildability
TEAM VERSIONS (each element ≤2 sheets of 3/4 in plywood + hardware-store stock): CRAG — 2 sheets of plywood (four faces + two shelves + depot tray), 1.5 in closet dowel or Schedule-40 pipe cut at 45 deg for pegs, three 18 in lengths of 6 in Sonotube concrete form (~$15) screwed on at 30/15 deg for sockets, 2x4 internal frame; total <$180. HEADWALL — one plywood sheet + 2x4 A-frame lumber leaned at 15 deg (a speed-square angle) + three 4 ft lengths of 1.25 in black iron pipe as rungs on stagger brackets; a single-lane practice version fits in any shop. GAME PIECES — crates: upholstery foam + duct tape or sewn ripstop (~$10); O2 cells: 4 in PVC + pipe insulation (~$8); rope coils: large rubber ring dog toys or foam pool rings (~$8), all durable enough to be driven over (dog-toy-cargo doctrine, no fragile 2024-note foam). Every robot-critical dimension is a round or half-inch value: 12 in cube, 5 in cylinder, 10 in ring, 1.5 in pegs/rungs, heights at 24/30/42/54/66/78 in, angles ONLY at 15/30/45 deg — no 35-deg-branch traps anywhere on the field.

## Risks
- Midfield congestion around the Crags could stress pinning/contact refereeing in elims; four-face scoring and wide corridors mitigate it, but a disciplined two-defender strategy might still choke the center corridor and needs playtesting with real drive teams.
- Weight-forced specialization means a regional alliance drawn without an O2- or ring-capable partner may be mathematically locked out of the Expedition RP — the Depot 'any piece' path softens this, but qual-schedule luck could sting at small events.
- The Summit traversal may see <10% success in weeks 1-2 (Steamworks-climb frustration pattern); the 12/20-point intermediate rungs are the pressure valve, but the stagger distance (12 in) and rung diameter need physical prototyping before the number is locked.
- O2 socket insertion tolerance (0.75 in per side) is designed to be forgiving, but if field-build variance shrinks it, the task flips from 'looks hard, is achievable' to s-neff frustration — the socket ID must be a strictly-toleranced field dimension.
- The Forecast mechanic requires FMS game-data support; offseason and practice-field play needs a published fallback (dice/card draw + manual LED), or the interactive auto quietly degrades to a scripted one.
- Hanging rings on 78 in pegs at the 78 in extension ceiling invites high-CG tip-overs during defense; the no-contact rule near the Crag faces was deliberately NOT added to preserve defense, so tip-over frequency must be monitored in week-zero events.

## Spectator Hook
The final 30 seconds: both mountains glowing with earned Camp LEDs while six robots hit the Headwalls at once — three per alliance, side by side, swinging hand-over-hand up staggered rungs on a leaning wall, every rung reached banking points out loud, with a 30-point Summit grab on the buzzer able to flip the match. It is Rapid React's traversal drama times three lanes, framed directly in front of the crowd.
