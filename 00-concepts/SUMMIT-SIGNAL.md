# SUMMIT SIGNAL℠ presented by Kernel Peak Outfitters

**Theme:** High-altitude expedition crews race an incoming storm to rebuild and relight a dormant mountain signal-beacon network before nightfall.

**Narrative:** The storm is six hours out and the ridge has gone dark. For a century, the beacon line above Kernel Peak carried warnings from summit to valley — until the network failed. Two expedition alliances ascend with rings of cable, fuel cells, and signal flares, assembling beacons tower by tower until the mountainside blazes back to life. When the last flare seats and the summit lights, the ones who climb highest before the storm closes in will be the ones remembered.

## Game Pieces
### CACHE RING
- Shape/dims: Torus (ring): 14 in outside diameter, 4 in cross-section, 6 in inner hole. Weight ~1.3 lb.
- Material: Die-cut laminated EVA foam ring with a tear-resistant spiral fabric wrap (deliberately more durable than a 2024 Note). Team version: two 2.5-in pool noodles coiled and gaffer-taped into a 14-in torus.
- Handling challenge: Lies flat on carpet — forces an over-bumper ground intake and a re-orientation path from horizontal (floor) to horizontal-above-post (drop-on) or vertical (hang on 30-degree Signal Hooks). The 6-in hole over a 1.9-in post is forgiving, but stacking cleanly at 76 in demands elevator + wrist control.
- Count/staging: 14 per alliance in Supply Station stock, 4 staged on centerline marks, available as preload. Highest-count piece in the game.
- Scoring role: Volume currency. Cheapest piece (3-8 pts by height), rings drive the Signal RP piece count and form the base layers of every beacon. The bread-and-butter cycle piece for low-floor and high-ceiling teams alike.

### FUEL CELL
- Shape/dims: Spool (thick-walled open cylinder): 10 in diameter x 10 in tall with a 4.5-in through-bore. Weight ~2.5 lb.
- Material: Molded dense polyethylene foam spool with a cordura skin and reinforced bore liner. Team version: 10-in Sonotube core wrapped in pool-noodle foam, plywood-ring ends.
- Handling challenge: Bulky and must be presented dead-vertical to thread its 4.5-in bore over the post — forces a wide intake, indexing/orientation control (it arrives from the chute on its side), and a wrist or turret that holds it plumb at up to 82 in. The mid-weight, mid-precision piece.
- Count/staging: 10 per alliance in Supply Station stock, 3 staged on centerline marks, available as preload.
- Scoring role: Mid-value structural piece (4-9 pts). A beacon cannot light without at least one Cell beneath the Flare, so Cells are the bottleneck resource elite alliances manage for the Network RP.

### FLARE
- Shape/dims: Cone: 10-in-square base, 12 in tall, with a 3-in-diameter x 4-in-deep socket molded up into the base center. Weight ~1.8 lb.
- Material: Semi-rigid molded PVC cone with rubberized skin and retro-reflective tip band (functionally a re-engineered 12-in traffic cone). Team version: an off-the-shelf 12-in traffic cone, skirt trimmed to 10 in square.
- Handling challenge: The precision piece. Its blind socket must seat over the tapered post tip — a Charged-Up-cone-style grip plus tip-up pose control at heights from 38 to 82 in. The socket is 1 in oversize and post tips are chamfered, so it is a funnel fit, not a threading operation — it looks harder than it is.
- Count/staging: 8 per alliance in Supply Station stock, 3 staged on centerline marks, available as preload.
- Scoring role: The capstone. Highest per-piece value (5-11 pts), and seating a Flare on a post that already holds a Ring and a Cell LIGHTS THE BEACON — locking the stack, triggering the tower LEDs, and paying the +6/+10 completion bonus. Flares are the win-condition piece and the star of the First Light auto.

## Field Layout
Standard 27 ft x 54 ft carpeted field, rotationally symmetric. From each alliance wall outward: (1) STARTING ZONE — a 10-ft-deep zone against the alliance wall with three marked start slots. (2) ASCENT WALL — a 12-ft-wide x 8-ft-tall lumber-and-pipe endgame structure in the right-hand corner against the alliance wall (out of all scoring traffic, 2022-hangar-style), with three separated climb lanes. (3) RIDGELINE — the alliance's scoring arc, 10 ft from the centerline, spanning the field width: LOW TOWER (left third, two posts, base plate 8 in, post tips 38 in), MID TOWER (center third, two posts, base 28 in, tips 58 in, plus two SIGNAL HOOKS — 18-in pegs angled 30 degrees above horizontal at 40 in height on the tower's outer faces), HIGH TOWER (right third, one post, base 46 in, tip 76 in). All posts are 1.9-in OD with chamfered tips; every post accepts a maximum of 4 pieces, Flare always last. A CACHE DEPOT — a 4 ft x 8 ft plywood floor tray with a 2-in lip — sits at the foot of each Ridgeline. (4) THE SUMMIT — a single shared center structure, 48 in x 48 in footprint, 30 in truss base, carrying one alliance-colored post per side on opposite faces (base 52 in, tip 82 in) — the highest-value posts in the game, 48 in apart so opposing robots score back-to-back, never intake-to-intake. (5) SUPPLY STATION — each alliance's human-player chute is in the FAR corner (diagonal, beside the opposing alliance wall, opposite side from the opposing Ascent Wall), so every cycle crosses the full field through shared neutral space. Ten neutral pieces (4 Rings, 3 Cells, 3 Flares) are staged on marks along the centerline flanking the Summit. AprilTags (36h11) on every tower face, both Summit faces, Supply Stations, and Ascent Walls.

## Match Flow
2:30 total — 15 s AUTO, 2:15 TELEOP with the final 30 s as ENDGAME. The core loop: collect from the far-corner Supply Station or the floor, carry diagonally across the field through neutral space, and place on your Ridgeline or the Summit. Because each alliance's pickup is diagonal from its scoring arc, all six robots continuously cross paths through the middle — contested space by geometry, not forced collisions; the Summit's back-to-back post layout means the highest-value cycles happen 4 ft from an opponent without intake-vs-intake contact. Defense is legal everywhere except the opponent's Supply Station protected zone and Ascent Wall zones during the last 30 s. Archetypes diverge: ring-volume cyclers hammer Low/Mid and hooks, Cell haulers feed beacon skeletons, Flare specialists close beacons and hunt the Summit, and a kitbot pushes Depot pieces and hangs hook rings. Spectators read the match at a glance: towers physically fill with stacked pieces, and every completed beacon LIGHTS — the winning alliance is literally the brighter half of the mountain. Endgame pulls robots out of the scoring lanes to their own corner walls, so the last 30 s is three simultaneous climbs per side with no traffic collapse.

## Autonomous
15 seconds, and it is a decision tree, not a script. At T=0, the FMS randomizes FIRST LIGHT — one of the three Ridgeline towers (Low, Mid, or High) per alliance — broadcast simultaneously as a game-data character ('L'/'M'/'H', 2018-style, readable in robot code) and a white strobe on the physical tower. Every piece scored on the First Light tower in auto earns +2, and if the alliance LIGHTS that beacon in auto (Ring + Cell + Flare seated), it scores a +12 First Light bonus and banks a locked lit beacon toward the Network RP. Because one robot carries one preload, lighting First Light in 15 seconds requires genuine alliance coordination: teams pre-negotiate who preloads the Ring, the Cell, and the Flare, claim non-overlapping start slots, and their code must branch to the correct tower at runtime — three different tower positions times three preload roles means a serious auto suite is nine paths, all AprilTag-aligned. The greedy alternative is racing the 10 neutral centerline pieces (first-come contested space, 2022-cargo-style), so alliances choose between the coordinated First Light play, a volume auto, or a hybrid. Mobility is 3 pts each, all auto piece values are teleop+2, and everything scored in auto persists — a converted First Light is a locked beacon, an early Summit piece, and a ~25-30 pt head start that shapes which RPs are live for the rest of the match.

## Endgame
The ASCENT WALL: a 12-ft-wide climbing face per alliance in the corner against its own alliance wall, with three physically separated lanes and three tiers of pipe rungs — FOOTHOLD at 42 in, CRUX at 63 in, and the SUMMIT BAR at 84 in, each successive rung staggered 12 in horizontally so climbing higher means traversing, 2022-style. Points (final 30 s): Shelter park (bumpers inside the wall zone) = 2; hanging from Foothold = 6; Crux = 12; Summit Bar = 20; plus a +5 ROPE TEAM bonus if two or more robots on one wall finish at Crux or higher. Why it is tough: the Summit Bar demands a two-stage traversal climber — hooks that walk rung to rung under swing loads, the hardest mechanism class in recent FRC. Why it is rewarding and never a cliff: every tier banks real points the moment it is achieved, a failed reach for the Summit Bar still keeps Crux credit, and a pure drivetrain robot earns its park. Max alliance output is 65 (3x20 + 5), enough to swing any close match but only ~30% of a strong alliance's total — it cannot erase two minutes of beacon work. Three lanes per wall and corner placement mean zero endgame traffic jams, and six robots swinging upward on staggered silver rungs as beacons blaze behind them is the closing shot of every match video.

## Ranking Points
Win = 3 RP, Tie = 1 RP, plus three bonus RPs that all reinforce the core beacon game. (1) SIGNAL RP (entry volume): score N total game pieces (posts, hooks, or depot) — N = 16 Regional / 20 DCMP / 24 Champs; calibrated so a median three-robot alliance cycling ~6 pieces each hits it roughly half the time at Regionals. (2) NETWORK RP (stretch, ~3-3.5x entry difficulty): LIGHT N beacons — 4 Regional / 5 DCMP / 6 including the Summit beacon at Champs; requires ~12+ correctly distributed pieces plus 4-6 precision Flare caps across all heights, realistic only for coordinated elite alliances, and a converted First Light auto counts, tying auto directly into ranking. (3) ASCENT RP (endgame): total Ascent Wall points >= 24 Regional / 30 DCMP / 36 Champs — Regional threshold is exactly two robots reaching the mid-tier Crux (12+12), per the two-robots-mid-tier calibration. All three RPs are measured on the primary field elements — there are no side quests.

## Low Floor
A kitbot with a simple claw or roller intake contributes every second of the match: push and drop any of the three pieces into the Cache Depot (2 pts each, floor level, no lift required — every Depot piece also counts toward the Signal RP), grab Cache Rings and hang them on the 40-in Signal Hooks (4 pts, a single fixed-height arm), drop Rings on the 38-in Low Tower posts, play cross-field defense in the shared neutral lanes, and park in the Ascent Wall shelter for 2. Crucially the floor tasks LOOK easy and ARE easy (s-neff principle): a 6-in ring hole over a chamfered 1.9-in post is ring-toss forgiving, and the flashy tasks — the 82-in Summit cap and the 84-in traversal — LOOK exactly as hard as they are, so nobody is ambushed by hidden difficulty.

## High Ceiling
Elites chase the Network RP and the Summit economy: lighting all six beacons requires handling all three geometries at four heights, which no 115-lb robot does optimally — a do-everything intake costs the weight that a Summit-Bar traversal climber needs, so the meta forces specialization drafts (ring cycler + cell hauler + flare capper vs. two generalists + climber). The nine-path First Light auto, contested centerline pieces, Summit posts 48 in from opposing traffic, and the Cell-scarcity bottleneck (10 per alliance) keep strategy shifting: early season favors Low/Mid volume, late season favors Flare-capping speed and beacon-denial-proof sequencing (since caps lock stacks, ORDER of completion becomes the studied skill). Because value comes from distribution rather than one location, there is no single-optimal robot to converge on — the Reefscape lesson applied.

## Teaching Value
The game forces a Rapid-React-class mechanism spread: an over-bumper ground intake (flat Rings), a wide indexing/orientation intake (Cells arrive sideways, must score vertical), a cone-style gripper with pose control (Flares), an elevator-plus-wrist or arm decision spanning 8 in to 82 in, and a traversal climber with rung-to-rung hook sequencing. Software students get FMS game-data parsing with runtime auto branching (nine-path auto suite), AprilTag auto-align on every scoring face, full-field pose estimation for diagonal cycles through traffic, and superstructure state machines for three piece types. Strategy students get a live resource-economics problem (Cell scarcity, beacon completion ordering, RP arithmetic). It is deliberately a whole-team curriculum, not a one-mechanism game.

## Buildability
Every element decomposes to a hardware-store team version. Towers: a 2-ft-square half-sheet plywood base box, a 4x4 lumber core, 1.5-in schedule-40 PVC posts with chamfered coupler tips; Signal Hooks are PVC stubs on 30-degree fittings — one tower per half sheet plus scrap lumber. Summit: two sheets of plywood as a 48-in torsion box with two PVC posts (LEDs optional everywhere; a $15 string light and a lever switch under the Flare seat replicates the lighting). Cache Depot: one half sheet plus 1x3 lip. Ascent Wall: 4x4/2x4 A-frames with 1.25-in black-pipe rungs at 42/63/84 in — the proven 2022 team-version pattern. Pieces: Rings from pool noodles or EVA sheet, Cells from 10-in Sonotube plus foam wrap, Flares are literally trimmed 12-in traffic cones (~$8). All robot-critical dimensions land on round or half-inch values (1.9-in post OD, 6-in ring bore, 4.5-in cell bore, 3-in socket, 38/58/76/82-in tips, 42/63/84-in rungs), and every angle in the game is 30 degrees or vertical — no 35-degree-reef-branch traps. A full practice field fits in about 8 sheets of plywood, dimensional lumber, and PVC.

## Risks
- Three distinct piece geometries invite intake weight creep; if the 'handle everything' robot proves impossible under 115 lb, weaker teams may feel locked out of two-thirds of the game — mitigated by the Depot and Hooks accepting simple mechanisms, but the perception risk is real.
- Flare capping at 76-82 in is the precision ceiling; if the chamfered-tip funnel fit is not as forgiving as designed, capping becomes a frustration mechanic and the Network RP dies — the 1-in socket oversize and tip chamfer must be validated with real prototypes before the design freezes.
- Teams may develop short-range Ring lobbing onto Low posts and Hooks, eroding pick-and-place primacy; geometry self-limits this (deep stacks, 6-in bore, capping requires placement), but a launching meta at Low towers is possible and rules may need a launch-distance limit.
- The seat-sensor/LED beacon indicator is a field-reliability single point of failure; the fallback human-visible definition (Flare seated over Ring and Cell) keeps matches scoreable, but any sensor flakiness in week 1 would damage trust in the game's signature moment.
- Summit posts 48 in apart on a shared structure is the tightest robot-robot proximity in the game; back-to-back geometry should prevent G415-style intake entanglement, but aggressive Summit defense could still generate contact-rule controversy and needs a tightly worded protected-contact rule.
- Foam Cell durability under repeated 82-in drops and drivetrain run-overs is unproven; the piece needs Crescendo-note-lessons-learned abuse testing or teams will resent replacement costs.
