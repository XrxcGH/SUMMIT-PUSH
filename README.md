# SUMMIT PUSH

SUMMIT PUSH is an original offseason game for competitive-robotics design training. It follows the conventions of modern three-versus-three robotics competitions and is played virtually: teams design ROBOTS in CAD against a field that exists as a CAD model, and test vision code in simulation. This repository holds the complete game package: the design specification, the game manual, the field CAD package and drawing set, an Onshape field generator, the AprilTag vision guide and layout file, the brief for a CAD design challenge built on the game, a typesetting guide for the manual, and the research and concept work behind the game.

## Game summary

Two ALLIANCES of three ROBOTS carry three kinds of SUPPLIES (CACHE CRATES, O2 CELLS and ROPE COILS) to their own CRAG, a scoring tower on the FIELD centerline with scoring positions on all four faces. LED tier rings on the CRAG light as the ALLIANCE establishes CAMPS. At the start of AUTO a random FORECAST doubles the AUTO placement value of one SUPPLY type and sets which type the ROPED UP bonus needs two of, so an AUTO routine that goes for the bonus needs a branch for each FORECAST.

During MATCH setup each ALLIANCE makes a ROUTE DECLARATION, which raises one CAMP bonus for the whole MATCH. The increase is scaled to the difficulty of the tier, and the declaration is shown on the FIELD LEDs so that opponents can scout it. The final 30 seconds are ENDGAME, when the HEADWALL ZONES are protected and all six ROBOTS can climb at once, three to a HEADWALL. Each HEADWALL is a truss leaning back 15° with three side-by-side lanes of staggered rungs, and a climb to the SUMMIT RUNG is worth 30 points.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | 3v3. 2:30 MATCH: 0:15 AUTO, then 2:15 TELEOP, of which the final 0:30 is ENDGAME. |
| **SUPPLIES** | CACHE CRATE (12 in cube), O2 CELL (5 × 14 in cylinder), ROPE COIL (10 in ring); 21 of each. |
| **Placement** | Every position in a tier is worth the same, whatever the SUPPLY type. TELEOP: Low tier (24 / 30 / 30 in) 4 pts, Mid tier (42 / 54 / 54 in) 7 pts, High tier (72 / 78 in) 10 pts, BASE DEPOT 2 pts. AUTO: 7 / 10 / 13 / 4. |
| **CAMP bonuses** | CAMP I +6, CAMP II +10, HIGH CAMP +15; the declared ROUTE's CAMP pays +18 / +24 / +35 instead. Each CAMP needs one SCORED SUPPLY of every type its tier accepts. SUMMIT BEACON +10 when all three CAMPS are established. |
| **AUTO extras** | LEAVE 3 per ROBOT. The FORECAST doubles the PRIORITY SUPPLY's AUTO placement value. ROPED UP +10. |
| **ENDGAME** | HEADWALL climb: PARK 3, LEDGE RUNG 12, CAMP RUNG 20, SUMMIT RUNG 30. Three independent lanes per ALLIANCE. |
| **Fouls** | MINOR FOUL +3, MAJOR FOUL +8, to the opposing ALLIANCE. |
| **Bonus RPs** | SUPPLY LINE (SUPPLIES SCORED), EXPEDITION (CAMPS established), ASCENT (ENDGAME points). Thresholds rise with tournament tier (manual Section 4.7). |

## Using the package

SUMMIT PUSH is a virtual project. The field, the ROBOTS and the vision system exist only in CAD and simulation: the field is modeled from the drawing set, ROBOT designs are checked with interference and motion studies, and vision code runs against WPILib or PhotonVision simulation driven by `04-vision/apriltag-field-layout.json`. The weigh-ins, referees and field staff in the manual are part of the game's fiction. Only documents are printed.

### Read order

1. `01-design/DESIGN-SPEC.md`. It is short and it governs; the other documents elaborate it.
2. `02-manual/GAME-MANUAL.md`, the rules teams work from.
3. `05-cadathon/CADATHON-BRIEF.md`, for how the design challenge runs and how entries are judged.
4. `03-field/` and `04-vision/`, when field modeling and vision work begin.
5. `00-research/` and `00-concepts/`, for background on why the game has its current shape or for a design retrospective with students. `00-concepts/` holds v1.0 material and should not go to students before the results show (see below).

### Kickoff printing

- The game manual, `02-manual/SUMMIT-PUSH-Game-Manual.pdf` (Letter pages; print the Appendix A plates separately at ANSI C if you have a plotter): one copy per student, or at least one per subteam. Reading it cover to cover is the first team activity, as on a real kickoff day.
- A one-page reference sheet: the game summary above plus the Scoring Summary in manual Section 4.6 (Section 2.4 has a shorter version). Print it large for the wall.
- Optionally, the field top view and CRAG sheets from `03-field/renderings/` at 11 × 17 in for the strategy whiteboard. Use ANSI C (22 × 17 in) if the smallest note text must be readable.

### What to give students, and when

At kickoff, publish everything teams need, including the judging rubric in `05-cadathon/CADATHON-BRIEF.md`. The brief makes this binding on the organizers (§5.3): the rubric is final and public from Day 0, and no criteria are added, reweighted or reinterpreted after kickoff. A public rubric helps rookie CAD teams set priorities and makes judging transparent. The research behind the brief found that vague or hidden criteria are a common reason design challenges lose participants' trust.

Nothing design-relevant is released before Day 0. `00-research/` can be shared at any time: it gives nothing away and is useful reading on how games are designed. Hold back `00-concepts/` until the results show. `FIRST-ASCENT.md` is the concept that became SUMMIT PUSH and still carries v1.0 geometry and scoring, including a Summit Socket on the spire at rim 66 in that v2.0 moved to the SHELF FACE at 72. Handing it out early spoils the reveal and gives students a superseded ruleset. Release it after the results show, labeled as a v1.0 archive. Because this repository is public, students can find the archive on their own; organizers who want to keep the reveal should hand out the kickoff materials directly.

### Modeling the field

Work from `03-field/FIELD-CAD-PACKAGE.md` (the geometry specification and master dimension ledger), with the sheets in `03-field/renderings/` as the drawing set and `03-field/MATERIALS-AND-COLORS.md` for appearance. To build the field in Onshape without modeling it by hand, paste `03-field/featurescript/SummitPushField.fs` into a Feature Studio and add the SUMMIT PUSH Field feature to a Part Studio; `03-field/featurescript/README.md` gives the steps.

When modeling by hand:

- Use the package's coordinate frame, always-blue-origin NWU: origin at the right corner of the Blue alliance wall, +X toward Red. The specification and the AprilTag JSON both use this frame, so vision simulation runs against the model without changes.
- Hold the O2 socket ID of 6.50 ± 0.125 in, the one strictly toleranced dimension. Most other robot-critical dimensions are whole or half inches, and the angles are 15°, 30° and 45°, so the field models quickly.
- Model the Blue half once, then pattern it 180° about field center for Red. Do not model the Red CRAG or HEADWALL separately.
- Check the finished model: run interference and clearance checks against the toleranced dimensions, and drive WPILib or PhotonVision simulation with `04-vision/apriltag-field-layout.json` to confirm that the tag poses match the model.

### Issuing Team Updates

Handle rules questions and fixes during the event the way a real season does. Manual Section 1.5 gives Team Updates their authority, and the brief (§6) sets their schedule and format.

1. Number updates in sequence (Team Update 01, 02, ...) and date each one.
2. For each change, cite the rule number ("G404 is revised as follows: ..."), show deleted text struck through and added text in bold, and give a one-line rationale.
3. Publish every update in the same channel, on the schedule the brief sets. Each update amends the manual: the manual plus all updates is the current ruleset, and the most recent update governs where they conflict.
4. Keep a public, cumulative Q&A log for questions that need an answer but no rule change. Q&A answers interpret the manual; they do not change it.

Team Updates are for clarifications and fixes. The design specification stays fixed unless the organizer decides to change a design decision, and any such change is recorded in `REVISION-LOG.md`.

## Building and verifying

The manual build, the drawing generator and the verification suite need only bash and Python 3 (standard library). From the repository root:

```bash
bash verify/run-all.sh
```

This rebuilds `02-manual/GAME-MANUAL.md` and the six drawing sheets, then runs the five checks described in `verify/README.md`: the numbers, cross-references and superseded values, cross-document agreement, drawing layout, and drawing geometry. On a clean run every check reports zero failures: `RESULT: 0 failure(s)` from `verify.py`, `crossdoc.py` and `svg_geom.py`, `TOTAL layout issues: 0` from `svg_collide.py`, and, in the `consist.py` report, no dangling references, no missing glossary entries, every restated rule `ok` and every `STALE` line `CLEAN`. The script's last line says only whether every check ran to completion, so read each check's summary.

To rebuild without checking:

```bash
bash 02-manual/build.sh                           # compile the manual from 02-manual/sections/
python 03-field/renderings/generate_drawings.py   # regenerate the six drawing sheets
```

To rebuild the PDF edition (Python 3 with `markdown-it-py` and `pypdf`, Node 18 or later, and Chromium; see `06-style/pdf/README.md`):

```bash
bash 06-style/pdf/build.sh                        # -> 02-manual/SUMMIT-PUSH-Game-Manual.pdf
bash 06-style/pdf/render_figures.sh               # re-render 02-manual/figures after a geometry change
```

### Generated files

- `02-manual/GAME-MANUAL.md` is compiled. Edit `02-manual/sections/*.md` and rebuild.
- The SVG sheets are generated. Edit the constants in `generate_drawings.py` and regenerate; do not hand-edit the SVGs.
- `03-field/featurescript/SummitPushField.fs` is assembled from `03-field/featurescript/src/*.fs` by `build.py`. Edit the sources and rebuild.
- `04-vision/apriltag-field-layout.json` is generated from the tag table in `04-vision/VISION-GUIDE.md` §3 by `python 04-vision/make_layout.py`; `verify/run-all.sh` confirms they match, and `verify/verify.py` checks all 26 poses against the manual.
- `02-manual/SUMMIT-PUSH-Game-Manual.pdf` and `02-manual/figures/` are built by `06-style/pdf/`. Rebuild the PDF after any manual edit.

### Making a change

Take a geometry change through the documents in order of authority:

1. `01-design/DESIGN-SPEC.md`.
2. The master dimension ledger in `03-field/FIELD-CAD-PACKAGE.md` §10, and the element's section.
3. The manual section that states the value.
4. The constant in `03-field/renderings/generate_drawings.py`; then regenerate the drawings.
5. The constant in `03-field/featurescript/src/20_ledger.fs`; then rebuild and check the generator.
6. If a tag pose moved: `04-vision/VISION-GUIDE.md` and `04-vision/apriltag-field-layout.json`.
7. Rebuild the manual, run `bash verify/run-all.sh`, and record the change in `REVISION-LOG.md`.

Reports and change requests are easiest to act on when they cite a rule number (G412), a manual section (Section 4.5.4), a glossary term, a ledger row in `03-field/FIELD-CAD-PACKAGE.md` §10, or a constant in `generate_drawings.py`.

### FeatureScript generator

`03-field/featurescript/` has its own build and off-line checks, which `verify/run-all.sh` does not run. The main commands, from the repository root:

```bash
python 03-field/featurescript/build.py             # assemble SummitPushField.fs from src/ and lint it
python 03-field/featurescript/verify/run.py        # build the field off-line and check it
python 03-field/featurescript/verify/run_checks.py # run the independent per-element checks
```

The off-line build needs the `cadquery-ocp` and `numpy` packages. Setup, options, and the full command list are in `03-field/featurescript/README.md` and `03-field/featurescript/AUTHORING.md`.

## Provenance and independence

SUMMIT PUSH began with a research phase that reviewed community opinion of competitive-robotics games over several decades, recording what players, mentors and spectators praise and criticize, and turned it into a game-quality rubric (`00-research/`). Five game concepts were developed against that rubric (`00-concepts/`) and scored by a panel of three judges. The winning concept became SUMMIT PUSH. It took on a set of fixes the judges asked for and two mechanics from the runner-up concept, SUMMIT SIGNAL: anti-pollution scoring and beacon ignition. The result was locked into `01-design/DESIGN-SPEC.md`, from which every document in the package was written. The package was revised to v2.0 after a full geometry, rules and balance audit, to v2.1 after an adversarial audit of v2.0, and to v2.2 after a third pass that audited the v2.1 fixes. `REVISION-LOG.md` records every change.

SUMMIT PUSH is an original work produced independently as a student training exercise. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or derived from the branding of any robotics competition organization.
