# HIGHBALL℠ presented by the Transcontinental Freight Cooperative

**Theme:** Golden-age transcontinental railroading: two rival freight lines race to load their trains, run the mail, and get their crews aboard before the departure whistle.

**Narrative:** The dispatch board flickers to life and two freight lines get the same order: the overland express leaves at midnight, full or not. Crews sprint the yard, hauling crates to the flatcars, rolling fuel barrels to the tankers, and running mailbags hand-to-hand down the line to make the mail hooks in time. Every lamp lit on a full car brings the whistle closer — and when it finally blows, there's only one thing left to do: catch the train. HIGHBALL: proceed at full speed.

## Game Pieces
### CARGO CRATE
- Shape/dims: Cube, 11 in x 11 in x 11 in, slightly crowned faces, ~2.0 lb
- Material: Molded EPP foam core with a ballistic-nylon/vinyl cover and cross-stitched seams (POWER UP cube construction — proven durable and cheap to replicate with a foam block + fabric cover)
- Handling challenge: Flat-faced and grippy — easy to hold, awkward to orient. Forces a wide compliant claw or wheeled top-down intake plus a wrist to seat it flat on decks and through the 18-in stock car door. The 'looks hard but is easy' entry piece.
- Count/staging: 20 total: 6 in each alliance DEPOT (preloads drawn from this allotment, 1 per robot, any piece type), 6 contested at the midfield JUNCTION, 1 staged dockside at each train
- Scoring role: Volume currency. Accepted by the Caboose (1), Flatcar (2), and Stock Car (5). Drives the Freight RP piece count and the cheap full-car bonuses.

### FUEL BARREL
- Shape/dims: Rigid cylinder, 8 in diameter x 12 in tall, with 1-in rolled rim lips at both ends, ~1.5 lb
- Material: Blow-molded HDPE drum (hardware-store 2-gal utility pail blank / off-the-shelf HDPE drum with capped ends); team version: 8-in SDR-35 PVC pipe section with glued end caps
- Handling challenge: Rolls when dropped, must be scored UPRIGHT in the tank hatch — forces orientation control: rollers that re-stand it, or a cradle intake plus indexing. The rims give claws a purchase line at exactly 12 in spacing.
- Count/staging: 16 total: 4 in each alliance DEPOT, 4 at the JUNCTION, 2 staged dockside at each train
- Scoring role: Mid/high-value currency. Accepted by the Caboose (1), Flatcar (2, standing in a stanchion pocket), Stock Car (5), and the 52-in Tank Car hatch (7) — the premium drop that separates elevator bots from claw bots.

### MAILBAG
- Shape/dims: Soft stuffed duffel, 16 in long x 7 in diameter, with one 5-in-inside-diameter webbing loop handle sewn to one end, ~1.2 lb
- Material: 1000D Cordura shell, foam-bead fill, bar-tacked 2-in seatbelt webbing handle (a sewn dog-toy-class piece: floppy, cheap, nearly indestructible; team version is a stuffed duffel from any fabric store)
- Handling challenge: Floppy and conforming — defeats rigid claws, rewards compliant wheeled intakes; the handle must end up presentable to hang on a 15-degree peg at up to 60 in, and it is the designed robot-to-robot handoff piece for the Mail Relay.
- Count/staging: 12 total: 3 in each alliance DEPOT, 4 at the JUNCTION, 1 staged dockside at each train
- Scoring role: The teamwork piece. Hangs only on Mail Car hooks (40 in = 4 pts, 60 in = 7 pts) and scores +3 MAIL RELAY when two or more alliance robots possessed it during the delivery — HIGHBALL's Aerial-Assist signature.

## Field Layout
Standard 27 ft x 54 ft carpeted field, ROTATIONALLY symmetric (180 degrees). Key structures:

1. TRAINS (one per alliance, scored-on by that alliance only): a 16-ft-long, 36-in-wide train of five cars (Caboose, Flatcar, Stock Car, Tank Car, Mail Car, each ~38 in long) parked parallel to a guardrail side wall, 36 in off that wall, spanning from 6 ft to 22 ft from the FAR end wall. Critically, each alliance's train sits in the OPPOSITE half of the field from its driver stations — Red's train is in the Blue half along one side wall; Blue's train is mirrored in the Red half along the other side wall. All scoring faces point into the open field. A 12-in-wide BOARDING PLATFORM strip (paint/tape) runs along the field side of each train and hosts the endgame SIGNAL GANTRY overhead.

2. DEPOTS (one per alliance): a human-player loading station in each alliance's own end wall, offset toward the corner diagonally opposite that alliance's train. A 30-in-high delivery shelf plus a floor chute; the taped 8 ft x 8 ft DEPOT ZONE in front is protected space. Depot-to-train is a ~45-ft diagonal that crosses midfield — both alliances' shipping lanes cross at center by construction.

3. THE JUNCTION (center field): two GRADE CROSSINGS on the centerline — plywood humps with 15-degree ramps rising to a 6-in-high, 24-in-wide flat crown, each crossing 6 ft wide — placed to leave three open flat lanes (8 ft each side lane, 8 ft center lane). 14 neutral contested pieces (6 crates, 4 barrels, 4 mailbags) start staged on and beside the crossings. This is the game's contested ground and natural defense chokepoint, with routes around it so nothing forces collisions.

4. DOCKSIDE STAGING: 4 pieces (1 crate, 2 barrels, 1 mailbag) staged on the carpet 5 ft field-side of each train for fast auto deliveries.

5. STARTING LINES: each alliance has two — a HOME LINE 10 ft out from its own end wall, and a TRACKSIDE LINE alongside its own train in the far half. Robots choose either at setup (see autonomous).

AprilTags (36h11): two per car face (10 per train), one at each depot shelf, one on each gantry upright, one per grade-crossing crown post — full-perimeter pose coverage.

## Scoring Locations
All heights measured from carpet; all scoring faces point into the open field (approach from the field side; the wall side is dead space, keeping traffic one-sided and legible).

| Location | Height / Geometry | Approach | Pieces accepted | Capacity | Teleop | Auto (2x) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CABOOSE bay | Carpet level, entered up a 15-degree, 24-in-wide ramp with no lip | Drive-up push, any angle | Crate, Barrel, Mailbag | 10 | 1 | 2 |
| FLATCAR deck | 8-in-high deck, 12 in deep, 1-in retaining lip; 4 stanchion pockets for upright barrels | Side placement, wide angle | Crate, Barrel (upright) | 6 | 2 | 4 |

Bonuses and notes:
- FULL CAR: the instant any car reaches capacity, +10 alliance points and a 12-in amber marker lamp on that car's roof lights — scoring progress readable from the last row of the stands.
- MAIL RELAY: +3 per scored mailbag possessed by 2+ alliance robots between leaving a depot/staging/floor and scoring (refs track only the 12 mailbags, 2014-assist-style workload).
- PRIORITY DELIVERY (auto only): +5 per delivery made in Dispatch Manifest order; +10 ON-TIME DEPARTURE for completing all three in auto (see autonomous).
- Scoring definitions: a piece is SCORED when at rest, fully supported by the car (or suspended by a hook, touching neither floor nor any robot of the scoring alliance). Pieces in motion at the buzzer count if they settle scored within 3 seconds. Removing pieces from any car (either alliance's) is a tech foul and the piece counts as scored. Pieces leaving the field are returned to the nearest depot by field staff. Adding pieces to the opponent's train only helps them, so there is no junk-feeding exploit.
- LEAVE (auto): 2 points per robot fully leaving its starting line.
- Tie-breaks: endgame points, then auto points, then fewest foul points.

## Match Flow
2:30 total — 0:15 AUTO, 2:15 TELEOP with the final 0:45 as the boarding window. The core loop is a full-field freight cycle: load at your depot (or grab contested pieces at the Junction), run the ~45-ft diagonal through midfield, and place on your train at the far end. Because both alliances' lanes are mirror diagonals, all six robots stream through the Junction in opposite directions all match — constant crossing traffic, Aerial-Assist style, without a single forced collision point (three lanes plus two 15-degree hump routes). Defense concentrates naturally at the Junction and at the contested neutral pieces there; depots, boarding platforms, and gantries are protected zones so intake-vs-intake scrums never happen at the structures. The Mail Relay creates real passing plays: one robot ferries mailbags across midfield and hands them (by the handle) to a tall partner camped trackside, converting 7-point high hooks into 10-point relayed deliveries. Spectators read the match instantly: cars visibly fill (clear stock car and tank walls, silhouetted mailbags on hooks), and every FULL CAR lights an amber roof lamp — count the lamps, know the score. Endgames send each alliance to opposite ends of the field, so the final 45 seconds are two separate, simultaneous climbing dramas with zero cross-alliance traffic.

## Autonomous
15 seconds, built on three interlocking decisions:

1. STARTING LINE CHOICE (alliance coordination): each robot sets up on either its HOME LINE (near its depot, 1 preloaded piece, long full-field auto) or its TRACKSIDE LINE (beside its own train in the far half, where 4 dockside pieces are staged). A trackside robot can realistically score 2 pieces in auto; a home robot runs one long delivery and stages field position. Alliances must negotiate the split in queue — three trackside robots starve (only 4 dockside pieces), three home robots waste the staging.

2. DISPATCH MANIFEST (game data): at T=0 the FMS reveals a randomized ordering of {STOCK, TANK, MAIL} on the arena screens (2018-style). The first piece your alliance scores in each of those three car types must occur in manifest order to earn +5 PRIORITY DELIVERY each; completing all three within auto scores +10 ON-TIME DEPARTURE and lights the locomotive headlamp. This forces branching auto routines (6 orderings), pre-match role assignment ("you take tank if it's first, I take mail"), and real-time re-sequencing if a partner's auto dies — exactly the software depth 2018 was praised for.

3. VISION-DEPENDENT PLACEMENT: every auto scoring action is a pick-and-place at a defined pose — 52-in barrel drop, 60-in hook hang — practical only with AprilTag pose estimation and on-the-fly path planning to the tag on each car face.

Auto pieces score double, LEAVE is 2, and contested Junction pieces are legal to collect (auto contact is a tech foul, and the trackside/home split means most alliances never meet in auto). Auto matters downstream: pieces banked in auto count toward FULL CAR lamps, the Freight RP, and manifest completion feeds the stretch RP at higher event tiers — a great auto sets up the whole match rather than being a separate minigame.

## Endgame
CATCH THE TRAIN. A SIGNAL GANTRY — a 16-ft pipe truss on A-frame uprights — spans above each alliance's own train, with three independent boarding stations spaced 48 in apart (one per robot, no traffic jams, and each alliance's endgame is at its own far end of the field, so the two alliances never share endgame space). Boarding is legal only in the final 45 seconds; points are assessed 3 seconds after the buzzer. Three tiers of partial credit per robot:

- FOOTBOARD (3 pts): park fully supported on the 12-in-deep, 6-in-high step plate running along the boarding platform. Any drivetrain can earn this — the floor of the endgame.
- GRAB IRON (8 pts): hang from a rigid 1.25-in-OD bar at 48 in, fully off the carpet. A Rapid React mid-rung-class climb — the mid-tier action.
- BRAKEMAN'S BAR (15 pts): hang from a 1.25-in bar at 72 in that is HINGED from the truss and free-swings +/-20 degrees, reached either by direct extension or by transitioning up from the Grab Iron. The swing is the mastery gate: passive hooks that work on the rigid bar oscillate wildly here; elite solutions damp the swing (dual hooks, timed transfer, active wrist) exactly like 2022's traversal — brutally hard-looking, genuinely hard, and worth it.

Why it works: 3/8/15 per robot means a failed high attempt still banks 8 or even 3 — no Steamworks cliff, no wasted two minutes. Max 45 alliance points is enough to swing any close match (a lone Brakeman's Bar is a 15-point comeback on a dime) but is under a third of a good alliance's freight total, so cycling still decides matches. Contacting an opponent touching its gantry in the boarding window is a tech foul that awards the victim full Brakeman's Bar credit — climbs are protected, drama is guaranteed, and the three swinging silhouettes against the lit marker lamps are the closing shot of every match.

## Ranking Points
Win = 3 RP, Tie = 1 RP, plus up to 3 bonus RPs that all reinforce the core freight/boarding game (no side quests):

| Bonus RP | Definition | Regional | DCMP | Champs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FREIGHT RP (entry volume) | Total pieces scored on your train (any car) >= threshold | 16 pieces | 20 | 24 |
| FULL CONSIST RP (stretch) | Number of cars at full capacity (lamps lit) >= threshold, AND at least 2 auto Priority Deliveries | 2 cars | 3 cars | 3 cars + ON-TIME DEPARTURE |
| ALL ABOARD RP (endgame) | Alliance endgame points >= threshold | 16 (two Grab Irons) | 19 (two Grab Irons + a Footboard) | 23 (Brakeman's + Grab Iron) |

Calibration per the rubric: FREIGHT at 16 pieces is ~50% achievable for a median regional alliance (three robots averaging ~5-6 cycles); FULL CONSIST demands coordinated car-targeting and auto execution (~3x harder, elite-alliance territory — note it consumes the SAME scoring actions, just aimed, so chasing it never fights winning the match); ALL ABOARD at 16 is exactly 'two robots do the mid-tier action.' Escalation columns keep the meta moving through the season instead of being solved by week 3.

## Low Floor
A kitbot with a drivetrain and a simple pool-noodle-roller intake plays all match: push/carry any piece up the zero-lip 15-degree ramp into the CABOOSE (1 pt each, capacity 10), place crates on the 8-in FLATCAR deck with the crudest hinged arm (2 pts), ferry mailbags across midfield and hand them to a tall partner for +3 MAIL RELAYS (contributing to 10-point deliveries without ever lifting above bumper height), bank 2-pt LEAVE and a dockside caboose push in auto, and finish with a guaranteed 3-pt FOOTBOARD park. That is 15-25 real points plus relay assists plus defense at the Junction — and every one of those tasks looks exactly as easy as it is (s-neff principle: the ramp, the 8-in deck, and the step plate are visibly gentle; nothing low-floor has hidden difficulty).

## High Ceiling
Elites chase a stack of compounding, never-finished optimizations: sub-13-second full-field cycles threading Junction traffic; a triple-geometry intake (rigid cube, rolling cylinder, floppy sack) under one weight budget — nobody can do all three pieces at all heights at 115 lb, so archetypes fork (tank-hatch elevator specialists, mail-hook arm bots, caboose-volume floor sweepers, relay ferries) and alliance selection becomes real strategy; six branching manifest autos with mid-auto re-sequencing when a partner fails; choreographed Mail Relay chains that turn the 7-pt high hook into a 10-pt passing play; endgame mastery of the free-swinging Brakeman's Bar with a damped transfer from the Grab Iron; and the late-season meta question that stays open — is the third robot better as a Junction defender denying contested pieces, a caboose volume bot feeding the Freight RP, or a dedicated relay ferry? Piece scarcity (48 pieces, 37 needed for a full consist) means late-match contested-piece control at the Junction becomes its own endgame-adjacent skirmish. No single robot design solves HIGHBALL.

## Teaching Value
Forces the full Rapid-React/Power-Up-class mechanism spread: (1) ground intakes for three geometries — compliant wheels for the mailbag, orientation-correcting rollers for the barrel, wide claw or top-down for the crate; (2) indexing/transfer from intake to placement pose, including re-standing a barrel upright; (3) the elevator-vs-arm decision (52-in tank drop and 60-in hook favor elevators; 24-in stock door and 8-in deck favor arms — students must argue the tradeoff with numbers); (4) a wrist/end-effector that presents a floppy handle to a 15-degree peg; (5) climber engineering in three difficulty steps ending at a swinging bar — hook geometry, CG control, oscillation damping, sequencing. Software: AprilTag pose estimation and auto-align to car faces (hook hanging is effectively impossible without it), full-field path planning through Junction traffic, six-way branching autos from FMS game data, and alliance-coordination logic (who starts trackside, who takes which manifest slot). Drive practice teaches passing plays and traffic reading — sports-team skills, not just cycling.

## Buildability
Every element decomposes to a team version in <=2 sheets of plywood plus hardware-store stock, with all robot-critical dimensions on whole or half inches and all ramps at 15 degrees: TRAIN CARS are 3/4-in plywood boxes on 2x4 skids (Caboose: half-sheet box + 15-degree ramp; Flatcar: 8-in-high platform with 1-in lattice lip and four 8.5-in-ID pockets from 8-in pipe couplings; Stock Car: plywood frame with an 18x16 door at a 24-in sill, walls of clear polycarb or even chicken wire for the team version; Tank Car: a 52-in plywood tower topped with a 12-in-ID funnel cut from a bucket; Mail Car: a plywood face with 3/4-in Sch40 pipe pegs at 40 in and 60 in, angled 15 degrees in pipe flanges). SIGNAL GANTRY: two 2x6 A-frames + 1.25-in rigid conduit crossbar; Grab Iron is fixed conduit at 48 in, Brakeman's Bar is conduit on two gate hinges at 72 in. GRADE CROSSING: one sheet of 3/4 plywood ripped into two 15-degree ramp faces over 2x6 crowns. Marker lamps: $10 battery puck lights toggled by a limit switch or by a volunteer. Game pieces: crate = foam block + sewn cover; barrel = 8-in PVC with caps; mailbag = fabric-store duffel with seatbelt webbing — a full practice field for under ~$600.

## Risks
- Far-side scoring strains driver sightlines (your train is 45+ ft from your glass): mitigated by AprilTag auto-align, big marker lamps, and high-contrast car faces, but weak-vision teams will feel it — the caboose and flatcar exist partly as close-tolerance-free fallbacks.
- MAIL RELAY tracking adds referee workload (mailbag possession across robots): capped at 12 bags in play and modeled on 2014 assist refereeing, but wording on 'possession' must be airtight or it becomes a protest magnet.
- Mailbag handle durability is the single most-stressed feature on any piece (hung, yanked, handed off): needs Crescendo-note-level abuse testing; a torn handle mid-match needs a defined replacement rule.
- Free-swinging Brakeman's Bar at 48-in station spacing could let an oscillating robot contact a neighbor's climb: spacing may need to grow to 54-60 in after field testing, at the cost of gantry width.
- Auto traffic risk: trackside starters from both alliances plus full-field home runners share the field in auto; the tech-foul-on-contact rule and lane geometry should prevent 2016-style auto collisions, but six aggressive autos crossing midfield needs simulation before locking the starting-line positions.
- Stock car door (18x16 at 24-in sill) is a potential jam point if two partners feed it simultaneously; the interior needs a settling floor slope (5 degrees) so pieces clear the doorway, or capacity effectively drops below 8.

## Spectator Hook
The final 30 seconds: five amber car lamps burning down the length of a visibly loaded train, a last mailbag relayed hand-to-hand through midfield traffic to the high hook, and then three robots leaping for the free-swinging Brakeman's Bars at once — silhouettes swinging above their own train as the departure whistle sounds and the crowd counts the hangs.
