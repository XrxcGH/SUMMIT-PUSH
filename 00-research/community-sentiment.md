# Community Sentiment on FRC Games

## Key takeaways
- Consensus best games: Rapid React 2022 (top of the 725-voter pick-5 Chief Delphi poll), Aerial Assist 2014, Ultimate Ascent 2013, Stronghold 2016, Power Up 2018 and, with recency bias, Crescendo 2024, which won the 2025 Chief Delphi (CD) poll 208/563.
- Consensus worst game: Recycle Rush 2015, which still drew 145/401 'least favorite' votes a decade later. The charges are no defense or interaction, repetitive stacking, canburgling luck, OPR-predictable outcomes and dead crowds. The runners-up are Charged Up 2023 (meta solved before Houston), Lunacy 2009 (slick-floor luck, static shorts, traffic jams) and the 4v0 no-defense format of 2001.
- Lack of defense and alliance interaction is the strongest predictor of a disliked game. Every well-liked game has meaningful robot-to-robot contact, but defense that is too strong (2013 wall-bots, full-court-shooter lockouts) is also criticized.
- Watchability drives approval. Central, visible scoring (Rapid React's hub) and visible progress (Reefscape's reef) are praised. Hidden or opaque scoring is a recurring complaint (the 2025 processor, 'dead after quals'; chess-match games where you can't tell who's leading).
- 'Low floor, high ceiling' is the community's core balance ideal. Stronghold and Rapid React are the archetypes (Brian_Maher: 'easy to win events with a drivetrain and intake, still challenging at high levels'), and Karthik's Rapid React post is the standard list of what a good game does.
- RP structures are a perennial complaint: 'weird side quests that distract from the main mechanic and confuse spectators' (nuclearnerd). The 2018/2019 games produced rank-optimizing 'RP teams'. In 2025 the RPs were too easy, which made schedule luck dominant and the auto RP dependent on partners.
- Endgame tuning rarely satisfies. Make-or-break climbs (Steamworks) are disliked, and so are undervalued climbs (Crescendo). Endgame traffic jams recur (Lunacy, 2019 HAB, 2024 stage 'NASCAR pileups', 2025 barge visibility).
- A meta that is solved early damages a game's reputation (2015 capper-stackers, the 2023 solve before Houston). Top teams copying the meta robot mid-season is a growing complaint about homogenized designs. Games whose strategy evolves all season (Reefscape's L1/algae shift) get credit even from critics.
- Logistics affect sentiment. Fragile or unavailable game pieces (2024 notes) and expensive field elements that teams cannot build themselves (2025 reef branches at 35 degrees instead of a PVC-friendly 45) generate lasting resentment. The durable dog-toy cargo of 2022 is the praised counterexample.
- All polls carry heavy recency and age bias, which the threads themselves acknowledge. The pick-5 poll (2022) resists that bias best and puts 2022/2014/2013/2016/2018 in the durable top tier.
- Analytical rankings agree with sentiment at the extremes. The Blue Alliance (TBA) Blog's weighted-criteria ranking (match quality 30%, robot diversity 25%, spectator friendliness 20%, strategic diversity 15%, originality 10%) rated FIRST Frenzy 2004 best and Recycle Rush 2015 worst.
- Divisive games show the tension between criteria. Steamworks ranks high in polls for its theme and novelty but is also called 'two poorly-conceived games smushed together'. Reefscape polled 2nd-best and 4th-worst in the same 2025 poll (a strong design challenge, but segregated and dull match play at the top level).

## Sources

This report summarizes community sentiment from Chief Delphi polls and discussion threads (read through the forum's Discourse API), the TBA blog's analytical game rankings, and community retrospectives, including YouTube tier lists ([Tier Ranking FRC Games 2000–2022, Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jT5AKLmPI9I), [Part 2](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=0yn4Tt9lztw)). Reddit r/FRC threads could not be retrieved (Reddit blocks unauthenticated scraping, and search indexing of the subreddit was poor), so the report relies mainly on Chief Delphi, the busiest venue for this debate, and on the TBA blog.

## 1. Poll results

["Favorite FRC Game (2025 Updated)"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/favorite-frc-game-2025-updated/504677) (563 voters for favorite, 401 for least favorite) is the most recent large poll:

- **Favorite:** Crescendo 2024 (208, a clear winner), Reefscape 2025 (57), Rapid React 2022 (52), Steamworks 2017 (45), Stronghold 2016 (36), Aerial Assist 2014 (33), Charged Up 2023 (31)
- **Least favorite:** Recycle Rush 2015 (145, far ahead of the rest), Charged Up 2023 (52), Lunacy 2009 (38), Reefscape 2025 (28), Crescendo 2024 (21)

["Top 5 Favorite FRC Games"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/top-5-favorite-frc-games/411255) (2022, 725 voters, pick-5 format, which dampens recency bias) ranked Rapid React (513), Aerial Assist (435), Ultimate Ascent (431), Stronghold (404) and Power Up (346) highest, followed by Deep Space (291) and Steamworks (268). The poll left out Recycle Rush and Lunacy on purpose, which drew ironic protest ("Lunacy not being on the poll seems like an attempt to silence and censor the masses >:(").

The [TBA Blog's analytical ranking](https://blog.thebluealliance.com/2016/01/12/what-is-the-best-frc-game-and-where-could-stronghold-fall/) (Francis O'Rourke, 2016) scored the 2003–2015 games on weighted criteria: match quality (30%), robot diversity (25%), spectator friendliness (20%), strategic diversity (15%) and originality (10%). It ranked FIRST Frenzy 2004 best and Recycle Rush 2015 worst ("boring and extremely repetitive," with originality its only strength). A [2017 follow-up](https://blog.thebluealliance.com/2017/01/05/why-stronghold-may-be-the-best-frc-game-of-all-time/) argued that Stronghold ranked second analytically but first in lived experience because it "raised the floor of match scores" and "students loved the game."

Every poll thread acknowledges recency and age bias: "You're going to see a lot of recency bias here, especially as a lot of active CD users are still students" (pigrammer, 2025 thread); "I doubt many people will vote for a game from before they were involved" (Timebomb, [2019 thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/favorite-frc-game-of-all-time/347775)). Crescendo's lead in the 2025 poll should be read with that caveat. The top cluster of the pick-5 poll (2022/2014/2013/2016/2018) is probably the more durable consensus.

## 2. Most-liked games

### 2022 Rapid React
In ["What did Rapid React get RIGHT?"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/what-did-rapid-react-get-right/410496), strategist Karthik Kanagasabapathy called it "one of the best FRC games of all time," and listed its strengths: all non-endgame scoring in the center of the field ("fabulous for the audience"), tasks that "looked hard to the audience but were very achievable for teams of all levels," wide variety in robot design, a "dynamic, exciting" traversal-climb endgame, and game pieces that were "easy to source, relatively consistent, and very visual." Others emphasized emergent variety (Alex_Y: "No match of Rapid React is the same. Due to the random nature of the ball returns, match strategy can quickly change in the middle of a match") and alliance interaction: robots crossed paths while chasing cargo, unlike the segregated cycling of 2019/2020 (ferrari77). Knufire: "This, IMO, is the best game since 2014, and it's not close." The one repeated complaint was that mid-air ball collisions "made it feel like a lotto at times" (Cory_Walters).

### 2014 Aerial Assist
Aerial Assist is liked for the teamwork that assists forced: "The way some alliances meshed perfectly and were able to set up pick after pick, run circles around the defense... really made the game beautiful to watch" (Abhishek_R, [2016 best-game poll](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/what-was-the-best-frc-game-poll/146375)). "It was the game most like a real sport that I have seen" (Jsteel, [Best FRC Game?](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/best-frc-game/425334)). SPang: "Can FIRST just rerun Aerial Assist in 2024? It's the kind of well designed game that you can make an entire robotics sporting league from." Posters cite its watchability for lay spectators again and again.

### 2013 Ultimate Ascent
"Visually incredible to watch the frisbees fly," and "strategy was king that year — the winning alliance had no full-court shooters" (Ryan_Swanson). The counterpoint is that full-court shooters spoiled the game for weaker teams: "teams that could make full court shots were effectively playing a different game... you just showed up, watched the other team constantly score, and left the field after you lost" (Timebomb). Wall-bots "took [defense] to an outrageous level" (Camren).

### 2016 Stronghold
"Brilliantly balanced; it was easy to build a robot that could win events with just a drivetrain and an intake, but it was still a challenging game to play at high levels... among the more easily explainable to non-FRC folks" (Brian_Maher, Top-5 thread). The TBA blog praised its theme, the defenses selected by alliances and the raised scoring floor, while conceding that the low bar pushed teams toward suboptimal designs.

### 2024 Crescendo
Crescendo won the 2025 poll by a wide margin. The reasons given: "Extremely well-balanced, fun to watch, surprising viability of different strategies like passing" (ChiefK33f, [Thoughts on Crescendo](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/thoughts-on-crescendo-a-few-weeks-in/460982)); the amp/speaker pairing rewarded complete robots; and comebacks were possible: "any alliance could score 22 points on a dime, making every match somewhat winnable... 1678 [alliance] vs alliance 8 was interesting — itself a feat of game design" (TrevDay, 2025 thread). The complaints: field faults, a climb/trap endgame "not nearly worth enough points" (seg9585, Pizzatc), fragile notes with "atrocious" availability, and mid-season copying of the meta robot, which homogenized designs.

### 2018 Power Up and the chess-match games
Power Up is liked for contested control: "It really felt like a competition. You were fighting for control over something... the main scoring happened in the middle of the field which was amazing for spectating" (pkrishna3082); "big field elements make for a very cool audience view as you can see the advantage shifting from one alliance to another" (kierajreed). A vocal group of long-time participants wants a return to the back-and-forth play of 2004/2005/2007: "I am DYING for the era of chess match games to make a comeback... [they] felt like the two alliances were actually in dialogue during a match... ever since 2007 it's always just a race for points" (James1902, 2025 thread). The acknowledged downside: "some don't like them because you couldn't immediately see what alliance was in the lead" (GaryVoshol).

## 3. Most-disliked games

### 2015 Recycle Rush
Recycle Rush still tops the "least favorite" vote a decade later (145/401 in 2025). The specific charges, from the [worst-game poll](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/poll-worst-frc-game/146451) and [A Recycle Rush Reflection](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/a-recycle-rush-reflection/143576):
- No defense and no interaction: the two alliances stacked totes side by side with no contact allowed.
- Repetitive play: "Once on an alliance you did the same thing over and over... Can Wars decided your fate. No amount of strategy could change the results of an unfavorable matchup" (ACardinali).
- Luck and a degenerate meta: the center-field "canburgling" race decided matches in the first second, and "save for very few exceptions internal capper stackers were the only correct answer" (Gregor).
- Predictable outcomes: ranking by average score made results track OPR almost perfectly, and there were no comeback mechanisms.
- No atmosphere: "There was no energy from the crowd... teams left very early" (Ryan_Swanson, on his 2015 regional).

The game has defenders. It was "a wonderful design challenge" with "by far the most varied designs" (John_Retkowski), and eliminations at events with deep fields were tense (EricAnderson191: "a six-stack with can and litter tottering back and forth generates a thrill"). troy_dietz summed up the community verdict in the Top-5 thread: "TBF the title is 'FRC Games', not 'FRC Engineering Challenges'."

### 2009 Lunacy
"Generally 2 types of people voted in this poll: People who voted for Recycle Rush, [and] People who witnessed Lunacy" (BBray_T1296). The specifics: the regolith floor and mandated slick wheels made driving luck-heavy; static buildup was "shorting out electronics"; trailer scoring meant a robot couldn't protect its own goal; traffic jams stalled matches; and "the bottom 50% couldn't participate." Long-time participants counter that it was a fun design challenge if your team did well: "the group of people I know who really liked Lunacy is wholly contained within the group whose teams did really well" (Basel_A).

### 2023 Charged Up
Charged Up placed second-worst in the 2025 poll. "Charged Up felt 'solved' before teams even got to Houston; supercharging was a band-aid solution" (MrPotato, [Review of Reefscape](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/review-of-reefscape/499842)); "a game best played by upgrading your robot cycles by micro amounts in the shop, sort of like 2015" (Ben_Martin). Its charge-station endgame has strong defenders: "It was so much fun watching the charge station go back and forth in a close match... one of the best viewing experiences ever" (Clodsire).

### 2001 Diabolical Dynamics
The 2001 game used a 4-vs-0 cooperative format with no defense. Team 71 solved it so completely that "the 710 matches were less than 60 seconds... there was NOTHING you could do to stop them" (Tyler_Olds, EricH). Veterans cite it as evidence that no-defense formats fail.

### Divisive games
- 2017 Steamworks ranks high in polls (novel human-player interaction, steampunk theme, gear-cycling teamwork) but draws sharp criticism: "two poorly-conceived games smushed together... fuel was almost irrelevant. What was left was a Crescendo-like chore of running gears... and if you missed a climb the whole effort was wasted" (nuclearnerd). Pegs bent, and low-level teams "won/lost matches at random based on whether their partners could climb" (Kevin_Leonard).
- 2025 Reefscape was 2nd-favorite and 4th-least-favorite in the same poll. It was praised for hard but fair design tradeoffs, visible progress on the reef, and strategy that evolved all season (VR9450: "L1 became the most critical to win at champs, which is rare"). It was criticized as "mostly a scoring exhibition" (Ben_Martin); "at champs it felt like watching Recycle Rush, with neither alliance crossing the field" (WorseSquash9); "easily 2025 was the most boring [match strategy]: 'we'll cycle on this side, you cycle on that side, deep climb at end'" (TrevDay). Defense was uneconomical, and the processor was dead after quals.
- 2019 Deep Space and 2020 Infinite Recharge have middling reputations: "very predictable matches — one team would get shut down by defense while the other cycled" (Alex_Y).

## 4. Recurring complaints across games

### Hidden or illegible scoring
The community consistently criticizes games in which spectators can't tell who is winning. The 2025 processor is the newest example: scoring algae feeds the opponent, the value is opaque, and the processor was "dead after qual matches" (BenjaminMiller). The older chess-match games had the same problem ("you couldn't immediately see what alliance was in the lead"). The praised remedies are Rapid React's central hub (Anthony_Galea: "you don't have to dart your eyes across the field to figure out who's winning") and Reefscape's visible reef fill (FieryFuryGame: "every piece of algae and coral is trackable... a lot more fun to spectate").

### RP structures that distort play
From [Balancing Ranking Points](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/balancing-ranking-points/356543): RPs "come across as weird side quests that distract from the main mechanic and confuse spectators... captains who seeded high are maybe not prepared to play a different game in playoffs" (nuclearnerd). The 2018/2019 games spawned "RP teams" that optimized rank over winning; the 2016 RP is held up as the best integrated. In [2025's RP thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/thoughts-on-ranking-point-system-this-year/499699) the complaint inverted. RPs were too easy, which made win/loss and schedule luck dominant (Sands: "some of the best teams ranked in the 30s with top-5 EPA"), and the auto RP punished teams randomly paired with partners who couldn't move in auto (Alex_Y). Defenders note that RPs let good teams overcome bad schedules (Hjelstrom) and are better than pure win-loss ranking or the contentious 2012/2015 coopertition tasks (John_Bottenberg).

### Lack of defense and interaction
Lack of interaction is the strongest predictor of a disliked game (2015, 2001, late-season 2025). Every well-liked game (2013, 2014, 2016, 2018, 2022, 2024) has meaningful alliance interaction. Defense that is too strong is also criticized (2013 wall-bots, 2019 rocket lockdowns).

### Endgame balance
Steamworks' climb was "make-or-break the entire match" (Drew_B), and "every FRC game somehow thinks we need an overpowered mini game at the end" (nuclearnerd). Crescendo's climb, by contrast, was too weak (SBowling4: "I just wish endgame was more valuable"). Endgame traffic jams recur: Lunacy's pileups, 2019 HAB congestion, the 2024 mid-field stage causing "NASCAR pileups" (Rethum865), and 2025's hard-to-see cage climbs (pmattin5459).

### Early-solved metas and homogenized designs
The examples are 2015 (capper-stackers) and 2023 (solved before Houston). A newer complaint is that "top teams can completely steal the 'meta' robot mid-way through the season," making FRC "more homogenous" (ChiefK33f). Team 148's jjsessa mocked the trend: "This year we built a decoy turret bot."

### Game piece supply and durability
On the 2024 notes: "fragile... availability is atrocious. We spent the last 4 weeks testing with worn/broken notes" (seg9585). Rapid React's dog-toy cargo was praised by contrast (petercooperjr: "amazed at how durable these game pieces are").

### Field cost and practice-field buildability
From the [Reef Branch Rant](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/reef-branch-rant/482269): if 2025's branches had been 45° instead of 35°, teams could have built them from big-box PVC fittings. Instead, the official elements were expensive and required non-refundable pre-orders. Practice-field cost is a perennial equity complaint ([For teams with full practice fields, how?](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/for-teams-with-full-practice-fields-how/519911)).

### Auto and luck dominance
Elite play in 2025 came down to "who had the better auto and who can get more algae" (Boxsteam). Other examples are canburgling in 2015 and aerial ball collisions in 2022. Schedule luck is amplified whenever RPs are too easy or depend on partners.

## 5. Synthesis: an implicit design rubric

Across a decade of threads, the same criteria decide a game's reputation:

1. Watchability: visible, centralized, legible scoring.
2. Defense and alliance interaction, tuned so that defense matters but cannot fully shut out offense.
3. Low floor, high ceiling: a drivetrain-and-intake robot can contribute while elite teams stay challenged (Stronghold and Rapid React are the archetypes).
4. Strategic variety that stays unsolved all season (Reefscape's late L1/algae meta was praised; Charged Up's early solve was criticized).
5. An endgame that is exciting and can swing matches without invalidating the previous 2 minutes.
6. Comeback and randomness mechanisms that create drama without deciding matches by luck.
7. Accessible game pieces and field elements.

Recycle Rush fails nearly all seven. Rapid React and Aerial Assist pass nearly all of them, which matches where the polls put them.
