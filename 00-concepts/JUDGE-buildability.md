## Verdict
Greenlight FIRST ASCENT, with SUMMIT SIGNAL as a close second worth one revision cycle. FIRST ASCENT wins on the criteria this judge weighs hardest: its scoring definitions are latch-and-settle simple (six volunteers can call it), own-structure-only scoring eliminates every pollution and denial exploit by construction, and its field decomposes to the cheapest, most CAD-able element set with round dimensions and standard angles throughout — realistic for student teams to both CAD a robot against and build practice elements for in 9 days. Its fixes are tuning (protection apron, socket tolerance, RP thresholds), not redesign. SUMMIT SIGNAL has the best buildability and spectator moment of the set but ships with a genuine rules hole — Flare-capping an opponent's bare post permanently kills a beacon for a ~15-point gift, which is elims-positive math — that must be closed with the one-sentence completion-gate fix before it is trustworthy. HIGHBALL is the best-drafted rulebook but its ten-car field build and 2014-assist-class relay refereeing make it the wrong scope for this event; MAINLINE's continuous accrual scoring and knock-off causation fouls fail the volunteer-referee test as written; RELIQUARY's six-robots-one-structure geometry with a judgment-distance protection rule is the highest contact-controversy design of the five.

## Details
### FIRST ASCENT
- Strengths: Cleanest rules package of the five: own-crag-only scoring with no descore kills junk-feeding and pollution exploits outright; 'scored = supported + no alliance robot contact, latched at settle' is referee-callable by 6 volunteers with no possession tracking, no ownership timers, no causation attribution. Camp bonuses latch, so mid-match state disputes vanish. Best-documented low floor in the set (published kitbot path with a graduation ladder). Field decomposes best: one crag = 2 sheets, headwall = 1 sheet + A-frames, all angles 15/30/45, all heights on round inches. Pieces are dog-toy-doctrine durable and hardware-store sourceable (PVC+pipe insulation, rubber rings, Power Up cubes). Forecast mechanic elegantly ties one game-data read to both auto doubling and the match-long Storm Tier.
- Concerns: Endgame is heavier than claimed: max 90 alliance climb vs typical 100-160 supply scores is ~40-45% of a strong match, not the stated minority share, and ASCENT RP at 40 regional means two real extending climbers, above the 'two robots mid-tier' calibration if Camp rung (54 in, swinging) proves hard. Deliberate absence of crag-face protection plus 78-in ring hangs under legal defense is the highest tip-over/contact-controversy exposure in the concept — a robot bumped mid-placement at full extension is a yellow-card argument waiting to happen. O2 socket tolerance (0.75 in/side) will make or break s-neff compliance and is unvalidated. Depot 'ANY piece, any orientation, 4-in lip' needs an out-of-tray/overflow definition (pieces heaped above the lip, pieces bridging the lip).
- Fixes: 1) Add a line-based placement protection: no opponent contact while a robot's bumpers intersect a taped 3-ft apron on any crag face — a line call refs can make, unlike judgment-distance rules, while leaving the corridor and Center Cache fully open for defense. 2) Drop ASCENT RP regional threshold to 32 (Camp + Ledge) until week-1 data. 3) Freeze the socket ID only after a kitbot-grade arm hits 80%+ insertion in testing; publish the socket as a strictly-toleranced dimension. 4) Define Depot scoring as 'fully below the plane of the lip' and give field staff a leveling rule. 5) Publish the dice/card Forecast fallback in the manual, not as an afterthought.

### SUMMIT SIGNAL
- Strengths: Best buildability of the set: trimmed traffic cones, pool-noodle rings, Sonotube spools, PVC posts with chamfered couplers — a full practice field in 8 sheets with zero exotic parts, and the easiest field for a student team to CAD in 9 days. Beacon ignition is the strongest single spectator moment across all five concepts, and the referee fallback definition ('Flare seated above at least one Ring and one Cell,' visible from the stands) is exactly how a sensor-backed score should be worded. 'Any piece on a post scores for that post's alliance' is smart anti-pollution wording. Ring-toss post geometry (6-in bore over 1.9-in chamfered post) is genuinely forgiving at the floor.
- Concerns: The Flare-cap denial exploit is a real rules hole: because posts take max 4 pieces with 'Flare always last' and removal is a tech foul, an opponent can seat a cheap Flare on your bare post — gifting you ~5-7 points while permanently killing that post's beacon. At ~15 points spent to deny the Network RP (4 beacons regional), that trade is strongly positive in elims and turns the signature mechanic into a denial game. Second: 'regardless of who placed it' + no launch restriction invites Low-post/hook lobbing, admitted in-risks but unresolved. Summit posts 48 in apart is the tightest robot-robot proximity of any concept and the protected-contact wording doesn't exist yet. Flare capping at 76-82 in with a 3-in socket over a 1.9-in post (~0.55-in radial clearance) is tighter than it reads and is the precision ceiling for the whole Network RP. Cell foam durability under 82-in drops is unproven.
- Fixes: 1) Close the cap exploit with one sentence: 'A Flare is only scored when the post below it holds at least one Ring and one Cell; otherwise it is returned by field staff at the next stoppage and does not lock the post.' Capping becomes strictly a completion act. 2) Add a launch rule: a piece must leave robot contact with bumpers within 3 ft of the target tower base (line-callable). 3) Enlarge the Flare socket to 4 in or add an internal guide cone; validate at 82 in with a prototype before freeze. 4) Write the Summit protected-contact rule now: no contact with an opponent whose bumpers intersect its own taped Summit approach box. 5) Drop-test Cells from 84 in x500 cycles before piece spec freeze.

### HIGHBALL
- Strengths: The most complete scoring-definition drafting of the five: settle timing (3 s), out-of-bounds return, descore-counts-as-scored, and the observation that junk-feeding the opponent's train is self-defeating are all handled in the text. The 3/8/15 swinging-bar endgame with victim-gets-full-credit protection is the best endgame design here — partial credit everywhere, a genuine mastery gate, and a bright-line foul. FULL CAR marker lamps and clear car walls give real stands-legibility. Kitbot path (zero-lip ramp, 8-in deck, footboard) is honest about difficulty.
- Concerns: Field build is the heaviest by a wide margin: ten distinct car structures (5 cars x 2 trains, each with different geometry), two gantries, two depots, two grade crossings — for a 9-day CADathon that is 2-3x the element CAD of any other concept, and for real events it is the most expensive and slowest field reset. Refereeing is the second problem: Mail Relay possession tracking is 2014-assist workload, which refs disliked even with 3 balls — 12 bags is worse; plus per-car capacity counts across 10 cars, manifest-order verification in a 15-second auto, and lamp triggering all land on volunteers. Gantry 48-in station spacing with a free-swinging 72-in bar is a robot-robot contact incident admitted but unresolved. Mailbag handle is a single-point durability failure with no replacement rule yet. Far-side scoring punishes weak-vision teams hard despite mitigations.
- Fixes: 1) Cut the train to 3 cars (merge Caboose ramp + Flatcar deck into one 'Freight Car'; keep Stock/Tank/Mail as one combined mid car and one tall car) — halves field CAD, reset, and ref counting with minimal strategic loss. 2) Replace judged Mail Relay possession with a taped TRANSFER ZONE: a mailbag counts as relayed only if handed off while both robots' bumpers are in the zone — converts a possession judgment into a line call. 3) Widen gantry stations to 60 in before any field testing, not after. 4) Add the 5-deg stock-car settling floor and the torn-handle replacement rule to the base spec now. 5) Let FMS score manifest order from scorekeeper entry, and make car lamps sensor-driven (piece-count microswitch), never volunteer-toggled.

### MAINLINE
- Strengths: Physical score-state display (headlamp, semaphore paddles, lever CLACK) is the best legibility engineering of the five — the field itself is the scoreboard, and the lever flip is a genuinely iconic endgame act. The ownership economy with capacity-locking is the deepest strategy layer in the set. Back-to-back face geometry on shared structures is the right way to make contested space without intake-vs-intake. Team-version buildability is strong (wedge-rip 15-deg shelves, pipe-flange hooks, ~$250 field).
- Concerns: This game fails the six-volunteer test as written. Continuous per-second ownership accrual requires every placement's Signal Weight to be entered in real time with second-accurate timestamps — a late scorer entry silently changes the score, and 'strictly greater weight' ties flip ownership on single-piece events; this is a protest machine. Worse, knock-off attribution ('owner-caused = returned, opponent-caused = G-foul + restoration') asks refs to assign causation for pieces falling off a contested centerline structure mid-scrum — unenforceable consistently. The 4.5-ft chokepoint lanes concentrate exactly the pin/contact calls the concept admits are risky. Additive-only counterplay plus face-locking can kill the territory dialogue mid-match (admitted, unresolved). Drum-on-tilted-shelf is a looks-easy-is-hard trap for mid-tier teams. Field electronics (headlamp, strobes, mast lamps) are the largest field-reliability surface of any concept.
- Fixes: 1) Replace continuous accrual with discrete OWNERSHIP CHECKS: every 15 s (and at T=0 of teleop and endgame), the alliance with greater weight on each structure banks a fixed bonus (e.g., Tower 12, Depot 6). Scorekeepers verify state at known instants instead of streaming timestamps; the paddles still tell the story. 2) Make all knock-offs no-fault: any piece that leaves a structure is restored by field staff, full stop — delete the causation foul and cover deliberate descoring with the existing tech-foul rule for robot contact with scored pieces. 3) Widen chokepoint lanes to 6 ft. 4) Add a T-60 'second shelf row' unlock on locked depot faces to revive dead territory. 5) Declare the mechanical semaphore paddle the sole authority and the lamps decorative, in the manual.

### RELIQUARY
- Strengths: The one-face team-version decomposition (1.5 sheets + $60 for a full practice face) is the single best practice-field insight in any of the five concepts, and piece sourcing is excellent (hole-saw EVA medallions, planter-pot urns, XPS tablets under $4). Coin columns as center-field thermometers are strong legibility. Curator's Bonus is a clean auto-coordination forcer. Tipped-urn partial credit is thoughtful frustration-proofing.
- Concerns: Field geometry concentrates all six robots on one central structure from six directions — the worst traffic architecture of the set — and the mitigation is a 12-in Curation Zone proximity rule, which is a continuous distance judgment refs must make simultaneously on up to six faces; this is the G415 problem restated, not solved. Floor pedestal urns (1-in lip, 18 in off a contested face, upright = within 15 deg of vertical) will be tipped constantly by incidental contact, forcing refs to adjudicate 'who tipped it' — the same causation trap as MAINLINE. The vault gravity column is a jam-prone field element with unlimited capacity (jam = scoring stoppage on the signature low-floor task). Center congestion also degrades watchability: the structure occludes half its own faces from any seat. Urn under-use risk in quals is admitted. Endgame is a competent but generic rung ladder — least distinctive of the five.
- Fixes: 1) Replace the 12-in judgment zone with a painted hex APRON 30 in out from every face: opponent contact with any robot whose bumpers break the apron plane is the foul — a line call. 2) Make urn tipping no-fault: any urn leaving upright state on a pedestal is reset upright by field staff at the next stoppage, score latching only at settle windows; deepen the pedestal lip to 2 in. 3) Give the vault a ref-accessible clearing door and cap vault capacity at 12 with a full-column overflow tray so a jam never stops scoring. 4) Move the two floor pedestals from face A to standalone pedestals 4 ft off the rotunda, out of the face scrum. 5) Regional Masterpiece RP: 1 face + 8 vault coins, escalating later, per its own risk note.
