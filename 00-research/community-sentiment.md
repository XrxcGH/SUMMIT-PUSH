# community-sentiment

## KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Consensus best games: Rapid React 2022 (top of 725-voter pick-5 CD poll), Aerial Assist 2014, Ultimate Ascent 2013, Stronghold 2016, Power Up 2018, and (with recency bias) Crescendo 2024, which won the 2025 CD poll 208/563.
- Consensus worst game: Recycle Rush 2015 — 145/401 'least favorite' votes a decade later — for zero defense/interaction, repetitive stacking, canburgling luck, OPR-predictable outcomes, and dead crowds; runners-up are Charged Up 2023 (meta solved before Houston), Lunacy 2009 (slick-floor luck, static shorts, traffic jams), and 2001's 4v0 no-defense format.
- The single strongest predictor of a hated game is lack of defense/alliance interaction; every beloved game features meaningful robot-vs-robot contact, but over-strong defense (2013 wall-bots, full-court-shooter lockouts) is also punished.
- Watchability drives love: central visible scoring (Rapid React's hub) and visible progress (Reefscape's reef) are praised, while hidden/opaque scoring (2025 processor 'dead after quals', chess-match games where you can't tell who's leading) is a recurring complaint.
- 'Low floor, high ceiling' is the community's core balance ideal — Stronghold and Rapid React are the archetypes ('easy to win events with a drivetrain and intake, still challenging at high levels' — Brian_Maher; Karthik's Rapid React post is the canonical praise list).
- RP structures are a perennial gripe: 'weird side quests that distract from the main mechanic and confuse spectators' (nuclearnerd); 2018/2019 bred rank-optimizing 'RP teams', while 2025's too-easy RPs made schedule luck dominant and the auto RP partner-dependent.
- Endgame tuning never satisfies: make-or-break climbs (Steamworks) are hated, but so are undervalued climbs (Crescendo); endgame traffic jams recur (Lunacy, 2019 HAB, 2024 stage 'NASCAR pileups', 2025 barge visibility).
- Meta-solved-too-early kills a game's reputation (2015 capper-stackers, 2023 pre-Houston solve), and mid-season copying of the meta robot by top teams is a growing homogenization complaint; games whose strategy evolves all season (Reefscape's L1/algae shift) get credit even from critics.
- Logistics matter to sentiment: fragile/unavailable game pieces (2024 notes) and expensive, non-buildable field elements (2025 reef branches at 35 degrees instead of PVC-friendly 45) generate lasting resentment; durable dog-toy cargo in 2022 is the praised counterexample.
- All polls carry heavy recency/age bias (acknowledged in-thread); the pick-5 format poll (2022) is the most bias-resistant and puts 2022/2014/2013/2016/2018 as the durable top tier.
- Analytical rankings agree with sentiment on the extremes: TBA Blog's weighted-criteria ranking (match quality 30%, robot diversity 25%, spectator friendliness 20%, strategic diversity 15%, originality 10%) rated FIRST Frenzy 2004 best and Recycle Rush 2015 worst.
- Divisive games show the criteria tension: Steamworks ranks high in polls for theme/novelty but is called 'two poorly-conceived games smushed together'; Reefscape polled 2nd-best AND 4th-worst in the same 2025 poll (great design challenge, boring segregated match play at the top level).

## REPORT
# FRC Community Opinion: Best and Worst Games, and Why

This report synthesizes community sentiment from Chief Delphi polls and discussion threads (fetched directly via the forum's Discourse API), The Blue Alliance blog's analytical game rankings, and community retrospectives. Reddit r/FRC threads were not directly retrievable (Reddit blocks unauthenticated scraping and search indexing of the subreddit was poor), so this report leans on Chief Delphi — which is by far the richest and most heavily-trafficked venue for this debate — plus TBA blog and YouTube tier-list retrospectives ([Tier Ranking FRC Games 2000–2022, Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jT5AKLmPI9I), [Part 2](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=0yn4Tt9lztw)).

## 1. The Hard Numbers: What the Polls Say

**["Favorite FRC Game (2025 Updated)"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/favorite-frc-game-2025-updated/504677)** (563 voters, favorite; 401 voters, least favorite) is the most recent large poll:

- **Favorite:** Crescendo 2024 (208 — a runaway winner), Reefscape 2025 (57), Rapid React 2022 (52), Steamworks 2017 (45), Stronghold 2016 (36), Aerial Assist 2014 (33), Charged Up 2023 (31)
- **Least favorite:** **Recycle Rush 2015 (145 — dominant)**, Charged Up 2023 (52), Lunacy 2009 (38), Reefscape 2025 (28), Crescendo 2024 (21)

**["Top 5 Favorite FRC Games"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/top-5-favorite-frc-games/411255)** (2022, 725 voters, pick-5 format, which dampens recency bias): **Rapid React (513), Aerial Assist (435), Ultimate Ascent (431), Stronghold (404), Power Up (346)**, then Deep Space (291) and Steamworks (268). Recycle Rush and Lunacy were deliberately omitted from the poll — to loud protest from ironic fans ("Lunacy not being on the poll seems like an attempt to silence and censor the masses >:(").

The **[TBA Blog's analytical ranking](https://blog.thebluealliance.com/2016/01/12/what-is-the-best-frc-game-and-where-could-stronghold-fall/)** (Francis O'Rourke, 2016) scored 2003–2015 games on weighted criteria — match quality (30%), robot diversity (25%), spectator friendliness (20%), strategic diversity (15%), originality (10%) — and ranked **FIRST Frenzy 2004 best** and **Recycle Rush 2015 worst** ("boring and extremely repetitive," its only strength being originality). A [2017 follow-up](https://blog.thebluealliance.com/2017/01/05/why-stronghold-may-be-the-best-frc-game-of-all-time/) argued Stronghold ranked second analytically but first in lived experience because it "raised the floor of match scores" and "students loved the game."

Every poll thread self-flagellates about **recency and age bias**: "You're going to see a lot of recency bias here, especially as a lot of active CD users are still students" (pigrammer, 2025 thread); "I doubt many people will vote for a game from before they were involved" (Timebomb, [2019 thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/favorite-frc-game-of-all-time/347775)). Treat Crescendo's 2025-poll dominance with that caveat; the pick-5 poll's top cluster (2022/2014/2013/2016/2018) is probably the more durable consensus.

## 2. The Most-Loved Games and Why

### 2022 Rapid React — the modern consensus pick
In ["What did Rapid React get RIGHT?"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/what-did-rapid-react-get-right/410496), legendary strategist Karthik Kanagasabapathy called it "one of the best FRC games of all time," listing: **all non-endgame scoring in the center of the field** ("fabulous for the audience"), tasks that "looked hard to the audience but were very achievable for teams of all levels," large robot-design variety, a "dynamic, exciting" traversal-climb endgame, and game pieces that were "easy to source, relatively consistent, and very visual." Others emphasized **emergent variety** — "No match of Rapid React is the same. Due to the random nature of the ball returns, match strategy can quickly change in the middle of a match" (Alex_Y) — and **alliance interaction**: robots naturally crossed paths chasing cargo, unlike the segregated cycling of 2019/2020 (ferrari77). Knufire: "This, IMO, is the best game since 2014, and it's not close." The one repeated gripe: mid-air ball collisions "made it feel like a lotto at times" (Cory_Walters).

### 2014 Aerial Assist — "the closest we've had to a real sport"
Loved for forced **teamwork via assists**: "The way some alliances meshed perfectly and were able to set up pick after pick, run circles around the defense... really made the game beautiful to watch" (Abhishek_R, [2016 best-game poll](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/what-was-the-best-frc-game-poll/146375)). "It was the game most like a real sport that I have seen" (Jsteel, [Best FRC Game?](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/best-frc-game/425334)). SPang: "Can FIRST just rerun Aerial Assist in 2024? It's the kind of well designed game that you can make an entire robotics sporting league from." Watchability for lay spectators is cited constantly.

### 2013 Ultimate Ascent — frisbees and strategy
"Visually incredible to watch the frisbees fly," and "strategy was king that year — the winning alliance had no full-court shooters" (Ryan_Swanson). The counterpoint: **full-court shooters ruined it for weak teams** — "teams that could make full court shots were effectively playing a different game... you just showed up, watched the other team constantly score, and left the field after you lost" (Timebomb), and wall-bots "took [defense] to an outrageous level" (Camren).

### 2016 Stronghold — balance and legibility
"Brilliantly balanced; it was easy to build a robot that could win events with just a drivetrain and an intake, but it was still a challenging game to play at high levels... among the more easily explainable to non-FRC folks" (Brian_Maher, Top-5 thread). The TBA blog praised its theme immersion, alliance-selected defenses, and raised scoring floor, while conceding the **low bar pushed teams toward suboptimal designs**.

### 2024 Crescendo — the new-era favorite
Won the 2025 poll decisively. Reasons: "Extremely well-balanced, fun to watch, surprising viability of different strategies like passing" (ChiefK33f, [Thoughts on Crescendo](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/thoughts-on-crescendo-a-few-weeks-in/460982)); the amp/speaker duality rewarded complete robots; comeback mechanics — "any alliance could score 22 points on a dime, making every match somewhat winnable... 1678 [alliance] vs alliance 8 was interesting — itself a feat of game design" (TrevDay, 2025 thread). Complaints: field faults, a climb/trap endgame "not nearly worth enough points" (seg9585, Pizzatc), fragile notes with "atrocious" availability, and mid-season meta-copying homogenizing designs.

### 2018 Power Up and the "chess match" games
Power Up is loved for **contested control**: "It really felt like a competition. You were fighting for control over something... the main scoring happened in the middle of the field which was amazing for spectating" (pkrishna3082); "big field elements make for a very cool audience view as you can see the advantage shifting from one alliance to another" (kierajreed). A vocal old-guard contingent pines for 2004/2005/2007-style back-and-forth: "I am DYING for the era of chess match games to make a comeback... [they] felt like the two alliances were actually in dialogue during a match... ever since 2007 it's always just a race for points" (James1902, 2025 thread). The acknowledged downside: "some don't like them because you couldn't immediately see what alliance was in the lead" (GaryVoshol).

## 3. The Most-Disliked Games and Why

### 2015 Recycle Rush — the consensus worst
Still winning "least favorite" a decade later (145/401 in 2025). The specific charges, from the [worst-game poll](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/poll-worst-frc-game/146451) and [A Recycle Rush Reflection](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/a-recycle-rush-reflection/143576):
- **Zero defense / zero interaction**: two alliances stacking totes side-by-side with no contact allowed.
- **Repetitive and boring**: "Once on an alliance you did the same thing over and over... Can Wars decided your fate. No amount of strategy could change the results of an unfavorable matchup" (ACardinali).
- **Luck and degenerate meta**: the center-field "canburgling" race decided matches in the first second; "save for very few exceptions internal capper stackers were the only correct answer" (Gregor).
- **Predictable outcomes**: averaged-score ranking made results track OPR almost perfectly; no comeback mechanisms.
- **Dead atmosphere**: "There was no energy from the crowd... teams left very early" (Ryan_Swanson, on his 2015 regional).

Notably, defenders exist: it was "a wonderful design challenge" with "by far the most varied designs" (John_Retkowski), and elims at deep fields were genuinely tense ("a six-stack with can and litter tottering back and forth generates a thrill" — EricAnderson191). The community verdict, crisply put by troy_dietz in the Top-5 thread: "TBF the title is 'FRC Games', not 'FRC Engineering Challenges'."

### 2009 Lunacy — luck on a slippery floor
"Generally 2 types of people voted in this poll: People who voted for Recycle Rush, [and] People who witnessed Lunacy" (BBray_T1296). Specifics: the regolith floor + mandated slick wheels made driving luck-heavy; static buildup "shorting out electronics"; trailer scoring meant you couldn't protect your own goal; **traffic jams** stalled matches; "the bottom 50% couldn't participate." Old-timers counter that it was a fun design challenge if your team did well — "the group of people I know who really liked Lunacy is wholly contained within the group whose teams did really well" (Basel_A).

### 2023 Charged Up — solved too early
Second-worst in the 2025 poll. "Charged Up felt 'solved' before teams even got to Houston; supercharging was a band-aid solution" (MrPotato, [Review of Reefscape](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/review-of-reefscape/499842)); "a game best played by upgrading your robot cycles by micro amounts in the shop, sort of like 2015" (Ben_Martin). Yet its charge-station endgame has passionate defenders: "It was so much fun watching the charge station go back and forth in a close match... one of the best viewing experiences ever" (Clodsire).

### 2001 Diabolical Dynamics — the veterans' pick
4-vs-0 cooperative format with no defense; team 71 solved it so completely that "the 710 matches were less than 60 seconds... there was NOTHING you could do to stop them" (Tyler_Olds, EricH). Cited as proof that no-defense formats fail.

### Divisive games worth noting
- **2017 Steamworks**: ranks high in polls (novel human-player interaction, steampunk theme, gear-cycling teamwork) but draws sharp criticism: "two poorly-conceived games smushed together... fuel was almost irrelevant. What was left was a Crescendo-like chore of running gears... and if you missed a climb the whole effort was wasted" (nuclearnerd); pegs bent, and low-level teams "won/lost matches at random based on whether their partners could climb" (Kevin_Leonard).
- **2025 Reefscape**: 2nd-favorite AND 4th-least-favorite in the same poll. Praised: brutal-but-fair design tradeoffs, visible progress on the reef, strategy that evolved all season ("L1 became the most critical to win at champs, which is rare" — VR9450). Criticized: "mostly a scoring exhibition" (Ben_Martin); "at champs it felt like watching Recycle Rush, with neither alliance crossing the field" (WorseSquash9); "easily 2025 was the most boring [match strategy]: 'we'll cycle on this side, you cycle on that side, deep climb at end'" (TrevDay); defense uneconomical; processor dead after quals.
- **2019 Deep Space / 2020 Infinite Recharge**: middling; "very predictable matches — one team would get shut down by defense while the other cycled" (Alex_Y).

## 4. Recurring Cross-Game Complaints

**Hidden or illegible scoring.** The community consistently punishes games where spectators can't tell who's winning. The 2025 processor (scoring algae feeds the opponent, value opaque, "dead after qual matches" — BenjaminMiller) is the newest example; old chess-match games suffered the same ("you couldn't immediately see what alliance was in the lead"). The praised antidote is Rapid React's central hub — "you don't have to dart your eyes across the field to figure out who's winning" (Anthony_Galea) — and Reefscape's visible reef fill ("every piece of algae and coral is trackable... a lot more fun to spectate" — FieryFuryGame).

**RP structures that distort play.** From [Balancing Ranking Points](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/balancing-ranking-points/356543): RPs "come across as weird side quests that distract from the main mechanic and confuse spectators... captains who seeded high are maybe not prepared to play a different game in playoffs" (nuclearnerd). 2018/2019 spawned "RP teams" optimizing rank over winning; the 2016 RP is held up as best-integrated. In [2025's RP thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/thoughts-on-ranking-point-system-this-year/499699), the complaint inverted: RPs were **too easy**, making win/loss and schedule luck dominant ("some of the best teams ranked in the 30s with top-5 EPA" — Sands), and the auto RP punished teams randomly paired with partners who couldn't move in auto (Alex_Y). Defenders note RPs let good teams overcome bad schedules (Hjelstrom) and beat pure win-loss or the contentious 2012/2015 coopertition tasks (John_Bottenberg).

**No defense / no interaction = boring.** The single strongest predictor of a hated game (2015, 2001, late-season 2025). Conversely, every beloved game (2013, 2014, 2016, 2018, 2022, 2024) features meaningful alliance interaction. But over-strong defense is also punished (2013 wall-bots, 2019 rocket lockdowns).

**Endgame balance is never right.** Steamworks' climb was "make-or-break the entire match" (Drew_B); "every FRC game somehow thinks we need an overpowered mini game at the end" (nuclearnerd). Yet Crescendo's climb was too weak ("I just wish endgame was more valuable" — SBowling4). Endgame **traffic jams** recur: Lunacy's pileups, 2019 HAB congestion, 2024's mid-field stage causing "NASCAR pileups" (Rethum865), 2025's hard-to-see cage climbs (pmattin5459).

**Meta solved too early / design homogenization.** 2015 (capper-stackers), 2023 (solved pre-Houston), plus the modern complaint that "top teams can completely steal the 'meta' robot mid-way through the season," making FRC "more homogenous" (ChiefK33f) — famously mocked by 148's jjsessa: "This year we built a decoy turret bot."

**Game piece supply and durability.** 2024 notes: "fragile... availability is atrocious. We spent the last 4 weeks testing with worn/broken notes" (seg9585). Contrast: Rapid React's dog-toy cargo praised — "amazed at how durable these game pieces are" (petercooperjr).

**Field cost / practice-field buildability.** [Reef Branch Rant](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/reef-branch-rant/482269): if 2025's branches had been 45° instead of 35°, teams could have built them from big-box PVC fittings; instead official elements were expensive with non-refundable pre-orders. A perennial equity complaint ([For teams with full practice fields, how?](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/for-teams-with-full-practice-fields-how/519911)).

**Auto/luck dominance.** 2025 elite play reduced to "who had the better auto and who can get more algae" (Boxsteam); 2015 canburgling; 2022's aerial ball collisions; schedule luck amplified whenever RPs are too easy or partner-dependent.

## 5. Synthesis: The Community's Implicit Design Rubric

Across a decade of threads, the same criteria keep deciding reputations: (1) **watchability** — visible, centralized, legible scoring; (2) **defense and alliance interaction**, tuned so it matters but can't fully shut out offense; (3) **low floor, high ceiling** — a drivetrain-and-intake robot can contribute while elite teams stay challenged (Stronghold, Rapid React are the archetypes); (4) **strategic variety that stays unsolved all season** (Reefscape's late L1/algae meta praised; Charged Up's early solve damned); (5) **an endgame that's exciting and can swing matches without invalidating the previous 2 minutes**; (6) **comeback/randomness mechanisms that create drama without deciding matches by luck**; and (7) **accessible game pieces and field elements**. Recycle Rush fails nearly all seven; Rapid React and Aerial Assist pass nearly all — which is exactly where the polls put them.
