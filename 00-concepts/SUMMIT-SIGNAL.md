# SUMMIT SIGNAL

> This is a design-phase document kept for reference. The current game is defined by `01-design/DESIGN-SPEC.md` and the Game Manual (`02-manual/GAME-MANUAL.md`).

**Theme:** High-altitude expedition crews race an incoming storm to rebuild and relight a dormant network of mountain signal beacons before nightfall.

**Narrative:** The storm is six hours out and the ridge has gone dark. For a century the beacon line above Kernel Peak carried warnings from summit to valley, until the network failed. Two expedition alliances climb with rings of cable, fuel cells and signal flares, assembling beacons tower by tower until the mountainside is lit again. The last flare lights the summit, and the alliances then climb as high as they can before the storm arrives.

## Game Pieces
### CACHE RING
- Shape/dims: Torus (ring): 14 in outside diameter, 4 in cross-section, 6 in inner hole. Weight ~1.3 lb.
- Material: Die-cut laminated EVA foam ring with a tear-resistant spiral fabric wrap (designed to be more durable than a 2024 Note). Team version: two 2.5-in pool noodles coiled and gaffer-taped into a 14-in torus.
- Handling challenge: It lies flat on the carpet, which forces an over-bumper ground intake and a reorientation path from horizontal (floor) to horizontal above a post (drop-on) or vertical (hang on 30-degree Signal Hooks). The 6-in hole over a 1.9-in post is forgiving, but stacking cleanly at 76 in demands elevator + wrist control.
- Count/staging: 14 per alliance in Supply Station stock, 4 staged on centerline marks, available as preload. The highest-count piece in the game.
- Scoring role: Volume currency. The cheapest piece (3-8 pts by height). Rings drive the Signal RP piece count and form the base layers of every beacon. They are the main cycle piece for low-floor and high-ceiling teams alike.

### FUEL CELL
- Shape/dims: Spool (thick-walled open cylinder): 10 in diameter x 10 in tall with a 4.5-in through-bore. Weight ~2.5 lb.
- Material: Molded dense polyethylene foam spool with a Cordura skin and reinforced bore liner. Team version: 10-in Sonotube core wrapped in pool-noodle foam, with plywood-ring ends.
- Handling challenge: Bulky, and it must be presented vertically to thread its 4.5-in bore over the post. It forces a wide intake, indexing and orientation control (it arrives from the chute on its side), and a wrist or turret that holds it plumb at up to 82 in. The mid-weight, mid-precision piece.
- Count/staging: 10 per alliance in Supply Station stock, 3 staged on centerline marks, available as preload.
- Scoring role: Mid-value structural piece (4-9 pts). A beacon cannot light without at least one Cell beneath the Flare, so Cells are the bottleneck resource that elite alliances manage for the Network RP.

### FLARE
- Shape/dims: Cone: 10-in-square base, 12 in tall, with a 3-in-diameter x 4-in-deep socket molded up into the base center. Weight ~1.8 lb.
- Material: Semi-rigid molded PVC cone with a rubberized skin and a retro-reflective tip band (functionally a re-engineered 12-in traffic cone). Team version: an off-the-shelf 12-in traffic cone with the skirt trimmed to 10 in square.
- Handling challenge: The precision piece. Its blind socket must seat over the tapered post tip, which needs a Charged Up-style cone grip plus tip-up pose control at heights from 38 to 82 in. The socket is 1 in oversize and the post tips are chamfered, so it is a funnel fit rather than a threading operation, and it looks harder than it is.
- Count/staging: 8 per alliance in Supply Station stock, 3 staged on centerline marks, available as preload.
- Scoring role: The capstone. It has the highest per-piece value (5-11 pts), and seating a Flare on a post that already holds a Ring and a Cell LIGHTS THE BEACON: it locks the stack, triggers the tower LEDs and pays the +6/+10 completion bonus. Flares are the win-condition piece and the center of the First Light auto.

## Field Layout
Standard 27 ft x 54 ft carpeted field, rotationally symmetric. From each alliance wall outward:
1. STARTING ZONE: a 10-ft-deep zone against the alliance wall with three marked start slots.
2. ASCENT WALL: a 12-ft-wide x 8-ft-tall lumber-and-pipe endgame structure in the right-hand corner against the alliance wall (out of all scoring traffic, 2022-hangar-style), with three separated climb lanes.
3. RIDGELINE: the alliance's scoring arc, 10 ft from the centerline, spanning the field width. LOW TOWER (left third): two posts, base plate 8 in, post tips 38 in. MID TOWER (center third): two posts, base 28 in, tips 58 in, plus two SIGNAL HOOKS (18-in pegs angled 30 degrees above horizontal at 40 in height on the tower's outer faces). HIGH TOWER (right third): one post, base 46 in, tip 76 in. All posts are 1.9-in OD with chamfered tips; every post accepts a maximum of 4 pieces, with the Flare always last. A CACHE DEPOT, a 4 ft x 8 ft plywood floor tray with a 2-in lip, sits at the foot of each Ridgeline.
4. THE SUMMIT: a single shared center structure with a 48 in x 48 in footprint and a 30 in truss base, carrying one alliance-colored post per side on opposite faces (base 52 in, tip 82 in). These are the highest-value posts in the game, 48 in apart, so opposing robots score back to back and never intake to intake.
5. SUPPLY STATION: each alliance's human-player chute is in the FAR corner (diagonally across, beside the opposing alliance wall, on the opposite side from the opposing Ascent Wall), so every cycle crosses the full field through shared neutral space.

Ten neutral pieces (4 Rings, 3 Cells, 3 Flares) are staged on marks along the centerline flanking the Summit. AprilTags (36h11) are on every tower face, both Summit faces, the Supply Stations and the Ascent Walls.

Notes:
- Any piece on a post scores FOR that post's alliance, regardless of who placed it, which removes denial and pollution exploits. Removing pieces from any post is a tech foul.
- A piece is scored when it is at rest, fully supported by the post or tray, and free of robot contact.
- A lit beacon is locked; its points and RP credit cannot be undone. LEDs are triggered by a seat sensor under the Flare. The fallback definition that referees can verify is "Flare seated above at least one Ring and one Cell," which is visible from the stands.

## Match Flow
2:30 total: 15 s AUTO and 2:15 TELEOP, with the final 30 s as ENDGAME. The core loop: collect from the far-corner Supply Station or the floor, carry diagonally across the field through neutral space, and place on your Ridgeline or the Summit. Because each alliance's pickup is diagonally across from its scoring arc, all six robots cross paths through the middle continuously, so the geometry creates contested space without forcing collisions. The back-to-back post layout of the Summit means the highest-value cycles happen 4 ft from an opponent without intake-vs-intake contact. Defense is legal everywhere except in the opponent's Supply Station protected zone and in the Ascent Wall zones during the last 30 s. Archetypes diverge: ring-volume cyclers work the Low and Mid towers and the hooks, Cell haulers build beacon bases, Flare specialists complete beacons and go for the Summit, and a kitbot pushes Depot pieces and hangs hook rings. Spectators can read the match at a glance: towers fill with stacked pieces, and every completed beacon LIGHTS, so each half of the mountain shows its alliance's progress. The endgame pulls robots out of the scoring lanes to their own corner walls, so the last 30 s holds three simultaneous climbs per side without traffic.

## Autonomous
Auto lasts 15 seconds and is built on decisions rather than a fixed script. At T=0 the FMS randomizes First Light, one of the three Ridgeline towers (Low, Mid or High) per alliance, and broadcasts it at the same time as a game-data character ('L'/'M'/'H', 2018-style, readable in robot code) and as a white strobe on the physical tower. Every piece scored on the First Light tower in auto earns +2. If the alliance LIGHTS that beacon in auto (Ring + Cell + Flare seated), it scores a +12 First Light bonus and banks a locked lit beacon toward the Network RP.

Because one robot carries one preload, lighting First Light in 15 seconds requires alliance coordination. Teams negotiate in advance who preloads the Ring, the Cell and the Flare, claim non-overlapping start slots, and write code that branches to the correct tower at runtime. Three tower positions times three preload roles means that a serious auto suite has nine paths, all AprilTag-aligned. The greedy alternative is to race for the 10 neutral centerline pieces (first-come contested space, 2022-cargo-style), so alliances choose between the coordinated First Light play, a volume auto or a hybrid.

Mobility is 3 pts per robot, all auto piece values are teleop+2, and everything scored in auto persists. A converted First Light is a locked beacon, an early Summit piece and a head start of ~25-30 pts that shapes which RPs are live for the rest of the match.

## Endgame
The ASCENT WALL is a 12-ft-wide climbing face for each alliance, in the corner against its own alliance wall, with three physically separated lanes and three tiers of pipe rungs: FOOTHOLD at 42 in, CRUX at 63 in and the SUMMIT BAR at 84 in. Each successive rung is staggered 12 in horizontally, so climbing higher means traversing, 2022-style.

Points (final 30 s): Shelter park (bumpers inside the wall zone) = 2; hanging from Foothold = 6; Crux = 12; Summit Bar = 20; plus a +5 ROPE TEAM bonus if two or more robots on one wall finish at Crux or higher.

Difficulty: the Summit Bar demands a two-stage traversal climber, with hooks that walk from rung to rung under swing loads, the hardest class of mechanism in recent FRC. Reward, without a cliff: every tier banks points as soon as it is achieved, a failed reach for the Summit Bar still keeps Crux credit, and a pure drivetrain robot earns its park. The maximum alliance output is 65 (3x20 + 5), enough to swing any close match but only ~30% of a strong alliance's total, so it cannot erase two minutes of beacon work. Three lanes per wall and the corner placement prevent endgame traffic jams, and the match ends with six robots swinging up the staggered rungs in front of the lit beacons.

## Ranking Points
Win = 3 RP, Tie = 1 RP, plus three bonus RPs that all reinforce the core beacon game:

| Bonus RP | Requirement | Regional | DCMP | Champs | Calibration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SIGNAL RP (entry volume) | Score N total game pieces (posts, hooks or depot) | 16 | 20 | 24 | A median three-robot alliance cycling ~6 pieces each reaches it roughly half the time at Regionals |
| NETWORK RP (stretch, ~3-3.5x entry difficulty) | LIGHT N beacons | 4 | 5 | 6 including the Summit beacon | Requires ~12+ correctly distributed pieces plus 4-6 precision Flare caps across all heights; realistic only for coordinated elite alliances. A converted First Light auto counts, which ties auto directly into ranking |
| ASCENT RP (endgame) | Total Ascent Wall points >= threshold | 24 | 30 | 36 | The Regional threshold equals two robots reaching the mid-tier Crux (12+12), per the two-robots-mid-tier calibration |

All three RPs are measured on the primary field elements; there are no side quests.

## Low Floor
A kitbot with a simple claw or roller intake contributes throughout the match. It can push and drop any of the three pieces into the Cache Depot (2 pts each, floor level, no lift required; every Depot piece also counts toward the Signal RP), grab Cache Rings and hang them on the 40-in Signal Hooks (4 pts, a single fixed-height arm), drop Rings on the 38-in Low Tower posts, play cross-field defense in the shared neutral lanes, and park in the Ascent Wall shelter for 2. The floor tasks look easy and are easy (the s-neff principle): a 6-in ring hole over a chamfered 1.9-in post is as forgiving as ring toss. The showy tasks, the 82-in Summit cap and the 84-in traversal, look as hard as they are, so no team meets hidden difficulty.

## High Ceiling
Elite teams chase the Network RP and the Summit economy. Lighting all six beacons requires handling all three geometries at four heights, which no 115-lb robot does optimally: an intake that handles everything costs the weight that a Summit Bar traversal climber needs, so the meta forces specialized drafts (ring cycler + cell hauler + flare capper, or two generalists + a climber). The nine-path First Light auto, the contested centerline pieces, Summit posts 48 in from opposing traffic, and the Cell-scarcity bottleneck (10 per alliance) keep strategy shifting. Early season favors Low/Mid volume; late season favors Flare-capping speed and sequencing that cannot be denied (since caps lock stacks, the ORDER of completion becomes the skill teams study). Because value comes from distribution across locations, there is no single optimal robot to converge on, which applies the lesson of Reefscape.

## Teaching Value
The game forces a Rapid React-class spread of mechanisms: an over-bumper ground intake (flat Rings), a wide indexing and orientation intake (Cells arrive sideways and must score vertical), a cone-style gripper with pose control (Flares), a decision between an elevator plus wrist or an arm spanning 8 in to 82 in, and a traversal climber with rung-to-rung hook sequencing. Software students get FMS game-data parsing with runtime auto branching (a nine-path auto suite), AprilTag auto-align on every scoring face, full-field pose estimation for diagonal cycles through traffic, and superstructure state machines for three piece types. Strategy students get a live problem in resource economics (Cell scarcity, the order of beacon completion, RP arithmetic). The game is designed as a curriculum for the whole team.

## Buildability
Every element breaks down into a team version built from hardware-store materials.
- Towers: a 2-ft-square base box from a half sheet of plywood, a 4x4 lumber core, and 1.5-in schedule-40 PVC posts with chamfered coupler tips. Signal Hooks are PVC stubs on 30-degree fittings. One tower takes a half sheet plus scrap lumber.
- Summit: two sheets of plywood as a 48-in torsion box with two PVC posts. LEDs are optional everywhere; a $15 string light and a lever switch under the Flare seat replicate the lighting.
- Cache Depot: one half sheet plus a 1x3 lip.
- Ascent Wall: 4x4/2x4 A-frames with 1.25-in black-pipe rungs at 42/63/84 in, the proven 2022 team-version pattern.
- Pieces: Rings from pool noodles or EVA sheet, Cells from 10-in Sonotube plus foam wrap, and Flares from trimmed 12-in traffic cones (~$8).

All robot-critical dimensions land on round or half-inch values (1.9-in post OD, 6-in ring bore, 4.5-in cell bore, 3-in socket, 38/58/76/82-in tips, 42/63/84-in rungs), and every angle in the game is 30 degrees or vertical, with no 35-degree angles like the 2025 reef branches. A full practice field takes about 8 sheets of plywood, dimensional lumber and PVC.

## Risks
- Three distinct piece geometries invite intake weight creep. If a robot that handles everything proves impossible under 115 lb, weaker teams may feel locked out of two-thirds of the game. The Depot and Hooks accept simple mechanisms, which mitigates this, but the perception risk is real.
- Flare capping at 76-82 in is the precision ceiling. If the chamfered-tip funnel fit is less forgiving than designed, capping becomes a source of frustration and the Network RP becomes unreachable. The 1-in socket oversize and the tip chamfer must be validated with real prototypes before the design freezes.
- Teams may develop short-range Ring lobbing onto Low posts and Hooks, which would weaken the primacy of pick-and-place. The geometry limits this (deep stacks, 6-in bore, capping requires placement), but a launching meta at the Low towers is possible, and the rules may need a limit on launch distance.
- The seat-sensor LED beacon indicator is a single point of failure for field reliability. The fallback definition that people can see (Flare seated over Ring and Cell) keeps matches scoreable, but any sensor unreliability in week 1 would damage trust in the game's signature moment.
- Summit posts 48 in apart on a shared structure are the closest robot-to-robot proximity in the game. The back-to-back geometry should prevent intake entanglement like that of the 2026 G415, but aggressive Summit defense could still cause contact-rule disputes and needs a tightly worded protected-contact rule.
- The durability of the foam Cell under repeated 82-in drops and drivetrain run-overs is unproven. The piece needs abuse testing that applies the lessons of the Crescendo note, or teams will resent replacement costs.

## Spectator Hook
A beacon lights when a Flare seats: the tower LEDs turn on in the alliance color, one tower at a time, so the crowd watches the mountainside light up. The sequence ends with a lit Summit at center field while six robots swing from rung to rung up the Ascent Walls, and the brighter side of the mountain shows which alliance has built more.
