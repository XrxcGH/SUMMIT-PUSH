# History of FRC Games, 1992–2026

## Key takeaways
- FRC games fall into two main scoring archetypes that alternate: shooting games (1995, 2006, 2012, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022, 2026), which reward cycle speed and vision, and pick-and-place games (2005, 2007, 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023, 2025), which reward precise manipulators. Outliers include racing (2008), moving-goal trailers (2009) and pass chains (2014).
- Autonomous was introduced in 2003 (Stack Attack) and grew from scripted routines into a decision layer: randomized hot goals in 2014, randomized plate colors in 2018 that required branching on FMS data, the 2019 Sandstorm curtain that allowed driving blind by camera, and the 20-second auto of 2026, whose winner sets the hub-shift pattern for the whole match.
- Endgame design escalated steadily: ramp parking (2000s) -> bar hangs (2004) -> buddy lifts (2007, 2010) -> deployed minibots (2011) -> balancing puzzles (2012 bridges, 2020 generator switch, 2023 charge station) -> multi-level and traversal climbs (2013, 2019 HAB triple buddy climbs, 2022 monkey bars, 2024 chains with harmony, 2025 deep cages). The top tiers always demand either heavy investment in mechanisms or alliance coordination.
- Multi-piece games use three structures: dependency (2019 hatch before cargo), segregated destinations (2023 cone nodes and cube shelves, unified by Links) and asymmetric value (2017 gears and fuel). The 2017 game is the failure case, in which one piece became nearly worthless; 2025 fixed the problem by making algae removal a gateway and a coopertition currency.
- The modern bonus Ranking Point system began in 2016 (Breach/Capture). Since then most games have had one scoring-volume RP and one endgame RP (2017 and 2018 are the exceptions), which act as the minimum competitive spec sheet. 2025 raised a win from 2 to 3 RP to weight winning above RP farming.
- Coopertition recurs as a design theme: the word was coined in 2000, 2001 had an all-cooperative alliance, 2012 a shared bridge and 2015 a shared tote stack. Since 2023 it has been implemented as relief on RP thresholds (co-op grid, co-op amp button, 2 algae per processor lowering the Coral RP).
- 2015 Recycle Rush is the main structural outlier: no defense, parallel independent fields, and qualification ranking by average score instead of win/loss. It shows how much the ranking structure alone can change gameplay.
- Randomized or state-driven field elements are the main tool against scripted play: 2014 hot goals, 2016 audience-selected defenses (thousands of field configurations), 2018 randomized plates, 2020 control panel colors announced by the FMS, and the alternating active/inactive hubs of 2026, which add a timing and burst dimension to scoring.
- Game pieces have moved from commodity sporting goods (tennis balls, soccer balls, inner tubes) to custom objects that force manipulator design (2005 tetras, 2018 milk-crate cubes, 2023 rubber cones and fabric cubes, 2025 PVC coral and 16-inch algae). Shooting years settle on foam balls of ~5-10 inches (2016 boulders, 2020 power cells, 2026 fuel at 5.91 inches with 504 balls on the field).
- 2026 REBUILT specifics: a single game piece (Fuel foam balls into your own Hub, 1 pt only while it is active); a 20-second auto (the longest to date) that determines the shift advantage; teleop split into a transition shift, four 25-second alternating hub shifts and a 30-second endgame; a 3-rung Tower climb (10/20/30, with L1 climbable in auto for 15); RPs for Energized at 100 fuel, Supercharged at 360 fuel and Traversal at 50 tower points (kickoff values; `gap-critic.md` covers the mid-season changes); and a win worth 3 RP.
- Human players have scored since 1996 (throwing in Hexagon Havoc) and have repeatedly become strategic actors: 2004 human shooters, 2017 airship pilots, 2024 amplify-button operators and High Note throwers, 2025 processor-to-net feeders.
- Sources for verification: FIRST's official game-summaries-1992-2012 PDF (firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf), team358.org history pages for early games, the Wikipedia article for each game from 2013-2026, the 2025 Game Manual Section 6 for exact REEFSCAPE values, and frcmanual.com/2026 for REBUILT.

## Era 1: Early games (1992–1998)

These games had 2-minute matches, fully driver-controlled, with no autonomous period and no endgame. Strategy meant possession, denial and defense of a shared scoring resource.

- **1992 – Maize Craze.** Four robots on a 16'×16' arena covered in a 1.5" layer of whole corn kernels, which made traction part of the challenge. Piece: tennis balls of differing point values. Mechanic: collect, return to home base, and defend your cache. ([FIRST summaries PDF](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf))
- **1993 – Rug Rage.** Five large air-filled kickballs (5 pts) and twenty small water-filled balls (1 pt). Robots scored into their own goal and defended it, and could steal from opponents' goals. The game introduced wireless robot control ([Team 358](http://team358.org/history/1992-1999/1993RugRage.php)).
- **1994 – Tower Power.** Team-colored soccer balls into a shared two-level goal: high goal 3 pts, low goal 1 pt. This was the first high/low goal risk-reward tradeoff, a pattern FRC reuses constantly (2006, 2012, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022).
- **1995 – Ramp 'n Roll.** 24" and 30" vinyl balls. Robots raced down a 30-ft raceway over a speed bump, then pushed or shot balls over a 9-foot field goal. Balls could be rescored repeatedly (2 and 3 pts per score). The game had the first terrain obstacle and the first mechanic of shooting over a height.
- **1996 – Hexagon Havoc.** A hexagonal carpeted field with a central goal; twelve 8" balls (3 pts) and two 24" balls per team (10 pts in the hex goal, 5 pts on corner ramps). Belted-in human players could throw balls, the origin of the human player as an active scorer ([Team 358](http://team358.org/history/1992-1999/1996HexagonHavoc.php)).
- **1997 – Toroid Terror.** Color-coded inner tubes (toroids) placed on pegs on a central goal or over its top. This was the first game of placing pieces on pegs (an ancestor of 2007 and 2011).
- **1998 – Ladder Logic.** Color-coded balls placed onto side goals and a tall central goal; human players could throw directly at goals. Scoring followed a vertical hierarchy on a ladder-like structure.

Pattern: pieces were commodity sporting goods, scoring was a count of possessions at the buzzer, and defense of a cache took the place of an endgame.

## Era 2: Alliances and multipliers (1999–2004)

- **1999 – Double Trouble.** The first 2-team alliances. Pieces: "floppies," lightweight pillow-like discs with Velcro edges. Scoring was by elevation: floppies held 2"–8' off the floor = 1 pt; above 8' = 3 pts. An ancestor of the endgame: any robot climbing onto the central raised puck ×3 multiplied the alliance score, a high-variance multiplier ([FIRST PDF](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf)).
- **2000 – Co-opertition FIRST.** The word "coopertition" entered the vocabulary. 2v2; yellow balls (1 pt) and rarer black balls (5 pts) into goals; endgame points for robots on the ramp and a large bonus for hanging from the bar, FRC's first hanging endgame.
- **2001 – Diabolical Dynamics.** An experiment in which all four teams played as ONE alliance to maximize a single score. Small balls 1 pt, large balls 10 pts; 10 pts/robot in the End Zone; balancing the bridge doubled the score; ending the match early multiplied the score up to ×3; a large ball atop a goal gave a team ×1.1. It was a pure cooperation experiment with stacked multipliers.
- **2002 – Zone Zeal.** The first modern 2v2 alliance-versus-alliance game. The field was split into 5 zones, and three 130-lb movable goals were themselves game objects: robots stuffed soccer balls into goals, dragged whole goals into their scoring zone, and returned home before the buzzer. Tug-of-war over goals became the defining feature of the game.
- **2003 – Stack Attack.** Piece: plastic storage containers (Rubbermaid-style totes). Score = containers in your zone, with stack height determining multipliers; robots also earned 25 pts for being atop the central ramp ("king of the hill"). The major innovation was the first 15-second autonomous period, added to make programming matter ([FIRSTwiki](https://firstwiki.github.io/wiki/autonomous-mode), [Team 358](http://team358.org/history/2003/stackattack.php)). Many autonomous routines raced across the ramp to bulldoze the opponent's pre-staged stack.
- **2004 – FIRST Frenzy: Raising the Bar.** A multi-piece design. Robots passed 13" balls to human players, who shot them into fixed and movable goals. Three 30" multiplier balls, capped on a goal, doubled that goal's contents. Endgame: hang from a 10-ft bar (50 pts). Auto used IR beacons to find and knock down bonus balls. This is the template year: a multi-piece economy, a multiplier piece and a large hang.

## Era 3: The modern template (2005–2014)

- **2005 – Triple Play.** The first 3v3 alliances, the format ever since. Piece: pyramidal tetras. Nine goals formed a 3×3 tic-tac-toe matrix; capping a goal claimed ownership, and rows of three owned goals scored 10 pts. Territorial control was layered on pick-and-place at height (tetras capped ~7-ft goals). Auto featured a camera-tracked vision tetra.
- **2006 – Aim High.** Shooting returned: foam "poof" balls launched into a raised center high goal (3 pts) or floor corner goals (1 pt), with a green vision target for camera-aimed autonomous shooting. Endgame: robots raced back and climbed a ramp to a platform for bonus points. The match structure (auto / teleop / endgame sprint) is recognizably modern.
- **2007 – Rack 'N' Roll.** Pure pick-and-place: inflated tubes ("ringers") hung on the pegs of a 10-ft central Rack. Scoring was exponential in row length (2^(n−1) per contiguous line), with auto-placed Keepers locking spots and Spoilers negating opponents' tubes. Endgame: 15 pts for each robot in the home zone lifted ≥4" off the floor by a partner robot, the first buddy lift ([FIRST PDF](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/game-summaries-1992-2012.pdf)).
- **2008 – FIRST Overdrive.** A racing game: robots drove counterclockwise laps around a center barrier while moving 40" inflated Trackballs over or under a 6'6" overpass (hurdling scored heavily). Endgame: placing Trackballs back onto the overpass (12 pts). Auto became "Hybrid mode," in which a human Robocoach could send IR-remote signals to the robot ([Wikipedia – FIRST Overdrive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIRST_Overdrive)).
- **2009 – Lunacy.** Played on slick "regolith" (a low-friction polymer floor) with mandated slippery wheels to simulate lunar gravity. Pieces: 9" Orbit Balls ("moon rocks," 2 pts) scored into trailers hitched to opponent robots, so the goals moved and evaded the shooter. Super Cells (15 pts) scored only in the last 20 seconds, and human-player payload logistics (empty cells delivered to be exchanged) created a supply chain mid-match. There was no climb; the whole game was about relative motion.
- **2010 – Breakaway.** Soccer on a 27'×54' field split into three zones by bumps with tunnels beneath. Balls into corner goals = 1 pt each (by kicking or herding, since possession >3 sec was illegal). Endgame: a robot suspended off the floor from the tower bars = 2 pts, and a robot suspended from another robot = 3 pts, the first chained buddy hang ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakaway_(FIRST))).
- **2011 – Logo Motion.** FIRST's 20th anniversary. Triangle, circle and square inner tubes hung on wall pegs (1/2/3 pts by row height) were doubled by assembling them in the order of the FIRST logo; auto Ubertubes scored double and doubled tubes later hung on the same peg. Endgame: deploy a Minibot (an FTC-legal mini robot) onto a pole for a race worth 30/20/15/10 pts by finish order. No later game has repeated this robot-deploys-robot mechanic ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo_Motion)).
- **2012 – Rebound Rumble.** Foam basketballs into hoops at three heights; auto ("Hybrid") could use Microsoft Kinect gesture control ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/first-adds-kinect-technology-to-robotics-competition-tool-kit/)). Endgame: balancing 1–3 robots on seesaw bridges. A shared white Coopertition Bridge, balanced by robots from both alliances, gave both alliances seeding points, which made coopertition a scored mechanic within the match.
- **2013 – Ultimate Ascent.** Flying discs shot into low, middle and high goals (1/2/3 pts teleop, doubled in auto; a 5-pt goal for colored discs at the top of the pyramid). Endgame: climb a 3-level pyramid for 10/20/30 pts, the first vertical multi-level climb ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Ascent)).
- **2014 – Aerial Assist.** One giant 2-ft exercise ball per scoring cycle, with goals at each end (high 10 pts / low 1 pt). The central mechanic was the Assist bonus: each additional alliance robot that possessed the ball in a different field zone before scoring added 10/30 bonus points, plus 10 pts for throwing over the midfield truss and 10 for a partner catch. Auto had randomized "hot goals" (a 5-pt bonus for scoring in the lit goal), the first element randomized at match start. There was no climb; the endgame was the scoring rally itself. The game made passing the meta ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_Assist)).

## Era 4: Ranking points (2015–2026)

- **2015 – Recycle Rush.** The main outlier: no defense and no robot interaction. Alliances scored in parallel on separate field halves, and qualification ranking was by average match score instead of W/L. Pieces: gray totes (2 pts each per level stacked), green recycling containers (4 pts × level, up to 24 atop a 6-stack), and litter (pool noodles) stuffed in containers (6 pts) or thrown into the opponent's zone. Auto: moving robots, totes and containers into the Auto Zone, including a 20-pt stacked-tote set. Coopertition: both alliances jointly stacking 4 totes on the center step = 20 pts each. There was no endgame climb; the endgame consisted of capping stacks before the buzzer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recycle_Rush)).
- **2016 – FIRST Stronghold.** A medieval siege. 10" foam Boulders were shot into a tower's high goal (7'1", 5 pts) or low goal (2 pts) after crossing randomized defenses. Each alliance selected three of eight obstacle types (moat, drawbridge, portcullis, cheval de frise, ramparts, rock wall, rough terrain, sally port), one was selected by the audience, and the low bar was fixed, giving thousands of field configurations. Auto: reach (2 pts) or cross (10 pts) a defense. Endgame: Challenge (park on the batter, 5) or Scale the tower (15). The game introduced the modern bonus Ranking Point system, with RPs for Breaching (damaging 4 defenses) and Capturing (weakening the tower with 8+ boulders and surrounding it) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIRST_Stronghold)).
- **2017 – FIRST Steamworks.** A two-piece economy with asymmetric value: 5" wiffle Fuel balls (high boiler goal = 1 kPa per ball in auto and per 3 balls in teleop, low goal a third of that; kPa = points) and 11" plastic Gears delivered to the airship, where human pilots aboard the airship cranked rotors (1/2/4/6 gears per successive rotor, 40 pts each in teleop, 60 in auto). Endgame: a rope climb to a touchpad, 50 pts. RPs: 40+ kPa and all 4 rotors. Gears dominated fuel, which makes the game a cautionary tale about balancing two-piece economies ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIRST_Steamworks)).
- **2018 – FIRST Power Up.** Piece: 13" yellow Power Cubes. Scoring was by time-based ownership instead of placement count: tip your Switch or the center Scale with cubes and accrue ~1 pt/second while you own it. The headline innovation was plate colors randomized at match start (announced seconds before auto), which forced autos with three-path decision logic that read the FMS game data. Vault cubes bought Power-Ups (Force, Boost, Levitate; Levitate granted a free climb). Endgame: climb the Scale rung (30 pts). RPs for Auto Quest (all cross the line and own the switch in auto) and Face the Boss (all three robots climbing, which encouraged buddy-climb bars) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIRST_Power_Up)).
- **2019 – Destination: Deep Space.** Two pieces with a dependency chain: 19" polycarbonate Hatch Panels (2 pts) had to seal a bay before 13" orange Cargo balls (3 pts) could be scored in it, on two 3-level Rockets and a central Cargo Ship. The auto innovation was the Sandstorm: a physical curtain blocked the drivers' view for 15 seconds, and teams chose between pre-programmed autos and driving blind through robot cameras. Endgame: climb back onto the 3-level HAB platform (3/6/12 pts), which produced the well-known triple Level 3 buddy climbs. RPs: complete a Rocket; 15+ HAB climb points ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destination:_Deep_Space)).
- **2020/2021 – Infinite Recharge.** 7" foam Power Cells shot into a 3-tier Power Port (bottom 1, outer 2, inner 3; doubled in auto). Scoring thresholds unlocked Stages: spinning the Control Panel 3–5 rotations (10 pts), then rotating it to a color announced by the FMS (20 pts + RP path), a randomized task in the middle of the match. Endgame: hang from the Generator Switch, a giant balancing beam (25 pts/robot, +15 if level); Shield RP at 65+ endgame points. COVID canceled the 2020 season in mid-March. 2021 replayed the game as Infinite Recharge at Home: remote skills challenges (AutoNav, Hyperdrive, Interstellar Accuracy, Power Port, Galactic Search) plus a Game Design Challenge, the only non-standard season to date ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Recharge), [Orlando FRC](https://www.orlandofrc.org/history)).
- **2022 – Rapid React.** 9.5" inflated Cargo (oversized tennis balls) shot into the central omnidirectional Hub (upper 2 pts / lower 1, doubled in auto). Endgame: the Hangar, four ascending monkey-bar rungs (Low 4, Mid 6, High 10, Traversal 15) climbed by swinging from rung to rung. RPs: 20 cargo (18 with a 5-cargo "quintet" auto) and 16 hangar points ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_React)).
- **2023 – Charged Up.** The clearest two-piece pick-and-place game: yellow rubber Cones (~12" tall, square base) and inflated purple fabric Cubes (~9.5") placed on a 3×9 Grid per alliance. The bottom hybrid row took any piece; the middle and top rows had cone-only posts and cube-only shelves (2–3 / 3–4 / 5–6 pts by row and period). Three adjacent filled nodes formed a Link (5 pts); the Sustainability RP required enough links, and a Coopertition grid lowered the threshold. The Charge Station, a balancing seesaw, served both auto (dock 8 / engage 12) and endgame (6/10 per robot; balances with all three robots were a highlight). Activation RP at 26 charge points ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charged_Up_(FIRST))).
- **2024 – Crescendo.** Piece: orange foam Note rings (10" ID / 14" OD). Two goals with a human-in-the-loop multiplier: Notes into your Amp (1 pt) banked charges that human players spent to Amplify the Speaker (2 pts → 5 pts for 10 seconds / 4 notes), which made managing the scoring tempo a strategy. Endgame Stage: climb hanging chains (3 pts), Harmony (+2 for two robots on one chain), human players throwing High Notes onto microphone pegs to Spotlight climbers (+1), and Notes in the elevated Trap = 5. The Coopertition button (both alliances Amp a note early) lowered the Melody RP (18→15 notes); Ensemble RP = 10 stage points + 2 climbers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescendo_(FIRST))).
- **2025 – REEFSCAPE.** Two pieces again. Coral (11.875" lengths of 4" PVC pipe) was placed on a hexagonal Reef at four levels: L1 trough 3/2 pts (auto/teleop), L2 branch 4/3, L3 6/4, L4 (6 ft) 7/5. 16" Algae balls were removed from the reef and scored in the Processor (6 pts, which gives the opponent's human player a net shot) or thrown into the Net (4 pts). Endgame Barge: park 2, shallow Cage 6, deep Cage 12 (deep cages hang near the floor and demand powerful lifting climbs). RPs (final manual): Auto RP (all leave + 1 auto coral), Coral RP (≥7 coral on every level; Coopertition, 2 algae in each processor, cuts it to 3 levels) and Barge RP (16+ pts). A win is now worth 3 RP ([2025 Manual V13](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2025/Manual/Sections/2025GameManual-06GameDetails.pdf), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reefscape)).
- **2026 – REBUILT (presented by Haas).** Piece: Fuel, 504 yellow high-density foam balls, 5.91" diameter, ~0.5 lb, up to 8 preloaded. It is a return to high-volume ball shooting. Each alliance shoots into its own Hub, and the structural change is alternating Hub activation. A 20-second auto (the longest to date) is followed by a 10-s transition, then four 25-second Alliance Shifts in which the hubs alternate between active and inactive. The alliance that wins auto gets the shift-pattern advantage: its hub is inactive in Shift 1, which gives it the active hub in the final shift before endgame. Both hubs are live in the transition and the 30-s endgame. Fuel = 1 pt in an active hub and 0 in an inactive one, so when you score matters as much as how fast. Endgame/auto: climb the ladder-like Tower (L1 = 10 teleop/15 auto, L2 = 20, L3 = 30). RPs: win 3 / tie 1, plus Energized (100 fuel), Supercharged (360 fuel) and Traversal (50 tower points) at kickoff ([frcmanual.com](https://www.frcmanual.com/2026/game-details), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebuilt_(FIRST))).

## Structural design patterns across 35 seasons

### 1. Scoring mechanics
FRC alternates between shooting games (1995, 2006, 2012, 2013 (hybrid), 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022, 2024 (hybrid), 2026) and pick-and-place games (1997, 2003, 2005, 2007, 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023, 2025). The outliers are racing (2008), moving goals (2002, 2009) and pass-centric play (2014). Shooting games reward cycle speed and turret and vision technology. Placement games reward precise manipulators and make defense more valuable (a misplaced cone costs more than a missed shot).

### 2. High and low goals
The high/low goal risk-reward tradeoff is the oldest tool in FRC game design (1994 → 2026). Every shooting game offers an easy low goal so that rookie robots score, and a hard high or inner goal so that elite robots stand out.

### 3. Multi-piece economies
Multi-piece economies take three forms:
- Dependency (2019: the hatch must precede the cargo, so every team has to handle both).
- Segregation (2023: cone nodes and cube shelves allow specialization, but links reward mixed rows).
- Asymmetric value (2017: gears >> fuel). This is the failure mode, in which one piece becomes strategically dead. 2025 fixed it by making algae removal a prerequisite for reef access and a coopertition/RP currency.

### 4. Autonomous as a decision point
Autonomous began in 2003 as scripted 15-second routines. 2004 added IR beacons; 2008 and 2012 tried "hybrid" modes with human signals (Robocoach, Kinect); 2014's randomized hot goals and 2018's randomized plate colors forced autos to read the game state and branch; 2019's Sandstorm let camera driving substitute for code; 2020's doubled auto point values made auto central to the meta. 2026 lets auto set the match structure itself (shift order) and extends it to 20 seconds.

### 5. Endgames
Endgames escalated from parking to acrobatics: ramp parking (2000, 2003, 2006) → bar hangs (2000, 2004) → buddy lifts and hangs (2007, 2010) → deployed minibots (2011) → balancing puzzles (2012 bridges, 2020 generator switch, 2023 charge station) → multi-level climbs (2013 pyramid, 2016 tower, 2019 HAB with triple buddy climbs, 2022 traversal rungs, 2024 chains with harmony, 2025 deep cages, 2026 tower). The recurring design lever is to make the top tier require either extreme investment in mechanisms or alliance coordination (buddy climbs, harmony, the Levitate power-up).

### 6. Coopertition
Coopertition mechanics recur throughout FRC history: 2000 (name coined), 2001 (all-cooperative match), 2012 (shared bridge), 2015 (shared step stack), 2023 (co-op grid lowers the Link RP), 2024 (co-op button lowers the Melody RP), 2025 (co-op algae lowers the Coral RP + tiebreaker point). Modern designs turn coopertition into relief on RP thresholds, so it aligns with ranking incentives instead of working against them.

### 7. Ranking structures
Ranking structures shape robot design as much as points do. 2015's average-score ranking (no W/L) produced the least interactive game in FRC history. 2016's bonus RPs (Breach/Capture) permanently added two objectives of the form "build a robot that can do X," independent of winning. 2025–26 raised a win to 3 RP to weight winning more heavily against RP farming. Since 2016, the bonus RPs (usually one for scoring volume and one for the endgame, with a third since 2025) have been the de facto minimum spec sheet for a competitive robot.

### 8. Randomization and state changes during the match
Randomization keeps autonomous and strategy from being fully scripted: 2014 hot goals, 2016 audience-picked defense, 2018 plate randomization, 2020 control panel color announced by the FMS, and 2026 hub shifts determined by auto. The 2026 active/inactive hub goes furthest. It adds a timing dimension (scoring in bursts, hoarding during inactive shifts) that no earlier game had.

### 9. Game pieces
Game pieces have moved from commodity to custom. Tennis balls, soccer balls and kickballs (1992–2004) gave way to purpose-built objects that force manipulator innovation: tetras (2005), toroids (1997/2011/2024), milk-crate cubes (2018), fabric cubes and rubber cones (2023), PVC coral (2025). Shooting years settle on foam balls in the 5–10" range, which are safe to launch in high volume.
