# RELIQUARY

> This is a design-phase document kept for reference. The current game is defined by `01-design/DESIGN-SPEC.md` and the Game Manual (`02-manual/GAME-MANUAL.md`).

**Theme:** The world's great lost artifacts have been recovered, and alliances of robotic curators race to restore, catalog and display them in the Grand Rotunda before the museum's opening gala.

**Narrative:** For a century the Halcyon Collection sat scattered in crates, its medallions, tablets and urns lost. Tonight the doors of the Grand Rotunda open. Robots haul artifacts across a crowded gallery floor, place them carefully on pedestals, easels and pegs, and in the final seconds climb the restoration scaffolds to unveil the skylight.

## Game Pieces
### MEDALLION
- Shape/dims: Torus/ring (a 'coin' with a hanging hole): 6 in outside diameter, 2 in inside diameter, 1.5 in thick
- Material: Molded cross-linked EVA foam ring (density similar to a pool exercise ring), ~0.4 lb, dyed bronze with a molded laurel emboss. Team version: stacked 3/4 in EVA floor-mat circles cut with a hole saw and glued, or off-the-shelf 6 in foam rings (~$3 each).
- Handling challenge: Small and plentiful. It rewards a fast touch-it-own-it roller intake and internal indexing for multi-piece capacity. The center hole must be located and oriented to hang on a peg (a wrist and vision problem), but the medallion can also be slid along the carpet into the floor-level Vault slot with a plow (no orientation needed). The easy-looking task is easy; the 66 in high peg looks hard and is hard.
- Count/staging: 20 per alliance per match: 4 staged in the alliance Dig Site, 6 staged in the neutral midfield Excavation Zone (shared, contested), and up to 10 fed by the Archivist human player through the Archive Window at the FAR end of the field. Any robot may preload 1 artifact of any type.
- Scoring role: The volume currency. Vault slide = 2 pts (the kitbot's main job), low peg (30 in) = 3, high peg (66 in) = 5 (7/5/3 in auto; see the scoring table). Medallions drive the Collection RP count and fill the visible Vault coin column that spectators read at a glance.

### TABLET
- Shape/dims: Flat rectangular slab: 12 in x 9 in x 2 in, with a 3/4 in deep x 1.5 in wide finger groove routed around the 12 in edges
- Material: Rigid polyethylene foam core with a fiber-reinforced textured TPU skin (durability of a CHARGED UP cube), ~1.2 lb, sandstone color with engraved-glyph print. Team version: two laminated layers of 1 in XPS insulation board wrapped in duct or gaffer tape, with the groove cut on a table saw, under $4 each.
- Handling challenge: A flat slab lying on carpet is awkward to ground-intake; the edge groove is the designed 'way in' (pinch rollers or a tilting scoop). The tablet must then be reoriented ~75 degrees to slide into the 15-degree-tilted display easel rails at 48 in, which is the game's core teaching problem for elevator or arm plus wrist. It looks like a two-degree-of-freedom problem, and it is one.
- Count/staging: 8 per alliance: 3 in the alliance Dig Site, 2 in the neutral Excavation Zone, up to 3 fed through the far-end Archive Window.
- Scoring role: The mid-value cycle piece: 4 pts flat on the 24 in gallery shelf, 6 pts slotted in the 48 in easel (6/9 in auto). Tablets are the backbone of a scoring cycle for a second-year team and are required to complete an Exhibit Face (Masterpiece RP).

### URN
- Shape/dims: Tapered vessel: 12 in max shoulder diameter, 15 in tall, 4.5 in diameter open neck, 7 in diameter flat base
- Material: Blow-molded LLDPE (toughness of a trackball or bin), ~2.0 lb with a molded-in low ballast ring so it is bottom-heavy but still tippable, terracotta color. Team version: a 12 in plastic nursery planter pot with a taped-on 4.5 in PVC neck collar and a sand-filled zip bag in the base, under $10.
- Handling challenge: Big, round and tippy. It exceeds most intake widths, cannot be rolled over, and scores only UPRIGHT (axis within 15 degrees of vertical). It forces a dedicated wide clamp or cradle, or a neck-gripper arm with a stabilizing wrist, and that geometry conflicts directly with a medallion roller intake under the 120 in frame perimeter. Placing an urn on the 24 in high Plinth without tipping it looks hard and is hard, so perceived and actual difficulty align.
- Count/staging: 4 per alliance: 2 in the alliance Dig Site, 2 fed through the far-end Archive Window (none neutral; urns are never contested at midfield).
- Scoring role: The premium piece: 5 pts on a floor Pedestal, 8 pts on the raised Plinth (8/12 in auto). A tipped urn on a pedestal scores 2 (partial credit, no cliff). Urns gate Exhibit Face completion, so an alliance of pure medallion robots cannot take the Masterpiece RP; this anchors the forced-tradeoff meta.

## Field Layout
Standard 27 ft x 54 ft carpeted field, rotationally symmetric (180 degrees) about the center.

CENTER, THE GRAND ROTUNDA: a hexagonal display tower centered on the field, 6 ft across flats and 6 ft 6 in tall, with faces alternating RED/BLUE (3 faces each), so every alliance approaches from three different angles 60 degrees apart, on both halves of the field. Each alliance's three faces are specialized:
- FACE A, 'Urn Gallery': a 20 in diameter floor PEDESTAL disc with a 1 in lip, centered 18 in off the face, plus a 20 in diameter PLINTH platform at 24 in height built into the face.
- FACE B, 'Tablet Gallery': a flat gallery SHELF 24 in high x 13 in deep, and above it an EASEL: two guide rails tilted 15 degrees from vertical with a catch lip, entry slot at 48 in.
- FACE C, 'Medallion Gallery': two 1.25 in schedule-40 pipe PEGS angled 15 degrees above horizontal at 30 in and 66 in heights, and at the floor the VAULT SLOT (8 in wide x 4 in tall). Vaulted medallions gravity-feed into a clear polycarbonate COIN COLUMN running up the adjacent rotunda corner, LED-lit and visible from every seat.

A 36h11 AprilTag is centered at 36 in on every face.

NEUTRAL EXCAVATION ZONE: two 4 ft x 8 ft taped zones straddling the centerline on each long side of the Rotunda, holding the contested neutral artifacts (6 medallions + 2 tablets per zone).

ALLIANCE DIG SITE: a 6 ft x 6 ft protected staging zone in each alliance's own left corner, holding that alliance's staged pieces.

ARCHIVE WINDOW: each alliance's human-player feeding station is a 3 ft wide x 2 ft tall wall port in the OPPOSITE alliance's end wall (Steamworks-style), which forces full-field supply runs.

RESTORATION SCAFFOLDS (endgame): three independent scaffold bays spaced 8 ft apart along each alliance's own end wall. Each bay is a 4 ft wide frame with rungs at 27 in, 45 in and 63 in (the 63 in rung is offset 15 in horizontally toward the field).

Robot starting lines are 10 ft from each end wall. All critical dimensions land on whole or half inches, and all display angles are 15 degrees.

## Match Flow
2:30 total: 15 s AUTO and 2:15 TELEOP, with the final 30 s as ENDGAME. The cycle geography is convergent by design: every alliance scores on a SHARED central structure from three angles on BOTH halves of the field, and every alliance's human-player Archive Window is at the FAR end. A typical teleop loop is: collect at your Dig Site or the contested midfield Excavation Zone, place on a rotunda face (which may be on the opponent's half), continue to the far Archive Window for a fed premium piece, and cycle back. Robots from both alliances therefore cross paths around the rotunda and along both sidelines every 20-30 seconds without any forced intake-to-intake geometry. All placement is face-on to a structure, and a 'Curation Zone' rule (no opponent contact with a robot whose bumpers are within 12 in of its own rotunda face) prevents refereeing problems like those of the 2026 G415 while leaving the entire open floor legal for defense. Contested space: the neutral Excavation Zones and all transit lanes. Spectators can read the match quickly: rotunda faces fill visibly with terracotta urns and sandstone tablets, LED face borders light when a face is complete, and the two lit coin columns rise like thermometers on opposite corners of the tower. Late in the match, the crowd watches three separate scaffold bays at each end as climbs begin, while one last urn run to a plinth can still swing 8-12 points.

## Autonomous
Auto lasts 15 seconds and is built around decisions:
1. GAME DATA, the FEATURED ARTIFACT: at T-0 the FMS broadcasts a randomly selected artifact type ('M', 'T' or 'U') through the game data API and lights the rotunda crown in a matching pattern; every AUTO placement of the featured type scores DOUBLE. Teams must write branching autos (2018 Power Up style) that read the string and choose among pre-planned paths (a coin-peg auto, a tablet-easel auto, an urn-pedestal auto), or accept half value.
2. ALLIANCE COORDINATION, the CURATOR'S BONUS: if an alliance scores at least one medallion, one tablet AND one urn during AUTO, it earns +12 points. No single robot can realistically cover all three geometries, so alliances must negotiate before the match who covers which piece and which of the three rotunda faces each robot approaches. Three faces 60 degrees apart give three natural collision-free auto lanes, but only if the alliance plans them.
3. MOBILITY: 3 pts per robot for fully crossing its starting line.
4. CONTESTED OPTION: the neutral Excavation Zone is live in auto, so a fast robot may take midfield medallions before the opposing alliance arrives. The outcome depends on speed, since the pieces are symmetric and visible.

AprilTags on every rotunda face, plinth and archive window support auto-alignment with full pose estimation, and the 15-degree easel and peg geometry rewards approach control finer than an inch. A strong three-robot auto (mobility x3, doubled featured placements, Curator's Bonus) is worth 45-60 points; a kitbot contributes mobility + a 3-pt vault slide that it can do by dead reckoning.

## Endgame
THE RESTORATION SCAFFOLDS, in the final 30 seconds (climbing is legal earlier, but scaffold zones are protected only in the last 30 s). Each alliance has THREE physically separate scaffold bays 8 ft apart on its own end wall, one robot per bay, so there are no pileups like those on the 2024 stage. Each bay has three 1.66 in OD steel pipe rungs: LOW at 27 in, MID at 45 in and HIGH at 63 in, with the HIGH rung offset 15 in horizontally toward the field.

The mechanism challenge: LOW is a simple hook-and-winch pull that any team can build; MID requires a real extending climber (~50 in of reach) with the robot swinging free; HIGH demands a traversal-style transfer. The horizontal offset means a robot must reach past the rung it hangs from and hand itself over, an articulated two-hook or pivoting-arm problem that is hard to master and looks as hard as it is.

Points: PARK in the Restoration Zone (taped floor under the bays) = 3; LOW hang = 8; MID hang = 14; HIGH hang = 22. 'Hanging' = fully supported by the rung with bumpers off the carpet, assessed 3 s after the buzzer. Partial credit applies throughout: a failed high transfer that stays on mid keeps 14, and a failed low climb still parks for 3. Nothing is all-or-nothing, and no single climb can outweigh two minutes of artifact cycling (max endgame swing 66 of a ~180-220 pt elite match).

Reward: 22 points is the largest single placement in the game, three simultaneous side-by-side ascents are the most visible event on the field, and the endgame RP runs through MID, so the 'two robots do the mid-tier action' bar keeps every alliance engaged without demanding three traversals.

## Ranking Points
WIN = 3 RP, TIE = 1 RP, plus up to 3 bonus RPs (max 6/match). All three bonus RPs reinforce the core game (place artifacts, place all KINDS of artifacts, climb); there are no side quests.

| Bonus RP | Requirement | Regional | DCMP | Champs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| COLLECTION RP (entry volume) | Total artifacts scored on displays + vault by the alliance | 16 | 20 | 24 |
| MASTERPIECE RP (stretch, ~3.5x) | Complete EXHIBIT FACES (a face is complete when every position on it is filled: A = pedestal urn + plinth urn; B = 2 shelf tablets + easel tablet; C = both pegs full [6 medallions] + 8 vault coins) | 2 faces | 3 faces | 3 faces + 12 vault coins |
| RESTORATION RP (endgame) | Total endgame points ('two robots at mid' calibration: 14+14=28 base) | 28 | 30 (2 mid + park) | 36 (mid + high) |

Calibration: median regional alliances land 14-20 artifacts, which puts the Collection RP at a ~50% hit rate. 2 complete faces require coordinated coverage of urns, tablets and medallions, including a plinth urn and a 66 in peg, which is elite-alliance territory at ~3-3.5x the entry bar. Restoration at 28 equals two mid climbs. Tiebreakers: cumulative AUTO points, then ENDGAME points, then total artifact points.

## Low Floor
A kitbot with a plow or a single-roller ground intake has a real job for all 2:30. It slides medallions into the Vault Slot (2 pts each, floor level, 8 in wide target, no orientation, no elevation), which feeds both the Collection RP count and the Champs-level Masterpiece requirement (vault coins). In auto it earns mobility + a dead-reckoned 3-pt vault slide, and if 'M' comes up as the Featured Artifact its auto coin is worth 6. In the endgame, the 27 in LOW rung takes a $40 hook-and-winch for 8 pts, or the robot can park for 3 pts by driving to a zone. Nothing in this floor-level loop looks easier than it is: pushing a foam ring into a slot looks trivial and is trivial, in line with the s-neff alignment principle. A well-driven vault robot plausibly contributes 30-40 points and an RP-relevant service every match, the Stronghold/Rapid React archetype.

## High Ceiling
Elite teams chase:
1. AprilTag-aligned placements in under 3 seconds on the 66 in peg and 48 in easel from either half of the field;
2. the urn meta: a stabilized neck-gripper that can plinth an urn in one motion is the highest $/lb mechanism in the game;
3. multi-piece indexing (the carry limit is 2 artifacts, e.g., a tablet + a medallion in one cross-field cycle);
4. the traversal HIGH hang, plus the math of climbing early in blowouts.

The meta stays unsolved because the three piece geometries conflict under 115 lb / 120 in perimeter. A do-everything robot exists only as a compromise, so alliance value depends on composition: is the third robot best as a coin cycler, an urn specialist, or a defender who camps the neutral Excavation Zone? Featured Artifact randomization keeps auto selection dynamic all season, and the face-completion math of the Masterpiece RP (which needs urns AND tablets AND pegged medallions) shifts the optimal pick lists between offensive archetypes in the way Reefscape's L1/algae shift did, with room for a late-season 'vault-rush' or 'double-traversal' meta to emerge.

## Teaching Value
The spread of pieces forces a Power Up/Rapid React-class mechanism curriculum: a ground roller intake with indexing (medallions); a pinch or scoop intake with a reorienting wrist (tablets, from the floor to the 15-degree easel); a wide clamp or neck-gripper arm with tip control (urns); a decision between an elevator OR a pivoting arm (the 24/30/48/66 in targets make both viable); and three tiers of climber up to an articulated traversal transfer. Software: parsing FMS game data and branching autos, AprilTag pose estimation and auto-align on six faces carrying tags, full-field odometry for the archive runs, path planning with deconfliction of alliance lanes in auto, and driver-assist snapping to 15-degree approach vectors. For a student team, every subteam gets a substantial, separable problem with an obvious minimum viable version.

## Buildability
The Rotunda team version is ONE hex face, not the tower: a 2x4 lumber A-frame with one sheet of 3/4 in plywood providing the 24 in shelf, the 24 in plinth top and the 15-degree easel rails (cut with a circular saw, with angles set by a $10 speed square; all angles are 15 degrees and all heights are whole inches). The pegs are 1-1/4 in schedule-40 steel or PVC pipe in pipe flanges. The vault slot is a plywood cutout with a cardboard chute and any clear tube (or a coin bin). The total is under 1.5 sheets + ~$60 of hardware.
- Pedestal: one plywood disc on a 2x4 ring.
- Scaffold bay: two 4x4 posts, base plates and three 1-1/4 in pipe rungs at 27/45/63 in (the standard FRC pipe that teams already climb on). One bay is enough to practice all three tiers.
- Team-version game pieces: EVA-mat medallion rings (hole saw), XPS-board tablets in tape and planter-pot urns; a full field of practice pieces costs under $120.

Every robot-critical interaction dimension (slot 8x4 in, pegs 1.66 OD, neck 4.5 in, rungs 27/45/63 in) is a round or half-inch value.

## Risks
- Center-field congestion: six rotunda faces on one structure concentrate traffic. The 12 in Curation Zone protection must be refereed crisply or it will attract fouls. The mitigation is generous face width (36 in) and 60-degree face separation, but this needs playtesting.
- Urn frustration for mid-tier teams: a tippy 15 in vessel could become 'the piece my robot can't touch'. The 2-pt partial credit for a tipped urn, and the fact that urns are optional for the Collection RP (gating only the Masterpiece RP), relieve the pressure, but if urn handling proves too hard the piece could be under-used in quals.
- Low-resource teams may ignore the Featured Artifact game data (2018 showed that many teams struggled with branching autos). The doubled value is a real incentive, but the floor auto (mobility + vault) must stay viable so that the mechanic encourages teams instead of punishing them.
- Vault chute jams: foam rings feeding a gravity column can wedge. The field build needs an oversized chute and a clearing port for field staff, and the rules for the team version should not require the column at all.
- The Steamworks-style far-side Archive Window exposes human-player feeding to defense near the opponent wall. Protected-zone taping around the window (4 ft) is specified, but its size needs tuning to avoid either camping or a dead zone.
- The Masterpiece RP thresholds are calibrated by hand. If week-1 data shows face completion is rarer than ~10% at regionals, the 2-face requirement should drop to 1 face + 8 vault coins through a mid-season team update.

## Spectator Hook
The final 30 seconds: three robots per alliance climb side-by-side scaffold bays, one of them attempting the high-rung transfer, while a last urn specialist threads through traffic to place the 8-point plinth urn. The rotunda face lights up in LEDs as it completes, and the two lit coin columns at center field show who is ahead.
