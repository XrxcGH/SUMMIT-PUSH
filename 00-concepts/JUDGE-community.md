## Verdict
Greenlight FIRST ASCENT. It is the only concept with no catastrophic failure mode: scoring is visible and latched, defense is legal but structurally can't blockade, the kitbot path is honest, the endgame banks partial credit at every tier, and its worst problems (global Forecast luck, RP lockout by alliance draw) are fixable with one-line rule changes rather than field rebuilds. Steal SUMMIT SIGNAL's two best ideas during refinement — the beacon-ignition visual language (Camp LEDs already half-implement it) and the 'pieces score for the structure's owner regardless of who placed them' anti-pollution rule. SUMMIT SIGNAL is the runner-up on the strength of its spectacle but bets its identity on an unproven 82-inch capping tolerance and a nine-path auto nobody will run. MAINLINE has the deepest strategy in the set but points-per-second accrual is the most reliably divisive mechanic in FRC history and would need a fundamental rework (discrete visible ticks) before it's safe; HIGHBALL is a strong design dragged down by 2014-assist-style relay refereeing, 45-foot sightlines, and the biggest field build; RELIQUARY's single central structure hides its own scoring and funnels all traffic into a foul-magnet bubble — it would headline the least-favorite poll.

## Details
### HIGHBALL
- Strengths: Best-in-class score legibility (amber FULL CAR lamps, clear stock/tank walls — count the lamps, know the score). The 3/8/15 endgame with the free-swinging Brakeman's Bar is exactly the right difficulty curve: no Steamworks cliff, real 2022-traversal drama. The caboose zero-lip ramp is an honest kitbot job. Crossing-diagonal traffic gives Aerial-Assist flow without forced collisions.
- Concerns: Far-side scoring is the sleeper least-favorite-poll risk: your train is 45+ ft from your glass, and 2019 far-rocket complaints say weak-vision teams will hate every match. MAIL RELAY is 2014 assist-tracking reborn — refs disliked it then and 'possession' wording will be a protest magnet. Three piece geometries plus manifest branching plus relay choreography is the heaviest cognitive load of the five. The field build is the biggest here: two 16-ft five-car trains plus two gantries plus grade crossings — venues and offseason organizers will groan, and 'dead space' behind each train eats real estate. Stock car door jamming is a self-admitted capacity lie.
- Fixes: Cut the MAIL RELAY entirely or convert it to a zone-based handoff (bag scored within N seconds of leaving a taped transfer zone = bonus) so refs track geography, not possession. Move each train 8-10 ft closer to its own driver station or add a driver-side camera-feed allowance; the sightline problem cannot be patched by AprilTags alone for median teams. Reduce the manifest from 6 orderings to a 2-way branch (2018 proved 2-3 branches is the sweet spot). Slope the stock car floor and widen the door to 22 in before week zero.

### MAINLINE
- Strengths: The deepest strategy of the five — the Signal Weight ownership economy with capacity locking is genuinely novel, the 'race to fill your face' endgame-of-territory math is backgammon-grade, and the MAINLINE RP (hold all three structures) is the best stretch RP in the set. The lever-clack climb finisher is a legitimately great photographed moment. Kitbot crates carrying Signal Weight means the floor robot fights for ownership, not consolation points. Cheapest field build (~$250).
- Concerns: Points-per-second accrual is the single most divisive mechanic in FRC history — 2018's scale made half of CD call it a chess match and the other half call it unwatchable, and this doubles down with THREE accruing structures. A spectator can read the state (lamps, paddles) but cannot read the SCORE — 'who is ahead and by how much' requires integrating ownership time in your head, which is hidden scoring by another name. Additive-only counterplay means locked-out depot faces become mathematically dead mid-match — the designers admit the territory dialogue can collapse. The 4.5-ft chokepoints are a pin-foul factory; G415-style refereeing burden is baked into the geometry. If the 1 pt/sec Tower rate is even slightly hot, one lantern bot decides every match and the freight game is theater.
- Fixes: Convert accrual to discrete, visible ticks: award a fixed chunk (e.g. 5 pts) every 5 seconds of ownership with an audible/visual pulse, so the audience sees points land. Add a descore-adjacent counterplay for locked faces — e.g. a once-per-match 'switching order' that reopens one row — so dead territory revives. Widen chokepoint lanes to 6 ft and pre-write the placement-protection rule with a bright-line (bumpers within 6 in of a face = protected) before playtests, not after. Playtest the pts/sec rate with a deliberate lantern-monoculture alliance and tune until it loses to a balanced one.

### RELIQUARY
- Strengths: The coin-column thermometers are a good legibility device, the vault slot is an honest floor task that feeds the top-tier RP (rare and smart — the kitbot's coins matter at Champs), and the scaffold ladder (3/8/14/22) is properly tiered. The Curator's Bonus forces real alliance coordination in auto. Team-version buildability of a single hex face is well thought through.
- Concerns: A single central scoring structure is the game's fatal geometry: the tower physically occludes half its own faces from any given seat, so scoring literally happens behind the set piece — hidden scoring by architecture. Six faces on one structure funnels all twelve cycles per minute into one 6-ft hex, and the 12-in Curation Zone no-contact bubble around it is a G415-class foul magnet the designers themselves flag. The urn is a 2023-cone frustration piece — tippy, tapered, with an 'upright within 15 degrees' judgment call refs will be litigating on camera all season. Least interactive of the five in practice: the curation bubble plus protected dig sites shrink legal defense to open-floor shadowing. Masterpiece RP face-completion bookkeeping is the most complex RP accounting in the set.
- Fixes: Split the rotunda into two mirrored half-structures offset from center (or push alliance faces to opposite ends) so scoring is visible from the stands and traffic bifurcates. Replace the 15-degree urn-upright judgment with a physical definition: base fully within the pedestal lip = scored, anything else = tipped — zero ref discretion. Shrink or delete the Curation Zone and rely on the 5-second pin count plus generous face width. Drop the urn from the Collection RP path entirely (already partially done) and add a mid-value urn target at 12-18 in so mid-tier teams touch the piece.

### FIRST ASCENT
- Strengths: The most coherent package of the five, with no single catastrophic risk. Latched Camp bonuses with LED tier rings are legible AND permanent — the mountain visibly 'comes alive,' momentum is readable, and nothing un-scores. Four-face crag scoring structurally defeats blockade defense while keeping defense fully legal — the best interaction/defense balance in the set. The published kitbot path (crates-only, Depot + 24-in shelf, park) is the most honest low floor here, with a real graduation ladder. Camp completion mathematically requires mixed alliances — 2015's monoculture is impossible by construction. Forecast is a clean 3-branch (not 6, not 9), the right software ask. Endgame 3/12/20/30 on an inclined staggered wall is Rapid React traversal with better partial credit.
- Concerns: The Forecast globally doubling one Storm Tier injects matchup variance — an alliance drafted around high-tier robots gets a free +30 lane on GALE nights and a dead one on WHITEOUT; over a qual schedule this is luck teams will notice. Expedition RP can be mathematically dead on a bad alliance draw at small regionals (designers admit it). 78-in ring hangs under legal defense is a tip-over/safety flashpoint — 2016 taught us high-CG-plus-contact generates both injuries and rage threads. Two crags at midfield plus the center cache could still congeal under a committed two-defender elims strategy. It is also the least novel concept here — a competent remix of Rapid React geometry and Charged Up piece logic rather than a new idea.
- Fixes: Make the Storm Tier alliance-specific (each alliance's forecast drawn independently, or let alliances CHOOSE their storm tier at match start like a declared gambit) to convert luck into strategy. Add a Depot-based fallback path into the Expedition RP (e.g. Camp I satisfiable via N depot pieces of each type) so no alliance is drawn dead. Impose a no-contact rule on a robot with its mechanism extended above 60 in within 3 ft of its own crag face — narrow enough to preserve defense, wide enough to stop tip-farming. Playtest the two-defender center-corridor choke with real drive teams before locking crag spacing.

### SUMMIT SIGNAL
- Strengths: The best single spectator hook of the five: beacons physically IGNITING as Flares seat, with 'the winning alliance is the brighter half of the mountain' — that is the clip that wins the favorite-game poll. 'Any piece on a post scores for that post's alliance' is the cleanest anti-pollution rule in the set — an entire exploit class deleted in one sentence, with a stands-visible ref fallback for the sensor. Beacon completion ORDER as the studied skill (caps lock stacks) is subtle, real depth. Ring-toss post scoring is forgiving in exactly the right way for mid-tier teams. Buildable field (PVC posts, trimmed traffic cones).
- Concerns: The nine-path First Light auto suite is over-engineered — 2018 data says most teams shipped 2-3 branches; a coordination-gated +12 that needs three teams' preloads and code to align will convert to 'nobody attempts it' by week 3, and with it the auto's whole identity. Flare capping at 76-82 in is the precision ceiling the entire Network RP hangs on; if the chamfer funnel is one inch less forgiving than drawn, the signature mechanic dies and beacons never light at regionals. Seat-sensor LEDs are a field-reliability single point of failure for the game's emotional core. The admitted ring-lobbing meta at low posts would erode pick-and-place primacy and needs a rule, and launch-restriction rules are historically ugly to referee. Endgame is competent but the least distinctive of the five, and 2-pt park undersells the floor.
- Fixes: Collapse First Light to one randomized tower with the +2 per-piece bonus only (drop the 3x3 preload choreography); let the +12 trigger on lighting ANY beacon in auto. Prototype the Flare socket fit FIRST — make the socket 4-in over a 1.9-in post with a full-cone chamfer and validate with a kitbot-grade gripper before anything else freezes; if capping success is under 60% for a decent arm, lower the High/Summit tips by 6 in. Kill the lobbing question preemptively with a 'momentum rule' clone (piece must be in contact with the robot within 1 ft of the post) rather than a launch-distance judgment call. Raise park to 3 and make the beacon LED redundant (sensor + ref-confirmable flag).
