# RELIQUARY presented by the Halcyon Trust

**Theme:** The world's great lost artifacts have been recovered, and alliances of robotic curators race to restore, catalog, and display them in the Grand Rotunda before the museum's gala opening night.

**Narrative:** For a century the Halcyon Collection sat scattered in crates, its medallions, tablets, and urns lost to time. Tonight, the doors of the Grand Rotunda open at last — and only the finest curators will see their alliance's exhibit complete. Robots must haul artifacts across a crowded gallery floor, place them with a conservator's touch on pedestals, easels, and pegs, and in the final seconds scale the restoration scaffolds to unveil the skylight. When the lights come up, whose collection will history remember?

## Game Pieces
### MEDALLION
- Shape/dims: Torus/ring (a 'coin' with a hanging hole): 6 in outside diameter, 2 in inside diameter, 1.5 in thick
- Material: Molded cross-linked EVA foam ring (density similar to a pool exercise ring), ~0.4 lb, dyed bronze with a molded laurel emboss. Team version: stacked 3/4 in EVA floor-mat circles cut with a hole saw and glued, or off-the-shelf 6 in foam rings (~$3 each).
- Handling challenge: Small and plentiful — rewards a fast touch-it-own-it roller intake and internal indexing for multi-piece capacity. The center hole must be located and oriented to hang on a peg (wrist/vision problem), but it can also simply be slid along the carpet into the floor-level Vault slot with a plow (no orientation needed). Easy-looking task IS easy; the 66 in high peg looks hard and is hard.
- Count/staging: 20 per alliance per match: 4 staged in the alliance Dig Site, 6 staged in the neutral midfield Excavation Zone (shared, contested), up to 10 fed by the Archivist human player through the Archive Window at the FAR end of the field. Any robot may preload 1 artifact of any type.
- Scoring role: The volume currency. Vault slide = 2 pts (the kitbot's bread and butter), low peg (30 in) = 3, high peg (66 in) = 5 (7/5/3 auto... see scoring table). Medallions drive the Collection RP count and fill the visible Vault coin column that spectators read at a glance.

### TABLET
- Shape/dims: Flat rectangular slab: 12 in x 9 in x 2 in, with a 3/4 in deep x 1.5 in wide finger groove routed around the 12 in edges
- Material: Rigid polyethylene foam core with a fiber-reinforced textured TPU skin (durability class of a CHARGED UP cube), ~1.2 lb, sandstone color with engraved-glyph print. Team version: two laminated layers of 1 in XPS insulation board wrapped in duct/gaffer tape, groove cut with a table saw — under $4 each.
- Handling challenge: A flat slab lying on carpet is genuinely awkward to ground-intake — the edge groove is the designed 'way in' (pinch rollers or a tilting scoop). It must then be reoriented ~75 degrees to slide into the 15-degree-tilted display easel rails at 48 in — this is the game's core elevator/arm + wrist teaching problem. It honestly looks like a two-degree-of-freedom problem and it is one.
- Count/staging: 8 per alliance: 3 in the alliance Dig Site, 2 in the neutral Excavation Zone, up to 3 fed through the far-end Archive Window.
- Scoring role: The mid-value cycle piece: 4 pts flat on the 24 in gallery shelf, 6 pts slotted in the 48 in easel (6/9 in auto). Tablets are the backbone of a second-year-team scoring cycle and are required for Exhibit Face completion (Masterpiece RP).

### URN
- Shape/dims: Tapered vessel: 12 in max shoulder diameter, 15 in tall, 4.5 in diameter open neck, 7 in diameter flat base
- Material: Blow-molded LLDPE (trackball/bin toughness), ~2.0 lb with a molded-in low ballast ring so it is bottom-heavy but still tippable, terracotta color. Team version: a 12 in plastic nursery planter pot with a taped-on 4.5 in PVC neck collar and a sand-filled zip bag in the base — under $10.
- Handling challenge: Big, round, and tippy: it exceeds most intake widths, cannot be rolled over, and only scores UPRIGHT (axis within 15 degrees of vertical). Forces a dedicated wide clamp/cradle or neck-gripper arm with a stabilizing wrist — geometry that directly conflicts with a medallion roller intake under the 120 in frame perimeter. Placing one on the 24 in high Plinth without tipping it looks hard and is hard: perfectly aligned difficulty.
- Count/staging: 4 per alliance: 2 in the alliance Dig Site, 2 fed through the far-end Archive Window (none neutral — urns are never contested at midfield).
- Scoring role: The premium piece: 5 pts on a floor Pedestal, 8 pts on the raised Plinth (8/12 auto). A tipped urn on a pedestal scores 2 (partial credit, no cliff). Urns gate Exhibit Face completion, so a pure coin-bot alliance cannot take the Masterpiece RP — the anchor of the forced-tradeoff meta.

## Field Layout
Standard 27 ft x 54 ft carpeted field, rotationally symmetric (180 degrees) about center. CENTER — THE GRAND ROTUNDA: a hexagonal display tower centered on the field, 6 ft across flats, 6 ft 6 in tall, faces alternating RED/BLUE (3 faces each, so every alliance approaches from three different angles 60 degrees apart, on both halves of the field). Each alliance's three faces are specialized: FACE A 'Urn Gallery' — a 20 in diameter, 1 in lipped floor PEDESTAL disc centered 18 in off the face, plus a 20 in diameter PLINTH platform at 24 in height built into the face; FACE B 'Tablet Gallery' — a flat 24 in high x 13 in deep gallery SHELF, and above it an EASEL: two guide rails tilted 15 degrees from vertical with a catch lip, entry slot at 48 in; FACE C 'Medallion Gallery' — two 1.25 in schedule-40 pipe PEGS angled 15 degrees above horizontal at 30 in and 66 in heights, and at the floor the VAULT SLOT (8 in wide x 4 in tall); vaulted medallions gravity-feed into a clear polycarbonate COIN COLUMN running up the adjacent rotunda corner, LED-lit, visible from every seat. A 36h11 AprilTag is centered at 36 in on every face. NEUTRAL EXCAVATION ZONE: two 4 ft x 8 ft taped zones straddling the centerline on each long side of the Rotunda, holding the contested neutral artifacts (6 medallions + 2 tablets per zone). ALLIANCE DIG SITE: a 6 ft x 6 ft protected staging zone in each alliance's own left corner holding that alliance's staged pieces. ARCHIVE WINDOW: each alliance's human-player feeding station is a 3 ft wide x 2 ft tall wall port in the OPPOSITE alliance's end wall (Steamworks-style), forcing full-field supply runs. RESTORATION SCAFFOLDS (endgame): three independent scaffold bays spaced 8 ft apart along each alliance's own end wall, each bay a 4 ft wide frame with rungs at 27 in, 45 in, and 63 in (the 63 in rung offset 15 in horizontally toward the field). Robot starting lines are 10 ft from each end wall. Critical dimensions all land on whole or half inches; all display angles are 15 degrees.

## Match Flow
2:30 total — 15 s AUTO, 2:15 TELEOP with the final 30 s as ENDGAME. The cycle geography is deliberately convergent: every alliance scores on a SHARED central structure from three angles on BOTH halves of the field, and every alliance's human-player Archive Window is at the FAR end. A typical teleop loop is: collect at your Dig Site or the contested midfield Excavation Zone, place on a rotunda face (which may be on the opponent's half), continue to the far Archive Window for a fed premium piece, and cycle back — so robots from both alliances naturally cross paths around the rotunda and along both sidelines every 20-30 seconds without any forced intake-to-intake geometry (all placement is face-on to a structure, and a 'Curation Zone' rule — no opponent contact with a robot whose bumpers are within 12 in of its own rotunda face — prevents G415-style refereeing chaos while leaving the entire open floor legal for defense). Contested space: the neutral Excavation Zones and all transit lanes. Spectators read the match instantly: rotunda faces fill visibly with terracotta urns and sandstone tablets, LED face-borders light when a face is complete, and the two glowing coin columns rise like thermometers on opposite corners of the tower. Late match, the crowd watches three separate scaffold bays per end as climbs begin while one last urn run to a plinth can still swing 8-12 points.

## Autonomous
15 seconds, and it is a decision tree, not a script. (1) GAME DATA — THE FEATURED ARTIFACT: at T-0 the FMS broadcasts a randomly selected artifact type ('M', 'T', or 'U') via the game data API and lights the rotunda crown in a matching pattern; every AUTO placement of the featured type scores DOUBLE. Teams must write branching autos (2018 Power Up style) that read the string and choose among pre-planned paths — a coin-peg auto, a tablet-easel auto, an urn-pedestal auto — or accept half value. (2) ALLIANCE COORDINATION — THE CURATOR'S BONUS: if an alliance scores at least one medallion, one tablet, AND one urn during AUTO, it earns +12 points. No single robot can realistically cover all three geometries, so alliances must pre-match negotiate who covers which piece and which of the three rotunda faces each robot approaches (three faces 60 degrees apart = three natural collision-free auto lanes, but only if the alliance plans them). (3) MOBILITY: 3 pts per robot for fully crossing its starting line. (4) CONTESTED OPTION: the neutral Excavation Zone is live in auto — a fast robot may steal midfield medallions before the opposing alliance arrives (drama without luck: the pieces are symmetric and visible). AprilTags on every rotunda face, plinth, and archive window support full pose-estimation auto-alignment; the 15-degree easel and peg geometry rewards sub-inch approach control. A strong three-robot auto (mobility x3, featured-doubled placements, Curator's Bonus) is worth 45-60 points; a kitbot contributes mobility + a 3-pt vault slide it can do by dead reckoning.

## Endgame
THE RESTORATION SCAFFOLDS — final 30 seconds (climbing legal earlier, but scaffold zones are protected only in the last 30 s). Each alliance has THREE physically separate scaffold bays 8 ft apart on its own end wall (no NASCAR pileups — one robot per bay, full stop). Each bay has three 1.66 in OD steel pipe rungs: LOW at 27 in, MID at 45 in, and HIGH at 63 in, with the HIGH rung offset 15 in horizontally toward the field. The mechanism challenge: LOW is a simple hook-and-winch pull (any team can build it); MID requires a real extending climber (~50 in of reach) with the robot swinging free; HIGH demands a traversal-style transfer — the horizontal offset means a robot must reach past the rung it hangs from and hand itself over, an articulated two-hook or pivoting-arm problem that is legitimately hard to master and looks exactly as hard as it is. Points: PARK in the Restoration Zone (taped floor under the bays) = 3; LOW hang = 8; MID hang = 14; HIGH hang = 22. 'Hanging' = fully supported by the rung, bumpers off carpet, assessed 3 s after the buzzer. Partial credit is everywhere: a failed high transfer that stays on mid keeps 14; a failed low climb still parks for 3; nothing is all-or-nothing and no single climb can erase or trump two minutes of artifact cycling (max endgame swing 66 of a ~180-220 pt elite match). Why it is rewarding: 22 points is the single largest placement in the game, three simultaneous side-by-side ascents are the most dramatic visual on the field, and the endgame RP runs through MID — so the 'two robots do the mid-tier action' bar keeps every alliance engaged without demanding three traversals.

## Low Floor
A kitbot with a plow or a single-roller ground intake has a real job for all 2:30: slide medallions into the Vault Slot (2 pts each, floor level, 8 in wide target, no orientation, no elevation) and it directly feeds both the Collection RP count and the Champs-level Masterpiece requirement (vault coins). In auto it earns mobility + a dead-reckoned 3-pt vault slide, and if 'M' comes up as the Featured Artifact its auto coin is worth 6. In endgame the 27 in LOW rung is a $40 hook-and-winch for 8 pts, or parking is 3 pts for simply driving to a zone. Nothing in this floor-level loop looks easier than it is — pushing a foam ring into a slot looks trivial and IS trivial — honoring the s-neff alignment principle. A well-driven vault bot plausibly contributes 30-40 points and an RP-relevant service every match, the Stronghold/Rapid React archetype.

## High Ceiling
Elite teams chase: (1) sub-3-second AprilTag-aligned placements on the 66 in peg and 48 in easel from either half of the field; (2) the urn meta — a stabilized neck-gripper that can plinth an urn in one motion is the highest $/lb mechanism in the game; (3) multi-piece indexing (carry limit is 2 artifacts: e.g., a tablet + a medallion in one cross-field cycle); (4) the traversal HIGH hang, plus 'climb early' math in blowouts. The meta stays unsolved because the three piece geometries fight each other under 115 lb / 120 in perimeter: a do-everything robot exists only as a compromise, so alliance value is compositional — is the third robot best as a coin cycler, an urn specialist, or a defender who camps the neutral Excavation Zone? Featured-Artifact randomization keeps auto selection dynamic all season, and the Masterpiece RP's face-completion math (which needs urns AND tablets AND pegged medallions) shifts optimal picklists between offense archetypes exactly the way Reefscape's L1/algae shift did — with room for a late-season 'vault-rush' or 'double-traversal' meta to emerge.

## Teaching Value
The piece spread forces a Power Up/Rapid React-class mechanism curriculum: a ground roller intake with indexing (medallions), a pinch/scoop intake with a reorienting wrist (tablets, floor-to-15-degree-easel), a wide clamp or neck-gripper arm with tip control (urns), an elevator OR pivoting arm decision (24/30/48/66 in targets make both viable), and three tiers of climber up to an articulated traversal transfer. Software: parsing FMS game data and branching autos, AprilTag pose estimation and auto-align on six tag-bearing faces, full-field odometry for the archive runs, path planning with alliance-lane deconfliction in auto, and driver-assist snapping to 15-degree approach vectors. For a student team, every subteam gets a meaty, separable problem with an obvious minimum viable version.

## Buildability
Rotunda team version = ONE hex face, not the tower: a 2x4 lumber A-frame with one sheet of 3/4 in plywood providing the 24 in shelf, 24 in plinth top, and 15-degree easel rails (cut with a circular saw, angles set with a $10 speed square — all angles are 15 degrees, all heights whole inches); pegs are 1-1/4 in schedule-40 steel or PVC pipe in pipe flanges; the vault slot is a plywood cutout with a cardboard chute and any clear tube (or just a coin bin) — total under 1.5 sheets + ~$60 hardware. Pedestal = one plywood disc on a 2x4 ring. Scaffold bay = two 4x4 posts, base plates, and three 1-1/4 in pipe rungs at 27/45/63 in (the exact FRC-standard pipe teams already climb on); one bay is enough to practice all three tiers. Team-version game pieces: EVA-mat medallion rings (hole saw), XPS-board tablets in tape, planter-pot urns — a full field of practice pieces under $120. Every robot-critical interaction dimension (slot 8x4 in, pegs 1.66 OD, neck 4.5 in, rungs 27/45/63 in) is on round or half-inch values.

## Risks
- Center-field congestion: six rotunda faces on one structure concentrates traffic; the 12 in Curation Zone protection must be crisply refereed or it becomes a foul magnet — mitigation is generous face width (36 in) and 60-degree face separation, but this needs playtesting.
- Urn frustration for mid-tier teams: a tippy 15 in vessel could read as 'the piece my robot can't touch'; the 2-pt tipped-urn partial credit and the fact that urns are optional for the Collection RP (only gating Masterpiece) is the pressure valve, but if urn handling proves too hard the piece could go under-used in quals.
- Featured Artifact game data may be ignored by low-resource teams (2018 showed many teams struggled with branching autos); the double-value carrot is real but the floor auto (mobility + vault) must stay viable so the mechanic inspires rather than punishes.
- Vault chute jams: foam rings feeding a gravity column can wedge; the field build needs an oversized chute and a field-staff clearing port, and the team-version rules should not require the column at all.
- Steamworks-style far-side Archive Window exposes human-player feeding to defense near the opponent wall; protected-zone taping around the window (4 ft) is specified but the size needs tuning to avoid either camping or a dead zone.
- Masterpiece RP thresholds are hand-calibrated; if week-1 data shows face completion is rarer than ~10% at regionals, the 2-face requirement should drop to 1 face + 8 vault coins via a mid-season team update mechanism.

## Spectator Hook
The final 30 seconds: three robots per alliance swinging up side-by-side scaffold bays — one attempting the crowd-audible high-rung transfer — while a last urn specialist threads through traffic to drop the match-swinging 8-point plinth urn on a rotunda face that erupts in LED light as it completes, the two glowing coin columns towering over center field showing everyone exactly who is ahead.
